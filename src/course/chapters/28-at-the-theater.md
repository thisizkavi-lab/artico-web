# CH28 — At the Theater

**Your goal:** Collect or choose tickets, find your seats, ask about the performance, and manage the intermission.  

## Study: At the box office

The **box office** is where a venue sells or distributes tickets. **Will call** can mean collecting tickets already reserved or purchased. Ask **Where do I pick up my tickets?** if the signs or instructions are unclear.

**Guest:** I'm picking up two tickets under Casey Duncan.  
**Staff:** Do you have your confirmation number?  
**Guest:** Yes. It's WD2048.  
**Staff:** Thank you. Here are your tickets.  

**Confirmation number** or **order number** identifies the transaction; the venue may call it a **booking reference**. You can show the number or read it out. **I bought them online** explains how you purchased them; it does not tell staff your name or performance date.

Use these fictional performance facts in Practice:

| Detail | This performance's information |
| --- | --- |
| Show | A Door in the Rain, tonight at 7:30 p.m. |
| Casey's reservation | Two orchestra seats, row B, seats 5 and 6; confirmation WD2048 |
| Tickets left tonight | Two balcony seats with a partly obstructed view |
| Tomorrow at 7:30 p.m. | Two orchestra seats still available |
| Running time | Two hours including one twenty-minute intermission |
| Intermission | Begins at 8:30 p.m.; audience returns by 8:50 p.m. |
| Guest services | Coat check available; no photography during this performance |

**Are there any seats left for tonight?** checks availability. **Do those seats have a clear view?** checks a different detail. A **partly obstructed view** means something blocks part of what you can see. You may accept those seats or ask **What about tomorrow?**

## Study: Inside the theater

| Word | Meaning in a live-performance venue |
| --- | --- |
| Stage | Area where the performance takes place |
| Orchestra seats | Seats on the main floor, distinct from an orchestra's musicians |
| Balcony | An upper seating level |
| Row / seat | A line of seats / your individual place |
| Front row | The first row closest to the stage in that section |
| Box | A small, partly separated group of seats |
| Aisle | Passage between seating areas |
| Audience / performer | People watching / someone taking part in the show |
| Set / prop | Scenery and stage environment / an object used in the performance |
| Curtain | Hanging material that can cover or frame the stage |

The venue's seating chart names its actual sections. **Orchestra** is useful for this fictional theater; not every room uses identical labels. **Theater** is the spelling used here; a venue's official name may use **Theatre**.

**Usher:** May I see your tickets?  
**Guest:** Here you go. We're in row B.  
**Usher:** Seats five and six. This aisle, then the second row.  
**Guest:** Thank you.  

An **usher** helps people reach their seats. If someone is in yours, try **Excuse me. I think this is my seat. Could we check the tickets?** This lets you compare the details without assuming which person made the mistake.

Other useful questions are **How long is the show?**, **Is there an intermission?**, **Do you have a coat check?**, and **When is opening night?** Opening night marks the official opening of a production. Ask staff about a step-free route or other access need rather than infer one from a seat name.

## Study: Before the show and during the break

**The show is about to start** means it will start very soon. You may hear **Please silence your phones** or a request to switch them off. Respond to the instruction for that performance.

An **intermission** is a break between parts of the performance.

**Guest:** How long is the intermission?  
**Usher:** Twenty minutes. Please be back by eight fifty.  
**Guest:** Thanks. Where's the restroom?  
**Usher:** In the lobby, to the right.  

**Companion:** Let's get a drink.  
**You:** I'd rather stay here. I'll see you back at our seats.  
**Companion:** Okay. I'll be back before eight fifty.  

If you arrive late, **Can we go in now?** asks about admission at that moment. Staff may say **Please wait here until we can seat you**. That is a response to follow, not a promise that every venue handles late entry the same way.

## Practice: Tickets, seats, and timing

Speak both roles alone. Use the performance facts and supplied lobby direction.

1. Collect Casey Duncan's two tickets. Staff ask for the confirmation number; supply it and confirm the row and seats.
2. You have no tickets. Ask about tonight and the view. Decline the partly obstructed seats and ask about tomorrow; staff offer the pair shown in the table.
3. Show your ticket to an usher and ask where row B is. The usher gives the model's aisle and row instruction.
4. Someone is in seat 5. Ask to compare tickets. Their ticket is for seat 15, and they say **Oh, sorry. I read it wrong**. Respond briefly.
5. Ask the length of the intermission and return time. Your companion wants a drink; you prefer to stay. Arrange where to meet and when.
6. Hide the models. Re-create arrival and ticket collection with one changed detail. For late entry, staff say **Please wait here until we can seat you**; acknowledge that instruction.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Two tickets under Casey Duncan. The confirmation number is WD2048. We're in row B, seats five and six, right?**
2. **I'd prefer a clear view. Are there orchestra seats tomorrow?**
3. **We're in row B. Which aisle should we use?**
4. **I think this is my seat. Could we check our tickets? — Oh, sorry. — No problem.**
5. **When do we need to be back? — Eight fifty. — I'll stay here. See you at our seats before then.**
6. **Okay. We'll wait here.**

**Check:** Did the name, number and performance identify the tickets? Did your seat request account for the view? Did the meeting plan fit the return time?
