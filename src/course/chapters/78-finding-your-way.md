# CH78 — Finding Your Way

**Outcome:** Ask for directions, follow a short route, and check a location you are unsure about.  

## Study

Use a clear destination: “Excuse me, how do I get to the bus station?” “Is the clinic this way?” “Could you show me where that is?” Someone may know, hesitate, or say “Sorry, I'm not sure.” That answer is different from giving you a route.

### Direction words

- **Go straight / straight ahead:** continue in the direction you face.
- **Turn left / right:** change direction to that side.
- **First / second left:** the first or second available left turn along the route described.
- **Go past:** continue beyond a place.
- **Cross the street:** move to the other side of the street.
- **Around the corner:** beyond a nearby turn or corner.
- **Next to:** beside. **Across from / opposite:** on the other side, facing.
- **Behind / in front of:** relative positions around a place or object.
- **Block:** the section between neighboring street intersections in the route here.

Directions depend on where you start and which way you face. “On your left” is not an unchanging property of a building.

### A complete fictional layout

You start at Main Street and Oak Street, facing north. Pine Street is one block north of Oak. Cedar Street is one further block north. All three cross Main Street.

- The **clinic** is on Main, just past Pine, on your left as you walk north.
- The **cafe** is on Pine, half a block east of Main.
- The **bus station** is on Cedar, one block west of Main.

Here **north** is the direction you face; **east** is to your right and **west** to your left at the starting point.

**Visitor:** How do I get to the clinic?  
**Resident:** Go straight along Main. Go past Pine Street. The clinic is just past that corner, on your left.  
**Visitor:** So I don't turn at Pine?  
**Resident:** Right. Keep going on Main.  
**Visitor:** Thanks.  

“Right” here confirms information; it does not instruct a right turn. Repeat a route detail if that meaning could be confusing.

### Ask again and give a manageable route

**Visitor:** And the cafe?  
**Resident:** Go one block to Pine, turn right, and continue half a block.  
**Visitor:** Could you say that more slowly?  
**Resident:** Main to Pine. Right at Pine. Half a block.  
**Visitor:** Got it.  

To direct someone to the station: “Go two blocks north to Cedar, then turn left. The station is one block along Cedar.” Landmarks can help, but do not invent one you cannot identify. “I'm not sure how far it is” is more useful than pretending certainty.

## Practice

Use the layout above; no external map is needed.

1. Ask for the clinic. Partner gives the model route. Confirm whether you turn at Pine.
2. Ask for the bus station. Partner asks “Where are you starting?” State Main and Oak, facing north. Follow the route in words.
3. A partner tells you “Turn left at Pine to reach the cafe.” Check that against the layout and correct the direction.
4. Give one route without looking at the model. Later start the explanation by stating your starting point again.

Solo: read a visitor question and give directions out loud; then reverse roles and summarize the route you were given.

**Possibilities:** “I'm starting at Main and Oak.” “Isn't the cafe east of Main? Should I turn right?” “Two blocks to Cedar, then left for one block.” Check the start, turn, distance, and destination. A different wording can describe the same correct route.
