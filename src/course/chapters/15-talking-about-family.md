# CH15 — Talking About Family

**Your goal:** Describe family relationships and contact, ask a useful follow-up, or keep personal information private.  

## Study: Ask and give a short account

Chapter 14 supplies relationship words. For this chapter, a small set is enough: **parents**, **siblings**, **brother**, **sister**, **children**, **grandparents**, and any relationship that matters to your account.

- **Do you have any siblings?** Ask about brothers or sisters.
- **I have an older brother and a younger sister.** Describe the relationship and age order.
- **I'm an only child.** Say you have no siblings.
- **I'm the youngest.** Identify your position among the children.
- **Do you have any children?** Ask whether someone has children.
- **I have two kids.** Give a short conversational answer.

**Two classmates talking**

**Hana:** Do you have any siblings?  
**Miles:** Yes, one older sister. How about you?  
**Hana:** I have an older brother and a younger sister. I'm the middle child.  
**Miles:** Do they live nearby?  
**Hana:** My sister does. My brother lives in another city.  

**Nearby** means not far away. **My sister does** is a short answer meaning she lives nearby. You can use the full sentence if that is easier.

## Study: Talk about contact and closeness

**Are you close to your family?** asks about the relationship, not just physical distance. People can be close emotionally and live far apart.

- **We talk almost every day.** Describe frequent contact.
- **I see my parents twice a month.** Describe visits.
- **We grew up together.** Describe childhood experience.
- **We get along well.** Describe a positive relationship.
- **We're not very close.** Describe limited closeness.
- **We're not in touch right now.** Say you are not communicating now.

**Miles:** Are you close to your brother?  
**Hana:** Yes. We don't see each other much, but we talk every week.  
**Miles:** That's nice.  
**Hana:** How about you and your sister?  
**Miles:** It's complicated. We get along better now than we used to.  

You can describe a difficult relationship without turning it into a complete personal story. **It's complicated** signals that there is more to it; it does not supply the details.

## Study: Extended family and family names

**We're visiting my grandparents this weekend** names a planned visit. **My aunt will be there too** adds another relative. Ask **Do you have grandchildren?** before assuming a person does; **How many?** can follow a yes answer.

**Miles:** Do you have grandchildren?  
**Pat:** Yes. I have a grandson and a granddaughter.  
**Miles:** Do you get to see them often?  
**Pat:** About once a week.  

**Mom**, **Dad**, **Grandma**, **Grandpa**, and other family names depend on the person's family. You can say **my mother** or **my father** when describing the relationship. **This is my mom, Renee** introduces the person using your family name for her. You do not need to replace the name your family actually uses.

## Study: Keep a boundary

Family questions can be personal. You can give a short answer, change the subject, or state that you do not want to discuss it.

**Hana:** Do you have children?  
**Miles:** No. I'd rather not talk about that right now.  
**Hana:** Okay. No problem. How's your class going?  

More direct **That's personal** is also available if you intend a firm boundary. You can ask **Would you rather talk about something else?** when the other person's response leaves you unsure. A short answer is not an invitation to demand an explanation.

## Practice: Give only the details you choose

Start with this invented profile: **You have an older brother and a younger sister. Your sister lives nearby. Your brother lives far away. You speak to him once a week. You and your sister get along well.**

1. Answer **Do you have any siblings?** Include your place among the three children.
2. Answer **Do they live nearby?** Distinguish the two siblings.
3. Answer **Are you close to your brother?** Your role feels close despite the distance. Supply a reason from the profile.
4. Change the role: you are an only child. Answer without using the first profile's siblings.
5. Change the intention: you do not want to discuss your family. Rehearse a boundary and the other person's acknowledgment. Support: **I'd rather not talk about my family**, **Okay**, **Let's talk about something else**.
6. Hide the profile. Give a short true or invented family account using three details. On your own, then ask and answer one follow-up that stays within those details.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Yes. I have an older brother and a younger sister. I'm the middle child.**
2. **My sister lives nearby, but my brother lives far away.**
3. **Yes. We talk once a week even though he lives far away.**
4. **No, I'm an only child.**
5. **I'd rather not talk about my family right now. — Okay. Let's talk about something else.**

**Check:** Did you describe actual relationships and contact? Did your follow-up fit the information given? Could you participate without revealing details you wanted to keep private?
