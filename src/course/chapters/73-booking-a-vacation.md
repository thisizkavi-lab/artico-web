# CH73 — Booking a Vacation

**Outcome:** Describe the trip you want, compare an offer, and ask about conditions before deciding.  

## Study

A **package** combines specified travel services. **All-inclusive** is a description of an offer; ask exactly what it includes. **Cancellation policy** describes conditions for canceling. **Refundable** means a payment can be returned under stated conditions. **Deposit** is an initial payment, not necessarily the whole price.

### Explain your priorities

**Traveler:** I'm looking for a four-night beach vacation in July.  
**Agent:** For how many people?  
**Traveler:** Two adults and a child. We'd like a quiet place, but with activities for kids.  
**Agent:** What's your budget?  
**Traveler:** Around eighteen hundred dollars for the accommodation. Flights are separate.  
**Agent:** Would you consider a package?  
**Traveler:** Yes, if the price and cancellation conditions work for us.  

You can say “We'd rather avoid a party resort,” “I'm interested in a city trip,” or “We're considering a hiking tour.” **Cruise**, **camping trip**, **ski trip**, **road trip**, **spa getaway**, and **safari** describe different kinds of travel. The title of an offer is a starting point, not enough information to book.

### Compare the details

Fictional offers for this exercise:

- **Harbor Stay:** four nights, $1,600; breakfast included; children welcome; cancellation terms supplied separately.
- **Quiet Cove:** four nights, $1,700; adults only; meals included except room service; flights and transfers excluded.

**Traveler:** What does “meals included” cover?  
**Agent:** At Quiet Cove, restaurant meals are included, but room service isn't.  
**Traveler:** Is it suitable for a child?  
**Agent:** That property is adults only.  
**Traveler:** Then it won't work for us. Tell me about Harbor Stay.  

Ask “What's the total after taxes and fees?” “Are airport transfers included?” and “Are those dates available?” A **transfer** here can be transportation between an airport and accommodation, not a train connection.

For access needs: “We need a step-free room. Could you confirm the room features?” “I'm traveling with a service dog. Who can confirm the arrangements?” These requests do not replace checking actual accessibility or eligibility details.

### Do not decide before you understand

**Traveler:** What happens if we need to cancel?  
**Agent:** I'll send the conditions in writing.  
**Traveler:** Please do. I'd like to read them before paying.  
**Agent:** Would you like me to hold the offer?  
**Traveler:** Is there a charge, and how long would you hold it?  

“Let's book it” commits to proceeding. “I'd like to think about it” does not. “That price is higher than I expected” reports a concern without agreeing. For international travel, ask “Where can I verify the entry requirements for my passport?” The correct answer depends on the actual traveler and destination; this lesson gives no visa rule.

## Practice

Use the family role and the two fictional offers.

1. Describe your dates, party, and accommodation budget. Partner asks “Are flights included in that budget?” Clarify.
2. Partner recommends Quiet Cove. Identify the conflict with your needs and request the other offer.
3. Ask two questions still unanswered by Harbor Stay's card. Partner replies “I'll send those details.” Explain what you need before agreeing.
4. Repeat the task as a solo traveler who prefers an adults-only stay. Your decision can now differ.

Alone, read the agent prompts and answer out loud, then make a short comparison with the cards hidden.

**Possibilities:** “Flights are separate.” “We're traveling with a child, so adults only won't work.” “Please send the cancellation terms before I book.” Check that your choice follows your priorities and the stated facts. No invented package is a real offer or a universal booking policy.
