# CH24 — At a Restaurant

**Your goal:** Reserve or request a table, order with relevant needs, explain a problem, and settle the check.  

## Study: Get a table

**Host:** How many people are in your party?  
**Guest:** Four. We have a reservation for seven under Chen.  
**Host:** Yes, I have it here. Your table is ready.  

Here, **party** means your group. **Under Chen** gives the reservation name. Without a reservation, try **Do you have a table for two?** The answer may include a wait: **About twenty minutes**. You can ask **Can we put our name on the list?** or decide **We'll try somewhere else, thanks**.

For a future visit: **I'd like to make a reservation for four this Friday at seven**. Staff may confirm a date or offer another time. Ask about a practical need instead of assuming it: **Do you have outdoor seating?**, **Is there parking nearby?**, **Can you accommodate a group of eight?**

## Study: Order and ask about ingredients

Use this fictional menu and information in Practice:

| Item | Menu description and practice price |
| --- | --- |
| Tomato soup | Tomatoes, onion, broth and cream; $6 |
| Vegetable pasta | Pasta with vegetables and tomato sauce; $15 |
| Grilled fish | Served with rice and salad; $19 |
| Side salad | Greens and tomatoes; dressing served separately; $5 |
| Apple dessert | Baked apples with vanilla ice cream; $7 |

**Scene facts:** The restaurant can leave the cream out of the soup. Its kitchen handles peanuts, and staff cannot assure the guest that peanut cross-contact is prevented. Cash and cards are accepted; separate checks are available if requested before ordering. These are facts for this practice scene.  

**Server:** Are you ready to order?  
**Guest:** Could I ask about the soup first? Can you make it without cream?  
**Server:** Yes, we can leave that out.  
**Guest:** Then I'll have the soup without cream and the vegetable pasta.  

**I'll have…**, **I'd like…**, and **Could I get…?** work for orders. **On the side** asks for something separately: **Could I have the dressing on the side?** A menu **entrée** can mean the main dish in American usage.

An allergy statement needs more precision than a dislike:

**Guest:** I have a peanut allergy. Does the dish contain peanuts, and can you check how it's prepared?  
**Server:** It has no peanuts in the recipe, but we handle peanuts in the kitchen and can't assure you there won't be cross-contact.  
**Guest:** Thanks for checking. I won't order food here today.  

**Cross-contact** means an allergen gets into food unintentionally, including through shared preparation equipment. Ingredient absence and a statement about preparation are different information. The exchange practices asking and responding; a menu name alone cannot establish suitability for a particular allergy.

## Study: Name objects and requests

Ask **Could we have some water?** or **Could I get another fork?** rather than wait for a server to guess.

| At the table | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Menu / wine list | Read food / wine choices |
| Napkin | Wipe your mouth or hands |
| Fork, knife and spoon | Eating utensils |
| Plate / side plate | Main plate / smaller plate for an accompanying item |
| Water glass / wine glass | Glasses for the named drink |
| Pitcher of water | Container holding water to pour |
| Salt and pepper | Seasonings you may request |

**We're still deciding** explains that you need time. **Could we have a few more minutes?** requests it.

## Study: Evaluate or report a problem

**The fish is delicious** gives a positive evaluation. **It's a little too salty for me** or **It's nothing special** expresses a less positive one. You can have different opinions from your companion.

For something that needs attention, identify the mismatch:

**Guest:** Excuse me. I ordered the vegetable pasta, but this is fish.  
**Server:** I'm sorry. I'll check the order.  
**Guest:** Thank you. Could you bring the pasta instead?  

Other reports include **This soup is cold**, **This glass is dirty**, and **We haven't received the salad yet**. **Could you replace it?** requests a remedy. A clear problem report is useful; you do not need to call the food wonderful before describing it.

## Study: Finish and pay

**Server:** Would you like dessert?  
**Guest:** No, thanks. I'm full. Could we get the check?  
**Server:** Of course.  

**Check** and **bill** identify the amount to pay. Ask **Can we have separate checks?** for each person's charges, or **Can we split the check evenly?** to divide the total equally. These are different requests, and availability depends on the restaurant. **This one's on me** offers to pay. A companion can accept or say **I'd prefer to pay for my own meal**.

You can ask **Is a service charge included?**, **Do you take cards?**, and **Could I have a receipt?** This lesson does not set a universal charge or tipping amount.

## Practice: Complete a restaurant visit

Speak both roles alone, using the menu and scene facts above.

1. Arrive for the four-person Chen reservation at seven. The host asks about your party and reservation name.
2. Order soup without cream and vegetable pasta. Ask for a few more minutes before deciding on dessert.
3. Change the need: your character has a peanut allergy. Ask about ingredients and preparation. Staff give the supplied kitchen information; respond without turning their uncertainty into a safety guarantee.
4. You ordered pasta but received fish. Describe the mistake and request the correct dish. Staff agree to check it.
5. You and a companion disagree about the fish. One thinks it is delicious; the other finds it too salty. Then decline dessert and request the check.
6. Hide the models. Repeat the visit with a two-person walk-in party, grilled fish for your order, and separate checks requested before ordering. If told the wait is twenty minutes, accept or leave.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Four people. We have a reservation under Chen for seven.**
2. **Can you make the soup without cream? — Yes. — I'll have that and the vegetable pasta.**
3. **Can you check how it's prepared?** After the supplied answer: **Thanks. I won't order food here today.**
4. **I ordered vegetable pasta, but this is fish. Could you bring the pasta instead?**
5. **It's delicious. — It's too salty for me.** Later: **No dessert, thanks. Could we get the check?**
6. **Do you have a table for two? Could we have separate checks when we order?**

**Check:** Did you distinguish an ingredient preference from an allergy question? Did your problem report match what happened? Did both people understand how they intended to pay?
