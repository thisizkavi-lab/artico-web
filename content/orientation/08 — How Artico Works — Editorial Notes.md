# How Artico Works — editorial notes

## What the author supplied

The source ramble established these convictions:

- Artico begins with beginners but must respect multiple entry levels.
- The Foundation can be skipped, revisited, or briefly previewed.
- Letters and sounds should be introduced before pronunciation practice.
- Tongue twisters begin the Practice path and require repeated work over time.
- Social Fluency should use frequent, reusable dialogues, phrases, and words.
- Theory should provide big picture, small picture, relevance, explanation, and embedded natural audio.
- Practice should receive most of the learner's time and give exact instructions.
- The course should be self-contained at lesson level.
- Real-world missions should move language beyond the screen.
- Artico should behave like an integrated course, instructor, motivator, and philosophical guide.

## Structural decision

The ramble used “two sections” in more than one sense. The compiled chapter separates two independent dimensions:

| Dimension | Part 1 | Part 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curriculum stage | Foundation | Social Fluency |
| Learning mode | Learn | Practice |

This makes the product logic coherent. Tongue twisters can begin early in Practice and continue while Social Fluency progresses in Learn. Foundation is therefore not a long gate in front of useful everyday language.

## Sound-count fact check

Use the learner-facing wording **“approximately 44 phonemes in a common teaching model”**, not “English has exactly 44 sounds” and not “46 sounds.”

- The British Council's TeachingEnglish phonemic-chart resource provides posters for 44 phonemic symbols: https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/teaching-resources/teaching-secondary/teaching-tools/phonemic-chart-english-teachers
- Cambridge University Press describes English as having “approximately 44” phonemes and 26 alphabet letters, while discussing accent variation: https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/02255/excerpt/9781108402255_excerpt.pdf
- Cambridge's learner reference notes that English is spoken with many regional accents: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/pronunciation

The number is a teaching convention tied to an accent model and phonological analysis. The course should teach the distinction between letters, phones, and phonemes without pretending that all English varieties have one fixed inventory.

## Important reframes

### “Train the muscles”

Preserved as a physical-practice metaphor, but reframed as **coordination, transitions, and automaticity**. Tongue twisters do not enlarge a “language muscle”; they provide repeated articulatory and rhythmic practice.

### “At least a month”

Preserved as a suggested four-week routine, not an outcome guarantee. A month can organize returns; it cannot promise completion.

### “Talk like a New Yorker”

Preserved as a legitimate personal model or aesthetic ambition. It is not the course's universal definition of correct English and not a learner promise. The first reference target is clear, stable, socially comfortable speech.

### “Everything is self-contained”

Qualified as **lesson-level self-containment**. Every chapter should contain what its target requires, but the product should not claim to contain the entire language or eliminate the value of people, dictionaries, media, and real life.

### “Understand enough to become a teacher”

Preserved as a comprehension standard: explain the idea simply to another person. It is not a professional-teacher credential.

## Product implications

- Navigation should show both the curriculum stage and the current mode.
- Practice remains globally accessible and begins with Tongue Twisters.
- A learner may start Social Fluency while maintaining a Foundation routine.
- Foundation should offer `Preview`, `Start`, and `Skip for now`; no placement test is required.
- Every theory chapter needs a natural-audio slot.
- Every practice chapter needs a separately directed shadowing-audio slot.
- Every chapter needs a specific return cue and one real-world mission.
- No score, streak, fluency countdown, or native-accent promise should appear.
