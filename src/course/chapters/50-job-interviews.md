# CH50 — Job Interviews

**Outcome:** Give relevant answers about your background and strengths, clarify a question, and ask about the role.  

## Study

An interview question usually invites information, not just a polished adjective. **I'm reliable** states a quality; a short example shows what you mean. Connect your answer to the work being discussed.

### Introduce relevant experience

For practice, use this invented role: Sam worked in a café for a year, took orders, answered questions and organized supplies. Sam completed a business certificate, can work afternoons and weekends, and wants a bookstore role because of an interest in books and helping customers.

**Interviewer:** Tell me a little about yourself.  
**Sam:** I worked in a café for a year, mainly helping customers and taking orders. I also completed a business certificate. I'm interested in bringing that customer-service experience to a bookstore.  
**Interviewer:** Why did you apply for this position?  
**Sam:** I enjoy books, and I like helping people find what they need. The customer-facing work appeals to me.  

**Mainly** selects the relevant part of your background. **I'm interested in bringing...** connects past experience to the new task. You can mention education, volunteering or projects when those are your relevant examples; you do not need to pretend they were paid employment.

### Explain a strength with an example

Useful qualities include **organized**, **reliable**, **quick to learn**, and **good at explaining things**. Choose a claim you can support.

**Interviewer:** What is one of your strengths?  
**Sam:** I stay organized when it's busy. In the café, I kept track of several orders and checked the details before passing them to the kitchen.  
**Interviewer:** Can you give me an example?  
**Sam:** Once, a customer changed an order during a rush. I confirmed the change, updated the order and made sure the kitchen saw it.  

The example has a **situation**, an **action**, and a **result or check**. It need not have an impressive number. **I don't have an example from paid work, but I can describe a school project** is honest support for an early-career applicant.

### Respond to a challenge or uncertain question

**Interviewer:** What would you like to improve?  
**Applicant:** I'm working on speaking up earlier when I need information. I now write down questions before a meeting so I can ask them clearly.  
**Interviewer:** How would you handle an unfamiliar task?  
**Applicant:** I'd check the instructions and ask the appropriate person before proceeding.  

Avoid giving an invented story merely because a question asks for one. **Could you clarify what you mean by “technical experience”?** requests the intended meaning. **Would you like an example of teamwork or individual work?** checks the scope. **Let me think for a moment** gives you thinking time.

### Practical questions

Possible questions include **When could you start?**, **What hours can you work?**, **Why are you leaving your current role?**, and **What pay range are you looking for?** Supported answers include **I could start November first**, **I'm available afternoons and weekends**, and **My current position is temporary**.

**Interviewer:** What pay are you looking for?  
**Applicant:** Could you share the range budgeted for the role? I'd like to understand the responsibilities as well.  
**Interviewer:** We can discuss the range and duties.  
**Applicant:** Thank you.  

If your role has a range, state it. For an invented practice role: **I'm looking for twenty to twenty-three dollars an hour.** Use your own relevant figures for your situation. Giving a range is one response choice; asking about the budget is another.

### Ask your own questions and close

**What would a typical shift involve?**, **How is training organized?**, **What would you want the new person to focus on first?**, and **What are the next steps?** seek information about the work and process.

**Interviewer:** Do you have any questions for us?  
**Applicant:** Yes. How is training organized for new staff?  
**Interviewer:** The manager can explain our plan for this role.  
**Applicant:** Great. And what are the next steps after this interview?  
**Interviewer:** We'll let you know the follow-up process.  
**Applicant:** Thank you for your time.  

## Practice

Alone, read one interviewer question, cover the model, and answer aloud. Then take the interviewer role and ask a follow-up. With a partner, switch roles.

1. Answer **Tell me about yourself** from Sam's facts. Keep the focus on relevant work and study.
2. Explain being organized using the café example. The interviewer then asks **What did you do personally?** State Sam's own action.
3. You do not understand **technical experience**. Ask for clarification before answering.
4. Answer availability and start-date questions using afternoons/weekends and November 1. Ask about training and next steps.

**Sample possibilities:** “I have a year of café customer-service experience and a business certificate.” / “I confirmed and updated the changed order.” / “Could you clarify what you mean by technical experience?” / “I could start November first. How is training organized?”  

Return later with your own truthful example or another supplied role. Check that the answer addressed the question, the example supported the claim, and unknown information stayed a question rather than becoming an invented fact.
