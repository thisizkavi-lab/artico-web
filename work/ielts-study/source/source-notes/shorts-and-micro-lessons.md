## Writing and vocabulary: accuracy before display

- [Band 9 Essay Structure](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/nwLbNFc3QUQ) (`nwLbNFc3QUQ`): gives an opinion-essay scaffold—paraphrase and state the view, develop two reasons in body paragraphs, then recap. It is one optional structure for a prompt that asks for an opinion, not an official four-paragraph requirement or a Band 9 guarantee. See the [Task 2 synthesis](writing-task2-band9-guide.md).
- [Band 9 Linking Words](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/YazGUYpUZ7I) (`YazGUYpUZ7I`): contrasts forced linking-word display with using cohesive devices only when they express the intended relationship. The course target is accurate, unobtrusive cohesion; IELTS assesses how effectively ideas are organized and linked, not a linker quota. ([IELTS Writing criteria](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/writing-test-resources))
- [Band 9 Students Keep It SIMPLE](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/nEyDXfXApf4) (`nEyDXfXApf4`): warns that ambitious words used inaccurately can weaken communication and encourages confidence in clear language. Keep the accurate-use lesson, while still developing the range and flexibility described in the official descriptors.
- [Band 9 Tip: STOP Changing Every Word in Your IELTS Essay!](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/fPpyckKi2HI) (`fPpyckKi2HI`): warns against indiscriminate thesaurus substitution and recommends repeating a key word when alternatives are inaccurate. This supports the contextual synonym lesson; paraphrase only when meaning and grammar remain correct.
- [Complex Sentences Are NOT Complex](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/db4hYhao0OM) (`db4hYhao0OM`): encourages mixing clear simple clauses with some complex sentences and prioritizing accuracy. Its definition of sentence types is simplified; teach clause structure accurately and choose complexity when it expresses the intended relationship.
- [Do You Need Idioms for a High IELTS Writing Score?](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/h7bzPM6CmZ8) (`h7bzPM6CmZ8`): correctly warns against forcing idioms to chase a score, but overstates the point by saying never to use an idiom in Writing. Teach register and naturalness: avoid conversational expressions when they do not fit an academic or formal response, without making a blanket ban. IELTS assesses vocabulary range, accuracy, and appropriacy. ([IELTS Writing criteria](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/writing-test-resources))
- [Don’t Be a “Peacock Student!”](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DM8aLf2JfWA) (`DM8aLf2JfWA`): cautions against decorative high-level vocabulary, formal linkers, and memorized templates that the writer cannot control. Retain the accuracy-first lesson while still teaching appropriate lexical and grammatical range.

- [Most Common IELTS Task 2 Topics](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/f6RXyFfBfd8) (`f6RXyFfBfd8`; four seconds): YouTube and Glasp expose no usable captions; local ASR captures only an isolated “Oh.” The clip shows the title card, then an on-screen “Environment” topic card over scenery. ## Listening

- [Get a Band 9 in IELTS Listening with this Strategy (Micro-listening)](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/sG9dy3-_8_I) (`sG9dy3-_8_I`): choose a short piece of naturally spoken English, listen to one or two sentences repeatedly, write what you hear, and check against subtitles only after trying. This can train close listening and sound-to-word recognition; it is supplementary practice, not a replacement for IELTS task practice. The source’s restriction to native-speaker audio is unnecessary: use varied, understandable English accents and material suited to the learner.
- [Band 9 Students Know THIS about Listening](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/V1Uosk72mQU) (`V1Uosk72mQU`): reminds learners that Listening has four parts and 40 questions and suggests giving Parts 3–4 more preparation time. The format facts are official; the assertion that difficulty rises in a way that separates band levels and the proposed practice allocation are coaching heuristics. Set practice priorities from a learner’s own diagnostic results. ([IELTS Listening format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-listening); [Listening course note](listening-course-only-listening-course.md))

- [How To Use Your Prep Time for IELTS Speaking Part 2](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/rWBrbXrNHwk) (`rWBrbXrNHwk`): focus planning on the main cue-card topic and jot down a few easy-to-develop ideas. - [IELTS Examiners Care About 1 Thing #ieltsspeaking](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/f1AvXL3GO1I) (`f1AvXL3GO1I`): warns against memorized answers, forced idioms, “fancy” vocabulary, and accent imitation, then prioritizes communication. - [IELTS Examiners Care About 1 Thing #ieltwriting](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/-gQng6rbdhg) (`-gQng6rbdhg`): recommends doing Academic Task 1 last so candidates learn to select key data quickly. - [IELTS Listening Tip #1](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/ospL4_naJ2s) (`ospL4_naJ2s`): introduce single-play listening before the exam so learners practise recovering and moving on. IELTS Listening recordings are played once, but during early learning, replay and transcript review can help diagnose errors; gradually remove support for realistic simulation.
- [IELTS Practice Tip 1](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/aGdPmiqpt8U) (`aGdPmiqpt8U`): argues that repeated practice without correction can reinforce poor habits. Preserve the need for accurate feedback and a specific repair task; do not treat the driving anecdote or the claim that practice can make performance worse as direct evidence about IELTS learners.
- [IELTS Reading Quick Tip: Matching Headings](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/njY3AOI9urU) (`njY3AOI9urU`): suggests reading a passage, generating a short gist heading for each paragraph, and then comparing those with the supplied choices.
