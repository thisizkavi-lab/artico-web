# CH55 — Online Meetings

**Outcome:** Check participation in an online meeting, take a turn and explain a connection or screen-sharing problem.  

## Study

**Mute** stops your microphone sending sound in the meeting; **unmute** allows it again. **Share your screen** lets others see selected screen content through the meeting system. **Chat** refers to the meeting's text-message area. Use the platform's actual controls when needed; these models practice the conversation around them.

### Get started

**Host:** Hi, everyone. Can you hear me?  
**Participant:** Yes, I can. Can you hear me?  
**Host:** Yes. Let's get started. I'll share the agenda.  
**Participant:** I can see the agenda, but the text is small.  
**Host:** I'll enlarge it. Is that better?  
**Participant:** Yes, thanks.  

Supported responses include **I can hear you**, **I can't hear you**, **I can see the screen**, and **I can only see your name**. Explain which part is working rather than saying only **It's broken**.

### Take a turn

**Host:** Who would like to comment first?  
**Maya:** Could I go first? I have a question about the schedule.  
**Host:** Go ahead.  
**Maya:** Does the trial start this week or next week?  
**Lee:** Sorry, I interrupted. Please continue.  
**Maya:** Thanks. I was asking which week it starts.  

**Go ahead** gives the other person the turn. **Please continue** returns it after an interruption. When talking overlaps: **I think we spoke at the same time. You go first** helps reset the exchange. You can also say **Could I finish my question?** if you need to keep the turn.

### Connection problems

**Participant:** Your audio is cutting out. I missed the last part.  
**Host:** Which part did you hear?  
**Participant:** I heard “the trial starts,” but not the date. Could you repeat it?  
**Host:** It starts Monday. I'll put that in the chat as well.  
**Participant:** Thanks. Monday, got it.  

**Cutting out** or **breaking up** describes interrupted sound. **The screen is frozen** describes an image that has stopped updating. **I got disconnected** explains leaving unintentionally. A text route can help: **Could you put the key point in the chat?**

**Participant:** Sorry, I got disconnected. What did I miss?  
**Coworker:** We confirmed the Monday start.  
**Participant:** Thanks. Was anything assigned to me?  
**Coworker:** Not yet. We're discussing that now.  

These are written role-play lines. Reading them does not test whether you can hear an actual poor connection; real listening practice comes with audio.

### Confirm the next step

**Host:** We'll meet again next Tuesday at three.  
**Participant:** Could you include the time zone in the invitation?  
**Host:** Yes, I'll include it.  
**Participant:** Thanks. Could you also send the action-item summary?  
**Host:** I will.  

**Which time zone is that?** asks an important missing detail. **Would you send an invite?** requests the meeting information instead of assuming everyone already has it.

## Practice

Read the host or coworker's line and respond aloud. Alone, switch roles. No live meeting or recording is needed.

1. The host asks whether you can hear and see the agenda. You can hear, but cannot see the screen. State both facts.
2. You missed the date after **The trial starts...** Request that specific part and ask for it in the chat.
3. You return after disconnecting. Ask what you missed and whether a task was assigned to you.
4. A participant interrupts your question. Ask to finish, or give them the turn and return to your question afterward.
5. The next meeting is Tuesday at 3, but no time zone was stated. Ask for it and an invitation.

**Sample possibilities:** “I can hear you, but I can't see the agenda.” / “Could you repeat the start date and put it in the chat?” / “What did I miss? Was anything assigned to me?” / “Could I finish my question?” / “Which time zone is that? Could you send an invite?”  

Repeat later with a different missing detail. Check whether your explanation identified the problem and enabled the other person to repair the communication.
