# CH03 — Conversation Fillers and Listening Signals

**Your goal:** Give yourself thinking time, organize a turn, and show how you are following another person's story.  

## Study: Small words can do different jobs

A conversation does not have to run without pauses. Small words can help organize it, but adding one to every sentence is not the goal. Some so-called fillers communicate a real attitude or change of direction.

| Expression | A function in this chapter | Original example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Well...** | Begin a thoughtful or qualified answer | **Well, I liked the ending, but the beginning was slow.** |
| **So...** | Move to a topic or next step | **So, what should we do next?** |
| **Okay...** | Acknowledge something or begin an action | **Okay, I'll send the message.** |
| **I mean...** | Clarify what you mean | **It was expensive. I mean, more than I expected.** |
| **Actually...** | Add a correction or contrast | **Actually, the meeting is on Thursday.** |
| **I guess...** | Present a less certain judgment | **The movie was okay, I guess.** |

These are examples of functions, not permanent translations. **Okay** can acknowledge information without enthusiastic agreement. **Actually** can correct someone; consider how the correction fits the relationship.

**Making a choice**

**Ari:** Did you enjoy the concert?  
**Lee:** Well, the music was great. The room was just too crowded.  
**Ari:** Oh, right.  
**Lee:** I mean, I enjoyed it, but I wouldn't choose that venue again.  

Lee does not simply answer yes or no. The small expressions help connect the positive and negative parts.

## Study: Casual speech and shades of meaning

**Kind of** can make an evaluation less definite: **I kind of liked it**. **To be honest** signals that you are presenting a candid view: **I'm too tired to go, to be honest**. Neither automatically makes the following comment gentle.

Informal **like** has several uses. **It took, like, twenty minutes** gives an approximate amount. **I was like, “Are we at the right place?”** presents a reaction, thought or reported words; it need not be an exact quotation. You can try these in an informal story. You do not have to copy them to make every sentence sound American.

**You know** can appeal to something you expect the listener to understand: **It was one of those days, you know?** The listener may still need an explanation.

## Study: Show you are listening

| Response | Possible meaning in a story |
| --- | --- |
| **Uh-huh.** | I'm following; keep going. |
| **Really?** | That interests or surprises me. |
| **Wow!** | That is striking. |
| **Oh no.** | That sounds unfortunate. |
| **That sounds fun.** | A positive reaction to an activity. |

**Lee:** We took the train to the coast.  
**Ari:** Uh-huh.  
**Lee:** But we got off at the wrong station.  
**Ari:** Oh no! What happened next?  
**Lee:** We walked to the beach. It was only ten minutes away.  
**Ari:** Oh, that's good.  

Following a story is different from agreeing with an opinion. **Uh-huh** alone does not tell Lee whether Ari approves of the decision.

## Study: Short checks at the end

A **tag question** adds a short question to a statement. Start with these complete patterns:

- **The class starts at six, doesn't it? — Yes, it does. / No, it starts at seven.**
- **You're coming tomorrow, aren't you? — Yes, I am. / No, I can't.**
- **You haven't seen my bag, have you? — No, I haven't. / Yes, it's by the door.**

Use the reply to give the actual information. You can also ask a full question such as **Does the class start at six?** if the tag feels difficult.

## Practice: Follow the meaning

Practice alone by speaking the story and then each response. With a partner, change roles.

1. Ari asks whether you liked a movie. Your role liked the acting but found the story confusing. Begin with **Well** and give both parts. Useful words: **The acting was good. The story was confusing.**
2. Add one listening response after each event: **We won two tickets. / Then we missed the bus. / A friend gave us a ride.** Choose a reaction that fits each event.
3. Correct this detail: **The class is on Friday.** Your information says Thursday. Start with **Actually** and supply the correct day.
4. Someone asks **You're coming tomorrow, aren't you?** You cannot come. Answer and give the true information.
5. Hide the models. Tell a three-sentence story about a concert or trip. Add one thinking or organizing expression only where it serves a purpose. Then respond as the listener.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Well, the acting was good, but the story was confusing.**
2. **Wow! / Oh no! / Oh, that's good.**
3. **Actually, the class is on Thursday.**
4. **No, I can't.**

**Check:** Did your small words help the listener follow? Did the reactions fit the events? Could you give the correct information even when a question suggested a different answer?
