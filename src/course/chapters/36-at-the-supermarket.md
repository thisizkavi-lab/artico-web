# CH36 — At the Supermarket

**Outcome:** Find or request a grocery item, answer checkout questions, and explain a simple scanning or product problem.  

## Study

**Where can I find...?** asks for an item's location. **Do you carry...?** asks whether the store sells it. **Do you have any more...?** asks about additional stock. You can begin with **Excuse me** when approaching a staff member.

Useful department words are **produce** for fruit and vegetables, **dairy** for foods such as milk and cheese, **frozen foods**, **bakery**, **deli**, **household supplies**, and **pet food**. An **aisle** is a passage between shelves; **checkout** is where you pay.

### Locating and requesting items

For practice, this fictional store has produce near the entrance, dairy in aisle 2, cereal in aisle 4, and pet food in aisle 7.

**Customer:** Excuse me, where can I find oat milk?  
**Employee:** Aisle two, with the other milk.  
**Customer:** Thanks. Do you have any more of this yogurt?  
**Employee:** Let me check. Which flavor?  
**Customer:** Plain, please. The small containers.  
**Employee:** We have a few in the back. I'll bring them out.  

**Which flavor?**, **What size?**, and **How many?** ask for details. Supply the detail rather than repeating only the original request: **Vanilla**, **The large container**, **Two, please**.

### At checkout

The next scene assumes a fictional total of $18.40 and a store that offers paper bags.

**Cashier:** Did you find everything?  
**Customer:** Almost. I couldn't find the cereal.  
**Cashier:** It's in aisle four. Would you like to get it?  
**Customer:** No, that's okay. I'll get it another time.  
**Cashier:** Do you need a bag?  
**Customer:** No, thanks. I brought my own.  
**Cashier:** Your total is eighteen forty.  
**Customer:** Can I pay cash?  
**Cashier:** Yes. Would you like the receipt?  
**Customer:** Yes, please.  

Other useful replies include **Yes, everything, thanks**, **One bag, please**, and **I have a rewards card. Can you scan it?** Do not assume every store has the same rewards program, accepted payments or bag policy; ask about the one you are using.

### Asking for help

**This item won't scan** describes a scanning problem. **This carton is leaking** describes a product problem. **The price on the shelf was different** reports a price mismatch without claiming you know why.

**Customer:** Excuse me, this item won't scan.  
**Employee:** I'll check it for you.  
**Customer:** Thanks. The shelf label said three dollars, too.  
**Employee:** Let me verify the price.  

You can request attention more firmly when needed: **I need help with this checkout, please.** Technical steps depend on the machine; describing the problem is enough for this language task.

## Practice

Read staff lines aloud and supply customer turns. Alone, rehearse both roles.

1. Use the store directory to ask for cereal and pet food. The employee gives their aisle numbers. Confirm one number with **Aisle four?** or **Did you say seven?**
2. At checkout, you found everything, brought a bag and want a receipt. Answer the three corresponding questions from the model.
3. Your milk carton is leaking. Ask for help. The employee says **I can get another one.** Accept or say you no longer want milk.

**Sample possibilities:** “Where's the cereal?” — “Aisle four.” — “Aisle four? Thanks.” / “Yes, everything, thanks. I brought my own bag. Yes, a receipt, please.” / “This carton is leaking. Could you help me?” — “I can get another one.” — “Yes, please.”  

Later, change the item and checkout preferences. Check that your request included the detail the staff member needed.
