# CH20 — Eating and Drinking

**Your goal:** Retrieve a useful set of drink, equipment, preparation and menu words for the food task you want to do.  

## Study: Drinks and additions

| Word or phrase | Short meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **black coffee** | Coffee without added milk or cream. |
| **coffee with milk / cream** | Specify the addition you want. |
| **espresso** | A concentrated coffee made by forcing hot water through ground coffee. |
| **latte / cappuccino** | Espresso drinks made with milk; a cappuccino includes a layer of milk foam. Recipes vary. |
| **iced coffee / iced tea** | Coffee or tea served cold, usually with ice. |
| **herbal tea** | A drink made by infusing herbs or other plant ingredients. |
| **hot chocolate / milkshake** | A hot chocolate drink / a thick cold blended drink commonly made with milk and ice cream. |
| **juice / soda / sparkling water** | Fruit or vegetable liquid / a soft drink / water with bubbles. |
| **whole milk / skim milk** | Milk types with different fat content; skim milk has most of the fat removed. |
| **oat milk / soy milk / almond milk** | Plant-based drinks named for their ingredients. |
| **reusable cup** | A cup designed to be used more than once. |

**No sugar**, **with milk**, and **without ice** add details to an order. Chapter 21 practices the full exchange.

## Study: Tools and actions

| Tool | A useful action or purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **knife; cutting board** | **Slice** into thin pieces; **chop** into pieces on the board. |
| **peeler; grater** | **Peel** off a skin; **grate** into small shreds. |
| **mixing bowl; spoon** | **Mix** ingredients; **stir** with a circular motion. |
| **whisk** | Beat or blend ingredients with a wire tool. The word is also the action. |
| **measuring cup; kitchen scale** | Measure volume; weigh ingredients. |
| **colander; strainer** | Drain or separate liquid from solid pieces. A colander is a bowl with holes. |
| **skillet / frying pan; saucepan** | A relatively shallow pan; a deeper pan for liquid or other food. |
| **blender; mixer** | Blend food with blades; mix ingredients with a machine. |
| **baking dish; microwave; toaster** | A dish for the oven; an appliance that heats food; an appliance for toasting bread. |
| **French press** | A coffeepot with a plunger that separates the grounds from the drink. |

Other useful actions are **pour** (make liquid flow into something), **roll out** (flatten dough), and **burn** (damage food with too much heat). These are vocabulary meanings, not a complete recipe.

## Study: Preparation words on a menu

**Baked** and **roasted** describe oven cooking. **Grilled** describes cooking on a grill; **broiled** describes direct radiant heat, for example under an oven broiler. **Fried** means cooked in hot fat or oil. **Stir-fried** means cooked quickly in a lightly oiled pan while the food is kept moving. **Deep-fried** means cooked submerged in hot fat or oil. **Boiled** means cooked in boiling liquid; **poached** means cooked gently in liquid; **stewed** means cooked slowly in liquid; **steamed** means cooked using steam. **Marinated** means the food has been left in a seasoned liquid. **Smoked** describes treatment with smoke.

You can ask **Is it baked or fried?** without knowing every cooking method. Chapter 25 develops recipe and cooperation language.

## Study: Eating out

- **Appetizer / starter:** an opening dish.
- **Main course / entrée:** the principal dish; entrée has this restaurant meaning in the U.S.
- **Side / side dish:** food served alongside another dish.
- **Dessert:** a sweet course or dish. **Pudding** can name a particular dessert, rather than all desserts.
- **Reservation:** an arrangement to hold a table or other place.
- **Check / bill:** the restaurant statement of what you owe.
- **Split the check:** divide payment between people.

**Vegetarian** food excludes meat and fish; **vegan** food excludes animal-derived ingredients. **Dairy-free** describes avoiding milk products. **Gluten-free** describes food without gluten or with gluten below an applicable labeling limit. Gluten is a group of proteins found in wheat, barley and rye. These labels describe different needs; gluten-free does not mean free from every allergen. Ask about the actual ingredients and preparation when those details matter. Chapter 24 supplies those questions.

## Practice: Select and retrieve

Use this invented order: **An iced tea with no sugar; baked vegetables as the main dish; a side of rice; fruit for dessert.**

1. Identify the drink, cooking method, main dish, side and dessert. Say the order in your own words.
2. Choose tools for three language tasks: slice an apple, weigh flour, and drain pasta. Explain each choice with **I'd use...**.
3. A customer asks **Is it baked or fried?** The scene card says baked. Reply with the correct method.
4. Pick five words you would actually need at a café, restaurant or in a kitchen. Cover their definitions and explain each without looking.
5. Make your own short order or preparation description using only that subset. Change one detail and say it again. If alone, ask and answer **What would you like?** or **What do you need?** yourself.

### Sample possibilities

1. **I'd like an unsweetened iced tea, baked vegetables with a side of rice, and fruit for dessert.**
2. **A knife and cutting board; a kitchen scale; a colander.**
3. **It's baked.**

**Check:** Did the word identify the drink, tool or method you meant? Could you choose a small useful set and retrieve it in a food context, rather than only repeat a long list?
