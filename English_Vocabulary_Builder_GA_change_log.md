# General American vocabulary update — complete change log

## Scope

This log records the editorial update applied to English_Vocabulary_Builder_vocabularies.md. The original book extraction is preserved as the baseline; this log documents every American normalization and every genuinely new practical entry added to the learner-facing edition.

- Baseline lesson entries: **3609**
- American-facing lesson entries after within-lesson deduplication: **3562**
- New practical entry occurrences: **649**
- Unique new practical entries: **595**
- Replacement/normalization occurrences documented: **102**
- Lessons covered: **78**

## What changed

1. British-first vocabulary was normalized to the General American term when the U.S. term is clearly more natural for everyday learners.
2. American spelling was made primary (color, gray, program, theater, license, jewelry, fiber, counseling, and similar forms).
3. Everyday service, school, work, travel, health, neighborhood, food, and technology vocabulary was added.
4. Source terms were not silently erased. Regional and specialized items remain available in the baseline and are retained in the American-facing lesson list unless a clearer U.S. primary form replaces them.

## De-emphasis and removals

- **No source vocabulary was silently removed.** The source file remains the audit baseline.
- Rare animals, specialist equipment, formal academic terms, science terms, idioms, and culture-specific terms are retained because they belong to the book’s topic lessons. They are not automatically presented as the first words to memorize.
- “Replacement” means the American-facing edition puts the U.S. term first and keeps the source form in a recognition note; it does not claim that the source form is universally incorrect.

## Research and editorial basis

- [Corpus of Contemporary American English (COCA) overview](https://www.english-corpora.org/COCA/help/coca2020_overview.pdf) — used as the American usage reference for prioritizing spoken, media, web, and everyday contemporary language.
- [COCA search and genre guidance](https://www.english-corpora.org/coca/help/tour.asp) — supports checking frequency and context rather than relying on intuition alone.
- [U.S. Office of Personnel Management: Plain Language](https://www.opm.gov/information-management/plain-language) — recommends common, everyday words, clear organization, active language, and avoiding undefined technical terms.
- [Cambridge: British and American English](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/british-and-american-english) — reference for regional vocabulary and spelling contrasts.
- [Oxford 3000 — American English](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.co.uk/external/pdf/wordlists/oxford-3000-5000/American_Oxford_3000_by_CEFR_level.pdf) — a learner-oriented American-English priority reference for checking high-utility basic vocabulary.
- [Cambridge Dictionary: flat](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/flat) and [apartment](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/apartment) — examples of explicit regional labeling.

The corpus and learner-list sources were used as editorial checks and prioritization guides; this file does not claim that every individual entry is a fixed frequency rank. Where a source term is specialized or regionally marked, the practical American term is placed first and the source wording is retained for recognition.
