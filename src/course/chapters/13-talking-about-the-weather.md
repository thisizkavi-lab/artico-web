# CH13 — Talking About the Weather

**Your goal:** Describe conditions, react to someone else's weather comment, and distinguish a forecast from what is happening now.  

## Study: Describe the weather you mean

**What's the weather like there?** asks about conditions. **What's the temperature?** asks for a temperature. **How was the weather on your trip?** asks about a past experience.

Support from Chapter 11:

- **It's sunny / cloudy / windy / foggy.** Describe a condition.
- **It's chilly.** Describe rather cold weather.
- **It's pouring.** Say it is raining heavily.
- **It was a mix of sun and rain.** Describe changing conditions.
- **It's seventy degrees Fahrenheit.** Give a temperature with its scale.

**Two friends in different places**

**Mina:** What's the weather like there?  
**Ben:** It's sunny, but really windy.  
**Mina:** Is it warm?  
**Ben:** About sixty degrees Fahrenheit. It feels chilly in the wind.  
**Mina:** It's raining here. It's been a gray day.  

**About** marks an approximate temperature. If someone says only **It's thirty degrees**, ask **Fahrenheit or Celsius?** before interpreting it. Informal **It's freezing** or **It's boiling** can describe how cold or hot the person feels rather than give an exact measurement.

## Study: React without copying the opinion

**Beautiful day, isn't it?** invites a response to a positive weather comment. You can agree, qualify it, or express a different preference:

**Ben:** Beautiful day, isn't it?  
**Mina:** The sunshine is nice, but it's too hot for me.  
**Ben:** I like the heat.  
**Mina:** I prefer cooler weather.  

Other possible reactions are **It really is**, **I'm glad it's cooler**, **I wish it would stop raining**, or **I actually like the rain**. You do not have to share the same weather preference to keep talking.

For unpleasant conditions, **It's awful out there** gives a strong evaluation. **It's a little wet** gives a much milder one. Make the strength fit your experience or role.

## Study: Talk about a forecast

A **forecast** describes expected weather for a place and period. It is different from an observation such as **It's raining now**.

Useful forecast wording:

- **It's supposed to rain tomorrow.** Report an expectation.
- **There's a chance of showers.** Rain may occur; this is not a guarantee at your location.
- **Scattered showers are expected.** Showers are expected in parts of the area.
- **The high is sixty-eight.** Give the forecast maximum, with the scale clear from context.
- **The low is forty-five.** Give the forecast minimum.
- **Snow is expected overnight.** Report an expectation for the night.

**A plan changes**

**Mina:** The forecast says there's a chance of rain Saturday afternoon.  
**Ben:** Do you want to meet inside instead?  
**Mina:** Yes. How about the café?  
**Ben:** That works. Same time?  

The friends do not say rain is certain. They choose a plan that suits the information. Chapter 12 helps confirm the revised arrangement.

## Practice: Use the supplied information

This is an invented forecast for practice, not a report for a real location.

| Period | Conditions | Temperature information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saturday morning | Partly cloudy; chance of showers | 54°F in the morning |
| Saturday afternoon | Sunny and windy | Daytime high: 68°F |
| Saturday night | Mostly clear | Overnight low: 45°F |

1. Answer **What's the forecast for Saturday morning?** Use **There's a chance...**, not a statement that rain is already happening.
2. Tell a friend about the afternoon and include the high with its scale.
3. Your friend's current observation is **It's raining here now**. Report that observation separately from the Saturday forecast.
4. A friend says **Great, a windy afternoon!** Your role dislikes wind. Respond with a different view. Support: **I like the sunshine**, **I'm not a fan of the wind**, **It's too windy for me**.
5. You planned an outdoor morning meeting. Suggest an indoor alternative and supply the friend's acceptance. Support: **Could we meet at the café instead?**, **That works for me**.
6. Hide the table. Give a short summary covering morning, afternoon, and night. On your own, then take the friend's role and ask one follow-up question.

### Sample possibilities

1. **It'll be partly cloudy, with a chance of showers.**
2. **It's expected to be sunny and windy, with a high of sixty-eight degrees Fahrenheit.**
3. **It's raining there now. Saturday afternoon is expected to be sunny.**
4. **I like the sunshine, but it's too windy for me.**
5. **Could we meet at the café instead? — Sure. That works for me.**

**Check:** Did you identify the right place and period? Did you preserve uncertainty? Could the listener tell whether you were describing an observation, a forecast or a personal reaction?
