# CH75 — City Sightseeing

**Outcome:** Ask about an attraction, choose an admission option, and follow a short sightseeing explanation.  

## Study

**Admission** means entry to a place or event; an **admission fee** is its entry price. A **guided tour** is led by a guide. An **audio guide** provides recorded information. A **visitor center** or **tourist information office** provides local information. **Souvenir** means an item kept as a reminder of a place or experience.

### Check the attraction

**Visitor:** What time does the museum open?  
**Employee:** Ten today. The last entry is at four thirty.  
**Visitor:** And when does it close?  
**Employee:** Five.  
**Visitor:** Is there a guided tour this afternoon?  
**Employee:** At one and three. Admission and the tour are separate tickets.  
**Visitor:** How much would both cost for one adult?  

**Last entry** is the final time visitors are admitted; it can differ from closing time. Ask “Is the audio guide included?” rather than assuming it is. “Is there a step-free entrance?” asks about a specific access feature. You can follow up with “Which entrance should I use?” or “Could you tell me about the route inside?”

Fictional museum card: adult admission $12; tour $5 extra; audio guide $3; opens 10:00; last entry 4:30; closes 5:00; tours at 1:00 and 3:00; step-free entry at the garden entrance.

### Choose, or decide against it

**Visitor:** I'd like one admission ticket and the three o'clock tour.  
**Employee:** That's seventeen dollars.  
**Visitor:** Thanks. Where does the tour meet?  
**Employee:** By the information desk, ten minutes before it starts.  

“I'm just looking at the options” postpones a purchase. “That's more than I wanted to spend” states a budget concern. “We'll come back another day” changes the plan. A friendly offer is not an obligation to buy.

### Talk about what you see

A tour guide may say “On your right is the old courthouse” or “Across from it is the public garden.” On a **tour bus**, these expressions describe your current viewpoint. Check a location if you lose track: “Do you mean the building with the clock?”

**Guide:** On your left is the Harbor Gallery. The visitor center is next to it.  
**Visitor:** Is the market behind the gallery?  
**Guide:** Yes, and the gardens are across the street.  
**Visitor:** Great. Is there time to visit the market after the tour?  
**Guide:** It closes at six in this itinerary.  

The landmarks and timetable are fictional. A **map** or **guidebook** can help you point to a place; you can ask for one instead of pretending you have it.

## Practice

1. Use the museum card. Ask about last entry, the three o'clock tour, and the combined adult price. Partner supplies the card's details.
2. You want the step-free entrance. Ask where it is and what else you need to know. Partner answers “The garden entrance,” then offers to explain the inside route.
3. Rehearse the short guide exchange. With the model hidden, tell a companion where the market and gardens are relative to the gallery.
4. Change your budget: you only want admission. State that choice without apologizing for it.

Alone, read the employee's answers and create your visitor turns; then describe the fictional landmarks out loud.

**Possibilities:** “Is four thirty the closing time or the last entry?” “Just admission, please.” “The market is behind the gallery.” Check that the ticket, time, access question, and landmark relationship are clear. Directions receive fuller practice in Chapter 78.
