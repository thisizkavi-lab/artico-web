# CH91 — Using the Internet

**Outcome:** Ask about internet access, explain an online task, and clarify a form or request before acting.  

## Study

A **network** connects devices. A **hotspot** provides a connection point. **Internet service** is access supplied by a provider; **Wi-Fi** describes wireless networking, not necessarily a working internet connection. **Account**, **form**, **upload**, **download**, and **confirmation** describe online tasks. These words do not supply product-specific instructions.

### Ask for access

**Visitor:** Is there guest Wi-Fi I can use?  
**Employee:** Yes. The network is called Cedar Guest.  
**Visitor:** Is a password required?  
**Employee:** I'll give you the guest instructions.  
**Visitor:** I'm connected to the network, but this page won't load. Who can help?  
**Employee:** I'll check with support.  

With a friend: “Could I use your hotspot for a few minutes?” The answer could be “Yes,” “My data is limited,” or “I'd rather not.” Ask rather than assuming permission. At home, questions for a provider include “What does the monthly price include?” “Is equipment included?” and “What are the cancellation conditions?” Actual contract details require the provider's offer.

### Describe what you are doing

“I’m booking an appointment.” “I'm ordering groceries.” “I'm tracking a delivery.” “I'm paying a bill.” “I'm checking renewal information for my driver's license.” “I'm looking up recipes.” These state tasks; not every task is available online in every location.

**Friend:** What are you working on?  
**Student:** I'm uploading my essay, then joining a webinar.  
**Friend:** How's it going?  
**Student:** The upload hasn't finished. I'll check whether I get a confirmation.  

Here *How's it going?* can ask about the task's progress. A **webinar** is an online seminar. **Sign up** registers for something. **Livestream** is a live broadcast. For leisure: “I'm making a playlist,” “I'm downloading the tickets,” or “I'm checking my socials.” *Socials* is a conversational term for social-media accounts or activity, depending on context.

### Interpret a supplied form

Fictional course form: Name; email address; course choice; button labeled “Submit”; message after completion: “Registration received. A confirmation will be sent.” No payment field appears.

**Learner:** What does “Submit” do here?  
**Helper:** It sends the completed form to the course office.  
**Learner:** Is my place confirmed when I see “Registration received”?  
**Helper:** Not from that message alone. It says a confirmation will follow.  
**Learner:** Okay. Who should I ask if it doesn't arrive?  
**Helper:** The course office.  

A **draft** is not necessarily sent. **Received** does not always mean accepted, paid, or approved. Read the actual status wording rather than treating every button as the final step.

### Clarify safety or family settings

**Person:** This message asks me to use a link to update my account. I wasn't expecting it.  
**Friend:** Let's verify the request through contact information we already know is genuine.  
**Person:** I'll ask the company whether it sent the message.  

The FTC recommends checking an unexpected request through a known genuine contact channel rather than relying on details in the message. This is not a rule to share no information with any legitimate service. Ask “Why is this information needed?” when its purpose is unclear.

For family settings, ask, “Where can I find the official instructions for parental controls on this device?” Exact menus differ by device and service; use the instructions for the one you're using.

## Practice

1. Ask about Cedar Guest Wi-Fi. Partner gives the name but says they must supply instructions. Ask about the password and report a page that won't load.
2. Use the course form. Explain what Submit and Registration received mean in this fictional case. Ask who can confirm acceptance.
3. Describe two tasks from the work, everyday, or leisure examples. Partner asks about progress; report completed, pending, or uncertain status.
4. Explain why you want to verify an unexpected update message before acting on its link.

Solo: read the helper questions, answer aloud, then reconstruct the course task with the form hidden.

**Possibilities:** “The upload is still in progress.” “Received doesn't confirm my place yet.” “I'll contact the course office.” Check that the action, status, and remaining question are clear. No real account, transaction, or device setting is changed in this lesson.  

*Source note: Unexpected-message verification follows [FTC phishing guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams).*
