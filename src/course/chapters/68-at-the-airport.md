# CH68 — At the Airport

**Outcome:** Ask for help through an airport journey, understand key crew language, and give arrival information.  

## Study

A **boarding pass** identifies your flight and boarding details. **Check-in** confirms your travel booking; **bag drop** is where a checked bag is accepted. **Carry-on** means baggage carried into the cabin. **Gate** is the departure boarding point; **baggage claim** is the arrival area for checked bags. These terms do not tell you an airline's exact baggage rules.

### Check-in and bag drop

**Agent:** Where are you flying today?  
**Traveler:** Denver, on Flight 246. Is it on time?  
**Agent:** At the moment, yes. Are you checking a bag?  
**Traveler:** One suitcase. Where's the bag drop?  
**Agent:** At the counter to your left.  
**Traveler:** Could I have an aisle seat if one is available?  
**Agent:** I'll check. The window seat is still available, too.  

Ask “What's the baggage allowance for this ticket?” “Is there a fee for this bag?” and “Where is the check-in counter?” A **window seat** is beside the window; an **aisle seat** is beside the passage. “Any chance of an upgrade?” is a conversational request, not a claim to an upgrade.

### Security and the gate

Procedures vary. Use “What do I need to take out?” or “Could you show me where this goes?” to follow the instructions for your actual checkpoint. **Tray / bin** names a container; **scanner** names equipment used for screening.

**Traveler:** Excuse me, which gate is Flight 246 using?  
**Employee:** Gate C12. Boarding starts at 2:20.  
**Traveler:** What time does boarding close?  
**Employee:** For this flight, 2:45.  
**Traveler:** The screen now says delayed. Has the gate changed?  
**Employee:** It's still C12. Please check the updated departure information.  

A delay, a boarding time, and a gate-closing time are different details. Ask about each rather than treating departure time as the whole plan.

### Boarding and the cabin

**Crew member:** May I see your boarding pass?  
**Traveler:** Here it is. I'm in 18C. Which way is that?  
**Crew member:** Down the aisle, on your left.  
**Traveler:** Could you help me with this bag?  
**Crew member:** Sure. Let's find space in the overhead bin.  

Useful cabin words: **row**, a line of seats; **seat belt**, the strap securing you in a seat; **tray table**, the small folding table; **reading light**, the light above your seat; **life vest**, emergency flotation equipment. **Turbulence** describes irregular air movement that can make a flight bumpy.

These model crew instructions teach meaning: “Please fasten your seat belt.” “Return your seat to the upright position.” “Stow your tray table.” “Switch your device to airplane mode.” Follow the actual crew's instructions; these examples are not a complete flight-safety procedure.

**Traveler:** Could I have some water, please?  
**Crew member:** Of course.  
**Traveler:** I'm feeling unwell. Could you help me?  
**Crew member:** I'll come over.  

### Arrival: a separate international visit

**Officer:** What's the purpose of your visit?  
**Visitor:** I'm visiting friends for ten days.  
**Officer:** Where are you staying?  
**Visitor:** At their home in Portland. I have the address here.  

Use information that describes your actual trip. If necessary, say “Could you repeat the question?” Immigration and customs requirements depend on your journey; this exchange is language practice, not entry-policy advice.

**Traveler:** Which carousel is our flight's baggage on?  
**Employee:** Carousel 5.  
**Traveler:** My suitcase hasn't arrived. Where can I report it?  
**Employee:** At the airline's baggage service desk.  

An **airport cart** carries luggage. At the rental counter: “I have a reservation under Maya Lee.” Staff may ask for documents. Ask “What documents do you need?” and “Where do I return the car?” For money exchange, ask “What's the exchange rate, and are there any fees?” For a pickup, ask “Where's the designated pickup area?”

## Practice

Fictional travel card: Flight 246 to Denver; gate C12; boarding 2:20; closes 2:45; one suitcase; aisle seat requested; arrival carousel 5. Separate international visitor role: arriving in Portland from abroad, for ten days with friends.

1. Make the check-in request. Partner says the aisle seat is unavailable. Accept the window seat or ask about another option.
2. Ask about the gate and boarding times. Partner uses the card. Repeat the three details in your own words.
3. Report missing baggage. Partner asks “Which flight were you on?” Supply the number and ask where to make the report.
4. Answer the arrival questions using the visitor role. Change the trip purpose to a vacation if desired, without inventing a visa rule.

Alone, read each staff question and answer out loud; then cover the card and reconstruct the journey.

**Possibilities:** “The window seat is fine.” “Flight 246. My suitcase isn't on Carousel 5.” “I'm visiting friends for ten days.” Check whether the other person can identify your flight, need, and next question. Policies and times in this lesson are fictional.
