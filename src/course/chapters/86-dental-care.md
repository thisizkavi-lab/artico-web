# CH86 — Dental Care

**Outcome:** Describe a dental concern, understand examination language, and ask about a proposed treatment.  

## Study

**Toothache** means tooth pain. A **cavity** is an area of decay in a tooth. A **filling** repairs a tooth with a material; a **crown** covers a tooth. **Braces** are devices used in orthodontic treatment. **Plaque** is a film that collects on teeth. A **hygienist** provides dental hygiene care. You can use these terms to ask about care without deciding the treatment yourself.

### Describe the problem

**Patient:** I have pain in a tooth on the lower left.  
**Dentist:** When did it start?  
**Patient:** Yesterday. It hurts when I chew.  
**Dentist:** Have you noticed anything else?  
**Patient:** A filling seems to have come out. I'm not sure.  
**Dentist:** Let's examine it.  
**Patient:** Could you tell me what you're looking for?  

Other reports: “My crown feels loose.” “My gums bleed when I brush.” “I've noticed a sensitive area.” **Gums** are the tissue around the teeth; **sensitive** can describe an area that reacts painfully to a stimulus. “I think…” signals uncertainty, not a diagnosis.

A child-related report could be “My child's first teeth are coming in.” A cosmetic request could be “I'd like to discuss whitening.” **Whitening** changes tooth color; asking about it is not a treatment recommendation. “Can we talk about braces?” opens a separate discussion.

### During the examination

**Dentist:** Please open a little wider.  
**Patient:** Like this?  
**Dentist:** Yes. Let me know if it hurts.  
**Patient:** Can we agree on a signal if I need you to stop?  
**Dentist:** Yes. Let's agree on that before we begin.  

**Rinse** means wash an area with liquid; **brush** and **floss** name different cleaning actions. Ask your dentist about the technique: “Could you show me the technique you're recommending?” This language lesson does not prescribe a routine or a technique.

If you cannot easily speak during treatment, an agreed signal can help communicate. The example is a request for an arrangement, not a claim that a particular gesture is always recognized.

### Ask about options

**Dentist:** I'd like to discuss a possible filling.  
**Patient:** What did you find, and what are my options?  
**Dentist:** I'll explain the findings and alternatives.  
**Patient:** What would the treatment involve? Could you explain the cost too?  
**Dentist:** We'll go through the plan before you decide.  

**Extraction / removing a tooth** names a procedure. Hearing the term does not mean you must infer it is necessary. “Do I need that treatment now?” asks about timing. “I'd like to understand the alternatives first” expresses a limit. “I'm nervous about pain” states a real concern; you do not have to hide it to cooperate.

## Practice

Fictional patient: lower-left tooth pain since yesterday; worse when chewing; possible lost filling, not confirmed. Dentist proposes an examination, then discussion of treatment options; no treatment selected.

1. Give your report. Partner asks when and what makes it hurt. Answer with the case.
2. Partner asks you to open wider. Confirm what they mean and request a stopping signal.
3. Partner mentions a possible filling. Ask about findings, alternatives, and cost before deciding.
4. Change the role to a loose crown or a whitening inquiry. The type of concern and intended outcome should change.

Solo: read the dentist lines and create your replies, then describe the concern with the model hidden.

**Possibilities:** “It hurts when I chew.” “Could we agree on a signal to pause?” “Could you explain the alternatives before I decide?” Check that the location, observation, uncertainty, and requested explanation are clear. Appointment changes belong to Chapter 82; this lesson focuses on the care conversation.
