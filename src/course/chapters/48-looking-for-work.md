# CH48 — Looking for Work

**Outcome:** Ask about an opening, explain the work you want, and answer initial questions about experience and availability.  

## Study

A **job opening** or **vacancy** is a position available to fill. A **job posting** advertises a position. A **recruiter** works on finding or assessing candidates; an **employment agency** helps connect job seekers and work opportunities. **Experience** is work or activity you have done; **availability** is when you can work.

### Read a short posting

This fictional posting provides the facts for practice:

> **Maple Books: part-time store assistant**  
> 16 hours a week, including Saturday afternoons. Help customers and organize shelves. Customer-service experience preferred. Ask the store manager for application instructions.

**Preferred** means wanted or favored in this posting; **required** would state a condition the employer says applicants must meet. Clarify any important uncertainty rather than guessing.

**Applicant:** Hi. Is the store-assistant position still open?  
**Manager:** Yes. Are you interested in part-time work?  
**Applicant:** I am. What hours would the job involve?  
**Manager:** Sixteen hours a week, including Saturday afternoons.  
**Applicant:** I can work Saturdays. How do I apply?  
**Manager:** I'll give you the application instructions.  

**Still open?** checks whether the advertised position remains available. **How do I apply?** asks for the next process step; it does not commit you to accepting an eventual offer.

### Describe what you are seeking

**I'm looking for a part-time customer-service job** gives a role and work arrangement. You can add **I'd prefer evening shifts**, **I'm available on weekends**, or **I'm interested in an entry-level role**. **Entry-level** describes a beginning position in a field; the actual posting determines its requirements.

**Recruiter:** What kind of work are you looking for?  
**Job seeker:** Part-time retail work. I worked in a café for a year.  
**Recruiter:** What experience do you have with customers?  
**Job seeker:** I took orders, answered questions and handled busy periods.  
**Recruiter:** When are you available?  
**Job seeker:** Afternoons and weekends. I have classes in the mornings.  

**Retail** concerns selling goods to customers. **Handle busy periods** explains coping with a rush, but a concrete example is stronger than that phrase alone. You can describe a transferable skill even if the setting changes: **I haven't worked in a bookstore, but I have experience helping customers**.

### Ask and answer honestly

Possible questions include **What skills do you have?**, **What pay are you looking for?**, **When could you start?**, and **Why did you leave your last job?** Supplied answers include **I could start next month**, **I'd like to understand the pay range first**, and **My previous job was temporary**.

If you need more information: **Could you tell me more about the responsibilities?** If you cannot meet the proposed hours: **I can't work mornings. Are there other shifts?** If the opening is filled: **Thanks for letting me know. Do you expect other openings?**

## Practice

Alone, read recruiter questions and answer from the role facts. Then rehearse both parts.

1. Ask about Maple Books' opening and schedule. Your role can work Saturdays but wants confirmation of the other hours.
2. Use this role: one year of previous café work; customer orders and questions; afternoons and weekends; can start next month. Answer the recruiter from the model.
3. The recruiter offers mornings only. Explain your limitation and ask about another shift. Alternatively, the position is filled; ask about future openings.

**Sample possibilities:** “Is the position still open? What other hours would I work?” / “I worked in a café for a year. I helped customers and took orders. I'm available afternoons and weekends.” / “I can't work mornings. Are there afternoon shifts?” / “Do you expect any other openings?”  

Later, change the role and availability. Check that you separated the work you want, the experience you actually have, and the employer's stated requirements.
