# CH57 — Finding a New Home

**Outcome:** Explain housing needs, ask about a property or rental, and clarify an offer or ongoing housing concern.  

## Study

Begin with priorities: **We're looking for a two-bedroom apartment**, **We'd prefer somewhere near public transportation**, or **We need a place that allows a cat**. **Need** presents a requirement; **prefer** describes a choice you may be willing to reconsider. **At least two bedrooms** means two or more.

Useful terms are **listing**, information advertising a property; **viewing** or **tour**, an opportunity to look at it; **asking price**, the advertised sale price; **rent**, payment for using the home; and **lease**, an agreement setting rental terms. A **landlord** owns or provides the rental; a **property manager** manages it; a **tenant** rents it.

### Compare two rentals

These fictional listings supply all practice facts:

| Home | Stated conditions |
| --- | --- |
| Pine Apartment | one bedroom; $1,200 monthly rent; water included; electricity not included; listed as unfurnished; pets require discussion |
| Cedar Apartment | two bedrooms; $1,600 monthly rent; water included; electricity not included; unfurnished; one-year lease listed; small balcony |

**Prospective tenant:** We're looking for two bedrooms. Could we tour Cedar Apartment?  
**Agent:** Yes. What would you like to know first?  
**Tenant:** Is the apartment furnished?  
**Agent:** It's unfurnished. You would bring your own furniture.  
**Tenant:** Are any utilities included in the rent?  
**Agent:** Water is included; electricity isn't.  
**Tenant:** We have a cat. Could you explain the pet conditions before we decide?  

**Furnished** means supplied with furniture; **unfurnished** means furniture is not supplied in the arrangement being described. **Utilities** refers to services such as water, electricity and gas. Ask which services a quoted payment includes rather than assuming.

### During a tour

**Agent:** What do you think of the apartment?  
**Visitor:** I like the layout, but the bedroom seems small for us.  
**Agent:** What size are you looking for?  
**Visitor:** Enough room for a desk as well as a bed. Could we check the measurements?  
**Agent:** Sure.  
**Visitor:** I'm also concerned about the traffic noise. Could we come back at another time to compare?  

A **layout** is the arrangement of rooms and spaces. Useful feedback includes **The location works for us**, **The rent is above our budget**, **It needs more work than we expected**, and **We'd like to think about it**. You can give a clear negative view without finding a positive comment first.

### Ask about rental terms

**How long is the lease?**, **What deposit is required?**, **What fees are listed?**, **When could we move in?**, and **What documents does the application require?** help identify missing information. A **security deposit** is money held under an agreement as security against specified costs, such as damage; ask about the actual terms and return conditions.

**Tenant:** Could you send us the proposed lease and all listed charges?  
**Agent:** Yes.  
**Tenant:** We'd like to understand the deposit and renewal conditions before deciding.  
**Agent:** Let me know which parts need explanation.  

### A buying conversation

In a separate fictional sale listing, the asking price is $275,000. The visitor is considering offering $270,000.

**Buyer:** We like the house, but we'd like to discuss an offer below the asking price.  
**Agent:** What amount are you considering?  
**Buyer:** Two hundred seventy thousand. What steps and conditions would be involved in making that offer?  
**Agent:** I'll explain the process before you decide how to proceed.  

**Make an offer** proposes an amount or terms. **We've made an offer** does not itself say it was accepted. **Has the seller responded?** checks the result.

### During a rental

**Tenant:** The washing machine is leaking. How should I report a maintenance issue?  
**Manager:** I'll give you the contact information and reporting instructions.  
**Tenant:** Thank you. Could you confirm that you've received my report?  

For a change to the property: **I'd like to put up shelves. What permission or conditions would apply?** The request leaves the answer to the relevant agreement and situation.

## Practice

Alone, read the agent's questions and answer from the listings, then switch roles.

1. Explain a need for two bedrooms and a preference for a balcony. Choose the matching listing and ask one unresolved question.
2. Ask about Cedar's rent, included utilities, deposit and proposed lease. Only answer facts supplied in the listing; request the others.
3. During a tour, the room is too small for a bed and desk. Give feedback and ask to check measurements.
4. For the sale listing, discuss a $270,000 offer without claiming acceptance. Ask about steps and conditions.
5. Report a leaking washing machine and ask how to follow up.

**Sample possibilities:** “We need two bedrooms and would prefer a balcony. Cedar seems closer to our needs. Are pets allowed?” / “What deposit is required?” / “This room seems too small. Could we check the measurements?” / “We're considering offering two hundred seventy thousand. What would be involved?” / “Could you confirm receipt of my maintenance report?”  

Repeat later with different priorities. Check whether you distinguished a preference, a listed condition, missing information and an actual agreement.
