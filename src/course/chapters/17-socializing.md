# CH17 — Socializing

**Your goal:** Join a social conversation, discover a connection, follow up on an answer, and leave or change the topic when you want.  

## Study: Enter a conversation

Use a brief Chapter 1 greeting or Chapter 2 introduction when needed. Here the next step is making contact beyond the greeting.

- **Mind if I join you?** Ask to join a person or group.
- **How do you know Mara?** Ask about a shared host or contact.
- **Have you been here before?** Ask about experience with the place.
- **What have you been up to?** Ask about recent activities when reconnecting.

**At a friend's gathering**

**Robin:** Hi. Mind if I join you?  
**Kai:** Sure. I'm Kai.  
**Robin:** I'm Robin. How do you know Mara?  
**Kai:** We work together. What about you?  
**Robin:** We took a photography class together.  
**Kai:** Oh, nice. Do you still take pictures?  

The follow-up uses something Robin just said. It gives Robin a clear way to continue instead of introducing an unrelated question immediately.

## Study: Work, study, and shared experience

At a social event, **What do you do?** can ask about someone's work. If you want to be clearer, use **What kind of work do you do?** A person can also explain study, retirement or another current situation.

- **I work at a library.** Identify your workplace.
- **I'm studying design.** Describe what you study.
- **I'm between jobs right now.** Say you are not currently in a job.
- **I'm retired.** Describe retirement.
- **We used to work together.** Describe a past connection.

**A shared college**

**Kai:** I went to Westbrook College.  
**Robin:** Really? So did I. When were you there?  
**Kai:** From 2018 to 2022.  
**Robin:** I started in 2020. We were there at the same time.  

Westbrook is invented for this scene. **So did I** means Robin also attended. The dates make the overlap clear. If you missed the name, **Could you say the name again?** supplies Chapter 4 repair.

## Study: Reconnect or make a comment

**Robin:** I didn't know you were coming! What have you been up to?  
**Kai:** I've been taking a cooking class. How about you?  
**Robin:** Mostly work, but I started hiking again.  
**Kai:** That sounds fun.  

The question is about recent activity, so an answer can mention a class, work, a trip or little change: **Not much, mostly work**. You do not need a dramatic story.

A specific compliment can also open a topic:

**Kai:** I like your jacket.  
**Robin:** Thanks. I found it at a thrift store.  
**Kai:** Nice find!  

A **thrift store** sells secondhand goods. Robin could give only **Thanks** if they did not want to explain where the jacket came from. A compliment does not require a price or shopping story.

## Study: Keep the conversation comfortable for your role

**I'd rather not talk about work tonight** can change a topic. **What do you do for fun?** asks about interests. **I'm going to get a drink. Good talking with you** can end the exchange; Chapter 10 gives other closing support.

If someone gives a short or private answer, use it rather than demand details:

**Robin:** How's work going?  
**Kai:** I'd rather not talk about it tonight.  
**Robin:** Sure. Have you done any cooking lately?  

## Practice: Follow one connection

Use these fictional guest cards. Speak both roles when practicing alone.

| Guest | Connection and available details |
| --- | --- |
| Avery | Works with Mara; works at a library; recently started hiking |
| Devon | Took a cooking class with Mara; studies design; likes making bread |
| Robin | Took a photography class with Mara; has been busy with work |

1. You are Robin. Join Avery, introduce yourself briefly, and ask how Avery knows Mara. Follow up on work or hiking.
2. You are Devon. Answer **What do you do?** using the study information. Supply the other person's acknowledgment: **Oh, nice. What kind of design?** For this scene, answer **Graphic design**.
3. Reconnect with Avery and ask about recent activities. Avery mentions hiking. Ask **Where do you like to hike?** Avery's supplied answer is **At the nearby park**.
4. Change the response: Avery does not want to discuss work. Acknowledge it and ask about hiking instead.
5. Hide the cards. Build a five-turn conversation from one shared connection. Close it when your role wants to leave.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Mind if I join you? — Sure. — I'm Robin. How do you know Mara? — We work together. — What kind of work do you do?**
2. **I'm studying design. — What kind? — Graphic design.**
3. **What have you been up to? — I started hiking. — Where do you like to hike? — At the nearby park.**
4. **Sure. How's the hiking going?**

**Check:** Did the follow-up connect to an answer? Did each person have room to contribute? Could you respect a topic change and close without making an unwanted promise?
