# CH05 — Opinions and Preferences

**Your goal:** Say what you like, give an opinion, choose between options, or explain that either choice works for you.

## Study: Say what you like and dislike

Use these phrases to show how strongly you feel. A preference describes your taste; it does not make the same choice right for everyone.

| Phrase | Example in use | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I like it.** | **I like it** because it's simple. | Say you have a positive opinion. |
| **I love it.** | **I love it** because the story is funny. | Show a stronger positive feeling. |
| **I'm into photography.** | **I'm into photography**, so I take pictures every weekend. | Say you are interested in an activity or subject. |
| **I'm not really into it.** | **I'm not really into it**, but I'll give it a try. | Say you have little interest in something. |
| **It's not my thing.** | **It's not my thing**, but I know other people enjoy it. | Say something does not suit your taste. |
| **I can't stand it.** | **I can't stand it** when people talk during movies. | Show a very strong dislike. |

**Dev:** Do you like escape rooms?  
**Riley:** Not really. **It's not my thing.**  
**Dev:** What are you into?  
**Riley:** **I'm into photography.** I'd rather go for a walk and take pictures.

**Note:** **Not really** is a mild way to say no; **I can't stand it** is much stronger. **I'm good** depends on context: after **How are you?**, it means you're well; after an offer, **No, I'm good, thanks** politely declines it.

## Study: Ask for and give an opinion

Ask what someone thinks, then give your view. A short reason helps the other person understand your opinion.

| Phrase | Example in use | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What do you think of the room?** | **What do you think of the room?** | Ask someone to evaluate something. |
| **How do you feel about the plan?** | **How do you feel about the plan?** | Ask for someone's view or reaction. |
| **I think the smaller room would work better.** | **I think the smaller room would work better** because it is quieter. | Give your opinion. |
| **In my opinion, we need more time.** | **In my opinion, we need more time.** | Clearly present something as your opinion. |
| **As I see it, the main problem is the cost.** | **As I see it, the main problem is the cost.** | Explain how you understand a situation. |
| **I like the idea, but I'm worried about the price.** | **I like the idea, but I'm worried about the price.** | Give a positive and a negative point together. |

**Dev:** What do you think of the room?  
**Riley:** **I like the location, but I think it's too small.**  
**Dev:** What's your view, Morgan?  
**Morgan:** **As I see it, we need a bigger space.** We expect twenty people.

**Note:** **I think** is natural in everyday conversation. You do not need a formal opening before every opinion. Give a reason when it helps explain your view.

## Study: Choose between options

Use **prefer** to compare choices. Use **would rather** to say which action you want to take.

| Phrase | Example in use | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I prefer tea to coffee.** | **I prefer tea to coffee** in the morning. | Say which of two things you like more. |
| **I'd prefer the earlier train.** | **I'd prefer the earlier train** because it gets me there on time. | Choose one option for this situation. |
| **I'd rather leave at six.** | **I'd rather leave at six** so we can avoid traffic. | Say which action you want to take. Use the base verb after rather. |
| **I'd rather not go tonight.** | **I'd rather not go tonight** because I'm tired. | Say you prefer not to do something. |
| **Either is fine.** | Tea or coffee? **Either is fine.** | Say that both choices are acceptable. |
| **Both work for me.** | Tuesday or Wednesday? **Both work for me.** | Say that either time suits you. |
| **I don't have a preference.** | Which seat would you like? **I don't have a preference.** | Say you do not have a particular choice. |
| **I don't mind.** | Inside or outside? **I don't mind.** | Say either option is acceptable to you; this often sounds relaxed. |
| **I don't care.** | Where should we sit? **I don't care.** | Say the choice does not matter to you. Depending on your tone, this can sound dismissive. |

**Dev:** Would you rather eat here or take the food home?  
**Riley:** **I'd rather take it home.** It's quieter there.  
**Dev:** That works for me.  
**Riley:** Great. Let's do that.

**Note:** Say **I'd rather leave**, not **I'd rather to leave**. **I don't care** can sound abrupt. If you simply want to accept either option, **Either is fine** or **I don't mind** is usually warmer.

## Optional: Strong reactions

**No way!** can show surprise, or it can strongly reject an idea. The words that follow make your meaning clear.

| Phrase | Example in use | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No way!** | You won? **No way!** That's amazing! | Show surprise or disbelief. |
| **No way! I'm not paying that much.** | **No way! I'm not paying that much.** | Strongly reject a suggestion or price. |

**Dev:** You won the photography contest!  
**Riley:** **No way!** That's amazing!  
**Dev:** Yes, your photo got first place.  
**Riley:** I can't believe it!

## Practice: Choose and explain

1. Ask someone what they think of a café. Give one positive point and one concern.
2. Choose between two options. Say what you prefer and give a reason.
3. You are tired and do not want to go to a loud concert. Give a clear but polite answer.
4. React to surprising news with **No way!** Then use it to reject an offer.

### Sample possibilities

- **I like the location, but I think it's too crowded.**
- **I'd prefer the earlier train** because I want to get home before dark.
- **I'd rather stay home. I'm tired, and loud concerts aren't my thing.**
- **You got the job? No way! That's amazing!**
