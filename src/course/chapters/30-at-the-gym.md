# CH30 — At the Gym

**Your goal:** Ask about membership and classes, decide whether to join, and request help during a first visit.  

## Study

### Choose the activity you mean

| Word or phrase | Meaning in this setting |
| --- | --- |
| **work out / a workout** | Exercise / a session of exercise. |
| **weight training** | Exercise using weights as resistance. |
| **personal trainer** | Someone who guides a person's exercise training. |
| **fitness class / instructor** | A group exercise session / the person leading it. |
| **spin class / indoor cycling** | A class using stationary exercise bikes. |
| **yoga** | A practice involving postures, movement and breathing. |
| **Pilates** | A method using controlled movements, on a mat or with special equipment. |
| **HIIT** | High-intensity interval training: periods of hard exercise alternating with recovery periods. |
| **dance class** | A class learning or practicing dance movements. |

Class names help you ask about the activity. **What does this class involve?** and **Is there a class for beginners?** let the instructor explain the particular session rather than leaving you to guess from its name.

### Ask about the membership

This fictional gym supplies the facts for the chapter:

| Choice | Practice conditions |
| --- | --- |
| Day pass | $10; gym access for that day; classes cost extra. |
| Monthly membership | $40 each month; no joining fee; gym access and cycling classes included; renews automatically. |
| Yoga class | $6 per class, including for monthly members. |
| Personal training | Available by separate appointment; ask staff for the price. |

For this monthly plan, staff require a cancellation request at least seven days before the next renewal. This is a scene condition, not a rule for all gyms. **Renewal** continues a membership; **automatic** renewal happens under the arrangement without a new purchase request each month.

**Visitor:** Hi. I'm interested in joining. Could you explain the membership options?  
**Staff:** A day pass is ten dollars. Monthly membership is forty, with no joining fee.  
**Visitor:** Are classes included?  
**Staff:** Cycling is included in the monthly plan. Yoga is six dollars per class.  
**Visitor:** Does the monthly plan renew automatically?  
**Staff:** Yes. To cancel before a renewal, we need your request at least seven days beforehand.  
**Visitor:** Could I have those terms in writing before I decide?  
**Staff:** Of course.  

**Included** means part of the stated price. **Extra** means additional. **Is there a joining fee?**, **How do I cancel?**, and **Can I pause the membership?** ask different questions; one answer does not settle all three.

Other useful inquiries are **Could I have a tour?**, **Where are the locker rooms?**, **Can I book a personal trainer?**, and **Do you offer women-only sessions?** The staff can explain availability and conditions. The fictional card does not answer the last question, so leave its answer open.

### Decide without being pushed into agreement

**Staff:** Would you like to sign up today?  
**Visitor:** I'd like to try a day pass first.  
**Staff:** Sure. Would you like a class as well?  
**Visitor:** Not today. Just the day pass, please.  

**Sign up** means register or join. **I'd like to join** makes a positive decision. **I'm just looking today** or **I don't want to sign up today** states a different intention. You can ask for time to compare options: **I'll think about it and get back to you.** That does not confirm a membership.

### Attend a first class

**Participant:** Hi. I'm here for the cycling class. It's my first time.  
**Instructor:** Welcome. We'll use bike five for you today.  
**Participant:** Could you show me how to adjust it?  
**Instructor:** Yes, I'll show you before we start.  
**Participant:** And how should I let you know if I need a break?  
**Instructor:** Just tell me.  

**It's my first time** identifies the help you may need. **Could you repeat that more slowly?** repairs an instruction. **I need a break** states a need; **I'd like to watch first** requests a different way to begin. These are communication choices, not exercise instructions.

With another participant: **Have you taken this class before? — A few times.** Their experience can start a conversation without proving that the class is suitable for you.

## Practice

Use the gym card. Alone, say the staff or instructor lines and then your own replies; with a partner, exchange roles.

1. Ask about the monthly price, a joining fee, cycling and yoga. As staff, answer only from the card. Confirm which class costs extra.
2. Ask how to cancel before a renewal. Staff give the seven-day condition. Repeat the condition and request it in writing.
3. Staff ask whether you want to join. Choose a monthly membership, a day pass, or no purchase. Say your choice clearly.
4. Arrive for your first cycling class and ask about adjusting the bike. Then change the cue: you did not understand an instruction. Ask for repetition.
5. You want a personal-training appointment, but the price is unknown. Ask about cost and availability without inventing an answer.

**Possible responses:** “So cycling is included, but yoga is six dollars extra?” / “You need the cancellation request at least seven days before renewal. Could I have that in writing?” / “Just a day pass today, please.” / “Could you repeat the instruction more slowly?” / “What does a personal-training session cost, and when could I book one?”  

Cover the models later and change the class or decision. **Check:** Did you distinguish the membership, an extra charge, an unconfirmed inquiry and your actual choice? Could you ask for help instead of pretending to understand?
