# CH02 — Making Introductions

**Your goal:** Give your name, connect your name to a useful detail, and introduce another person.  

## Study: Say who you are

After a greeting, **I'm** or **My name is** identifies you. A full name, first name, job or connection can help the other person understand who you are. Choose the detail the encounter needs.

**At a friend's gathering**

**Maya:** Hi, I'm Maya. I'm a friend of Jordan's.  
**Eli:** I'm Eli. Nice to meet you.  
**Maya:** You too.  

**A friend of Jordan's** means Jordan is your friend. **Nice to meet you** acknowledges the introduction; **You too** returns that feeling. You can also say the full **Nice to meet you too**.

Useful personal details:

- **I work with Jordan.** You are colleagues.
- **I'm on the design team.** You identify your team.
- **I'm a teacher.** You identify your job.
- **I'm here for the workshop.** You explain why you are there.

You do not have to give your whole background. **I don't think we've met. I'm Maya** can open an introduction when you are unsure whether you have met before.

## Study: A professional introduction

**At the start of a project meeting**

**Priya:** Hello, I'm Priya Shah. I'm the project manager.  
**Daniel:** Daniel Reed. It's a pleasure to meet you.  
**Priya:** Likewise. Please call me Priya.  
**Daniel:** Thanks, Priya. I'm on the research team.  

**It's a pleasure to meet you** offers a more ceremonious acknowledgment. **Likewise** means you share the feeling. **Please call me Priya** tells the other person how you want to be addressed. **I go by Dan** similarly explains the name you use.

Professional introductions can also use **Hi** and **Nice to meet you**. The location does not prescribe one level of formality. If someone gives a preferred name or title, you can use that information rather than guessing their preference.

You may encounter **How do you do?** as a formal introductory greeting. It can be returned with the same greeting; it is not asking you to describe how you do your work. It is an optional expression here, not a required first-meeting formula.

## Study: Connect two people

**Maya:** Eli, this is my colleague, Sam. Sam, this is Eli.  
**Eli:** Nice to meet you, Sam.  
**Sam:** You too. How do you know Maya?  
**Eli:** We take a photography class together.  

**This is...** works for a friend, partner, relative or colleague. **I'd like you to meet...** gives another introduction frame. **Have you met Sam?** checks first:

**Maya:** Have you met Sam?  
**Eli:** Yes, we met at the last workshop. Good to see you again, Sam.  

An introduction should give both people a way to continue. A shared activity is one possibility; an immediate personal question is not required.

If you have spoken online but are meeting physically for the first time, try **It's nice to finally meet in person**. Meeting and seeing formulas have flexible uses; do not turn them into a rigid test of whether contact happened before.

**Name support:** **Could you say your name again?** asks for repetition. **How do you spell it?** asks for the letters. Chapter 4 develops repair; these two questions are available here when needed.  

## Practice: Build the connection

Use these fictional cards first. Then substitute real details if you want. On your own, speak both sides and change roles on the second attempt.

| Person | Relevant detail |
| --- | --- |
| Taylor Brooks | A designer; on the design team; goes by Taylor |
| Morgan Lee | A researcher; on the research team; goes by Morgan |
| Avery Chen | Taylor's friend; takes a photography class with Taylor |

1. You are Avery at a gathering. Introduce yourself to Morgan with your connection to Taylor. Supply Morgan's acknowledgment and your reply.
2. You are Taylor at a project meeting. Give your name and team. Morgan asks what to call you. Answer with your preferred name.
3. Introduce Morgan and Avery to each other. Avery asks how Morgan knows Taylor; Morgan says they work together.
4. Change the response: Morgan says **We've met before, at the workshop**. Acknowledge that instead of pretending the introduction is new.
5. Hide the cards. Make a three-turn introduction using your own name or an invented identity. Ask for the name again if needed.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Hi, I'm Avery. I'm a friend of Taylor's. — I'm Morgan. Nice to meet you. — You too.**
2. **I'm Taylor Brooks. I'm on the design team. — What should I call you? — Please call me Taylor.**
3. **Morgan, this is Avery. Avery, this is my colleague Morgan. — Nice to meet you. How do you know Taylor? — We work together.**
4. **Oh, yes. Good to see you again.**

**Check:** Did the other person learn who you are and why you are connected? Did both people receive an introduction? Did you respond to what they actually said? Several wordings can succeed.
