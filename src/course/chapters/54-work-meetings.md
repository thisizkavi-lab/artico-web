# CH54 — Work Meetings

**Outcome:** Contribute to a meeting, manage a turn, clarify a proposal and confirm the agreed next action.  

## Study

An **agenda** lists meeting topics. An **action item** is a task resulting from the discussion. A **deadline** states when it is due. The **chair** or **facilitator** helps manage the meeting. You can participate without agreeing with every proposal.

### Begin with the purpose

This fictional team's agenda has three topics: customer questions, a proposed weekly staff update, and responsibility for the trial.

**Facilitator:** Thanks for coming. We have three topics today. Let's start with customer questions.  
**Coworker:** What decision do we need to make?  
**Facilitator:** Whether a weekly update would help staff answer common questions.  
**Coworker:** Okay. Can we look at what information is missing first?  

**What decision do we need to make?** identifies the meeting's practical goal. **Could we clarify the agenda?** asks about the scope. To begin a busy discussion directly: **Let's get started. We need to decide this today.**

### Take and share turns

Useful entry phrases are **Could I add something?**, **I'd like to respond to that**, and **Can I jump in for a moment?** To invite another person: **Lee, what do you think?** To protect an unfinished turn: **Let me finish this point, then I'd like to hear your view**.

**Maya:** A weekly update could reduce repeated questions.  
**Lee:** Could I add something? I like the idea, but we already have several updates.  
**Maya:** That's fair. Which ones overlap with this proposal?  
**Lee:** The daily message includes some of the same information.  
**Facilitator:** Let's compare them before adding another update.  

**I like the idea, but...** gives a mixed view. **I'm on board with that** expresses support. **I'm not convinced it solves the problem** expresses doubt. **That won't work with our current staffing** is a firmer objection. Give the reason when possible so others know what needs to change.

### Clarify, propose and respond

**Coworker:** Let's send it every week.  
**Maya:** Just to clarify, who would prepare it?  
**Coworker:** The shift supervisor.  
**Maya:** How about trying it with one team first?  
**Lee:** That would give us something to review before expanding it.  
**Facilitator:** Is anyone opposed to a one-team trial?  
**Maya:** I support the trial, provided we set a review date.  

**Provided we...** introduces a condition for agreement. **What do you mean by...?** checks meaning; **Are you suggesting...?** checks interpretation. A proposal, support and final decision are different stages. Do not report **We've agreed** when discussion has not produced agreement.

### End with a usable summary

**Facilitator:** To recap, we'll try the update with one team for two weeks. Lee will prepare the first version by Friday. Maya will collect feedback. We'll review it at the next meeting.  
**Lee:** Just to check, I'm preparing it, and Maya is collecting feedback?  
**Facilitator:** Correct.  
**Maya:** Could you send that summary to everyone?  
**Facilitator:** Yes. Thanks for your input.  

**To recap** introduces a summary. Confirm **what**, **who**, and **when**. If the summary differs from your understanding: **I understood the trial would last one week. Could we confirm the duration?**

### Continue a professional conversation

After a meeting, a short networking exchange can connect people without promising a collaboration:

**A:** Your project sounds interesting. I'd like to hear more about it.  
**B:** Sure. Would you like to exchange contact details?  
**A:** Yes. Could we continue the conversation next week?  
**B:** Let's check our schedules.  

## Practice

Alone, read the other person's turn, answer, then speak both roles. Use the agenda and model facts.

1. Ask which decision the meeting needs. Propose a one-team trial and give a reason.
2. Your partner says **We should expand it to everyone immediately.** Express doubt and suggest reviewing a trial first.
3. Someone interrupts before you finish. Ask to complete your point while leaving room for their response.
4. Summarize the two-week trial: Lee prepares the first update by Friday; Maya collects feedback. Confirm responsibility if the listener reverses the names.
5. Invite a colleague to continue a project conversation without claiming an appointment has already been made.

**Sample possibilities:** “What decision do we need today? Could we try this with one team first?” / “I'm not convinced we should expand it yet. Let's review a trial.” / “Let me finish this point, then I'd like to hear your view.” / “Lee prepares it by Friday; Maya collects feedback.”  

Later, change one proposal or responsibility. Check that you could disagree clearly, seek clarification and distinguish tentative ideas from agreed actions.
