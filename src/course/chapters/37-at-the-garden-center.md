# CH37 — At the Garden Center

**Outcome:** Explain the kind of plant you want and ask about its needs before choosing.  

## Study

You do not need to know a plant's name to begin: **I'm looking for a houseplant** or **I'd like to grow some herbs** explains your goal. A **houseplant** is grown indoors; **herbs** are plants such as basil or mint used for flavor or other purposes. **Seeds** start plants; a **bulb** is a plant part from which certain plants grow.

Describe the setting, not just the desired appearance: **My room doesn't get much sunlight**, **It's for a sunny balcony**, **I don't have much space**, or **I have a cat**. These details help you ask a relevant question without assuming that every attractive plant suits your situation.

### Choosing a plant

In this practice scene, the customer is comparing two plants. Use these fictional labels as scene facts:

| Label | Stated information |
| --- | --- |
| Plant A | indoor plant; mature height 12 inches; low indirect light |
| Plant B | outdoor plant; mature height 36 inches; bright light |

**Indirect light** does not shine directly on the plant. Neither label supplies watering or pet-safety information.

**Customer:** I'm looking for a plant for my apartment.  
**Employee:** What kind of space will it be in?  
**Customer:** A small room. It doesn't get much sunlight.  
**Employee:** Let's compare the information on these labels.  
**Customer:** How big does this one get?  
**Employee:** Plant A's label says twelve inches tall. Plant B's says thirty-six inches.  
**Customer:** And how much light do they need?  
**Employee:** Plant A is labeled for low indirect light indoors. Plant B is labeled for bright light outdoors.  
**Customer:** Plant A sounds closer to my space. I'm also looking for something safe around my cat.  
**Employee:** We can check that information before you choose.  

**Mature size** means the size a plant can reach as it develops. **I'm looking for something...** describes a requirement: **small**, **easy to care for**, or **suitable for indoors**. An employee may need to consult information; a quick answer is not always available.

### Asking about care

Use these questions to obtain information, not to guess a care routine:

- **How much light does it need?**
- **How often should I water it?**
- **How can I tell when it needs water?**
- **Can it stay outside in winter?**
- **What kind of soil does it need?**
- **Does it need fertilizer?**

**How often?** asks about frequency. **What kind?** asks about a type. If an answer is unclear, **Could you write that down?** or **Could you show me the care information?** gives you usable support.

### Supplies

A **pot** holds a plant. A **watering can** is used to pour water. A **trowel** is a small digging tool. **Pruning shears** cut plant stems. **Compost** is decomposed organic material used in gardening. **Potting mix** is material sold for growing plants in containers; ask about the product intended for your plant rather than treating all products as interchangeable.

**Customer:** Where are the small pots?  
**Employee:** On the shelf behind you.  
**Customer:** Thanks. Do you sell watering cans, too?  
**Employee:** Yes, beside the pots.  

## Practice

1. Use this role: small apartment; little sunlight; wants an indoor plant. Explain your need, then ask about size and light. Use the first model's employee turns if alone.
2. Compare the two labels. Which matches an indoor-space requirement, and what do its height and light entries say? What care question remains unanswered?
3. You do not understand an employee's answer about watering. Request written information rather than inventing a schedule.

**Sample possibilities:** “I'm looking for a small houseplant. My room doesn't get much sunlight. How big does this one get? How much light does it need?” / “Plant A matches the indoor requirement. Its label says twelve inches and low indirect light. I still need to ask about watering.” / “Could you write that down or show me the care information?”  

Rehearse the exchange again with a balcony or an herb-growing goal. Check that you explained the setting and asked for missing information.
