# CH22 — Takeout and Delivery

**Your goal:** Place or collect a food order, confirm delivery details, and report a delay or missing item.  

## Study: Choose pickup or delivery

**Takeout** is prepared food to eat away from the place selling it. **To go** describes that choice in an order. **Pickup** means you collect it; **delivery** means someone brings it to the address agreed for the order.

- **I'd like to place an order for pickup.** Begin an order you will collect.
- **Could I get two burgers to go?** Order food to take away.
- **Do you deliver to this address?** Check the delivery area.
- **How long will it take?** Ask about timing.
- **Is there a delivery fee?** Ask about a charge rather than assume one.

## Study: Place and collect an order

Use this fictional menu and order information throughout Practice:

| Item | Practice price |
| --- | --- |
| Vegetable rice bowl | $9 |
| Burger | $8 |
| Fries | $3 |

**Fictional scene conditions:** Pickup estimate: twenty minutes. Delivery estimate: forty minutes. Delivery fee: $3. These are this lesson's conditions, not a restaurant-wide rule.  

**Ordering by phone**

**Staff:** Is this for pickup or delivery?  
**Customer:** Pickup, please. One vegetable rice bowl and one order of fries.  
**Staff:** What name should I put on the order?  
**Customer:** Cameron.  
**Staff:** It should be ready in about twenty minutes.  
**Customer:** Okay. One rice bowl and fries for Cameron. Thank you.  

**Collecting it**

**Customer:** Hi. I'm here to pick up an order for Cameron.  
**Staff:** The rice bowl and fries?  
**Customer:** Yes, that's mine.  
**Staff:** Here you go.  

**About** identifies an estimate. You can ask **Is the order ready?** if you arrive and it is not clear. **I'm picking up an order** describes collection, not a new order.

## Study: Give delivery information

This delivery address is invented for language practice: **18 Maple Lane, apartment 3, Springfield, Illinois 62704**. A **ZIP code** is a U.S. postal code; here it is **62704**, said **six two seven zero four**. Use the actual address and its code when placing a real order.

**Staff:** What's the delivery address?  
**Customer:** Eighteen Maple Lane, apartment three, Springfield, Illinois. The ZIP code is six two seven zero four.  
**Staff:** Could you repeat the apartment number?  
**Customer:** Apartment three.  
**Staff:** Got it. The estimate is about forty minutes.  
**Customer:** Is the delivery fee included in the total?  
**Staff:** Yes, the total includes the three-dollar delivery fee.  

**Could you read the address back to me?** requests a check. If using a delivery app, the same language helps you describe the order to a person or write a message; this lesson does not depend on a particular app's buttons.

## Study: Explain an order problem

**Customer:** My order was expected at six thirty. It's seven now, and it hasn't arrived. Could you check the status?  
**Staff:** I'm sorry. Let me check. The new estimate is seven fifteen.  
**Customer:** Okay. Please let me know if that changes.  

A firmer response is **That delay doesn't work for me. Can I cancel the order?** You are asking about a possibility; the answer depends on the situation and seller's policy. **Can I get a refund for the missing item?** similarly requests a remedy without assuming the policy.

**Missing food**

**Customer:** I ordered a rice bowl and fries, but the fries are missing.  
**Staff:** Sorry about that. Would you like to collect them, or should we discuss another option?  
**Customer:** I'd like a refund for the fries, if possible.  

Other foods you may choose include **pizza**, **noodles**, **tacos**, **curry**, **sushi**, and **fried chicken**. Pick the actual item name from the menu rather than need every food word in advance.

## Practice: Confirm and resolve

Speak both sides when practicing alone. Use the supplied menu, prices and scene conditions.

1. Order a burger and fries for pickup under the name Sam. Ask when it should be ready. Collect it with a sentence that identifies the order.
2. Order a rice bowl for delivery using the invented address. Ask whether the fee is included. The staff say it is; the fictional pre-tax total is $12 for the bowl and delivery.
3. Change the response: staff say they cannot deliver to that address. Choose pickup or decline. Support: **I'll pick it up instead**, **Then I'll pass, thanks**.
4. Your expected arrival time was 6:30 p.m.; it is now 7 p.m. Ask for a status update, then request cancellation if the delay does not suit your role.
5. The fries are missing. Describe what you ordered and what arrived. Ask about a refund; staff reply **Let me check what we can do**.
6. Hide the models. Create an order, identify it at pickup, and handle one changed item or estimate.

### Sample possibilities

1. **I'd like a burger and fries for pickup. The name is Sam. How long will it take? — About twenty minutes.** At collection: **I'm here to pick up an order for Sam.**
2. **One rice bowl for delivery, please. Is the delivery fee included? — Yes.**
3. **I'll pick it up instead.**
4. **It hasn't arrived. Could you check the status?** Then **Can I cancel the order?**
5. **The fries are missing. Can I get a refund for them?**

**Check:** Were the item, collection method and identifying details clear? Did you preserve an estimate as an estimate? Did the problem report name the actual mismatch?
