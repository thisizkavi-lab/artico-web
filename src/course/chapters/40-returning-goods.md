# CH40 — Returning Goods

**Outcome:** Explain why you are returning an item, ask about a refund or exchange, and respond when the first option is unavailable.  

## Study

A **return** brings a purchased item back. A **refund** returns money. An **exchange** replaces the item with another one. **Store credit** is an amount available to spend with that store under its conditions. A **receipt** or **order number** helps identify a purchase, but acceptable proof and remedies depend on the actual seller and applicable rules.

### Begin with the request and reason

**I'd like to return this jacket. It's too small** combines the request and explanation. Other reasons include **I bought the wrong size**, **It arrived damaged**, **The lamp doesn't turn on**, and **I received the wrong item**. Describe what happened; you do not need to diagnose an electrical fault to report it.

**Employee:** How can I help?  
**Customer:** I'd like to return this jacket. It doesn't fit.  
**Employee:** Do you have the receipt?  
**Customer:** Yes. I bought it last week.  
**Employee:** Would you prefer an exchange or a refund?  
**Customer:** An exchange, if you have a larger size.  
**Employee:** I'll check that for you.  

This is an original scene, not a statement about every store's policy. **Would you prefer...?** asks which option you want. **If you have...** makes the exchange depend on availability.

### Practice with a complete fictional policy

For the next scenes only, Elm Shop's policy says: unused clothing can be returned within 30 days with a receipt; refunds or exchanges are available; without a receipt, staff must review the purchase and cannot promise an outcome. The jacket was bought 10 days ago and has not been worn. This policy is a fictional exercise condition.

**Customer:** I'd like a refund for this jacket. Here's the receipt.  
**Employee:** Thank you. Was it worn?  
**Customer:** No. I tried it on at home, but it was too small.  
**Employee:** Under our policy, we can process the refund.  
**Customer:** Thanks. How will I receive it, and how long will it take?  
**Employee:** I'll explain the details before we finish.  

Asking **How will I receive it?** avoids assuming a payment method. **How long will it take?** asks about timing without inventing a universal deadline.

### When there is a problem

**Customer:** I bought this online, but I can't find the receipt. I have the order number.  
**Employee:** I can look up the order, but I need to check which options are available.  
**Customer:** Okay. I would prefer a refund. What information do you need?  
**Employee:** The order number and the item, please.  

If an offer does not meet your need, you can say **An exchange won't work for me. What other options are available?** A firmer response is **I'm frustrated because this arrived broken. I'd like you to review the return options.** It states the problem without inventing a right or accusing the staff member of causing it.

If the answer is **We can't decide that here**, ask **Who can review it?** or **Could you show me the policy you're referring to?** If you accept an alternative, **Store credit would work for me** makes that clear. You can also say **I need time to think about that**.

## Practice

Speak both roles alone. Use Elm Shop's fictional conditions, not a real store's rules.

1. Return the unused jacket bought 10 days ago. You have the receipt and want an exchange. Ask whether a large is available.
2. The large is unavailable. The employee offers a refund. Accept it and ask about timing.
3. An online lamp arrived broken. You have the order number but no receipt. Explain the issue and ask what information is needed. Do not assume the outcome.
4. The employee offers only an exchange while reviewing the lamp's purchase. Explain that you prefer a refund and request a review.

**Sample possibilities:** “I'd like to exchange this for a large. Here's my receipt.” / “A refund would be fine. How long will it take?” / “This lamp arrived broken. I have the order number. What information do you need?” / “An exchange won't work for me. Could someone review the refund options?”  

Later, change the item and your preferred outcome. Check that you separated the reason for return, the remedy you want, and the seller's actual response. Several wordings can complete the task.
