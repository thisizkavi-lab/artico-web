# CH72 — Vacation Vocabulary

**Outcome:** Select words for accommodation, packing, activities, and sightseeing, then describe a possible trip.  

## Study

Here **vacation** means time away for rest or travel. You may encounter *holiday* for this meaning in other English settings. Pick the vocabulary that fits your own plan rather than memorizing every activity.

### Places to stay and features to ask about

A **hotel** offers guest rooms; a **motel** is lodging designed especially for people traveling by car, often with rooms near parking. A **bed-and-breakfast** offers lodging with breakfast as part of its stated arrangement. A **hostel** may offer shared rooms or other lodging options. A **cabin** is a small house or shelter, often associated with an outdoor setting. A **campground** has camping sites; an **RV** is a recreational vehicle used for travel and accommodation.

**Pet-friendly** says pets are welcomed under specified conditions. **Accessible** describes accessibility, but you need details: “Is there a step-free entrance?” “Does the bathroom have the features I need?” Ask about **Wi-Fi**, a **pool**, a **balcony**, a **crib** for a baby, **room service**, and a **buffet**, where food is laid out for selection. A label alone does not answer every question.

### Pack and arrange

**Passport**, **ticket**, **boarding pass**, **currency**, **travel adapter**, **charger**, and **sunscreen** are useful packing terms. An adapter connects plugs of different designs; it does not automatically describe voltage conversion.

You **book a flight**, **make a reservation**, **rent a car**, **reserve a campsite**, **pack a suitcase**, and **exchange money**. You **check in** when beginning a hotel stay and **check out** when ending it. **Go abroad** means travel to another country.

### Activities and sights

- **Hiking:** walking outdoors on routes or trails.
- **Kayaking:** traveling in a narrow boat called a kayak, using a paddle to move.
- **Sailing:** traveling in a boat moved by wind in its sails.
- **Surfing:** riding waves on a board.
- **Paddleboarding:** moving across water on a board using a paddle.
- **Snorkeling:** swimming near the surface while breathing through a tube called a snorkel.
- **Scuba diving:** diving underwater with carried breathing equipment.
- **Skiing / snowboarding:** snow activities using skis or a board.
- **Guided tour:** a visit led by a guide. **Audio guide:** recorded information for a visit.
- **Museum / art gallery / market / gardens:** places with collections, art, goods, or plants.
- **Pier:** a structure extending from shore over water. **Lighthouse:** a tower or building with a light used to guide ships.
- **Island:** land surrounded by water. **Mountains:** high, steep landforms.

A **life jacket** is flotation equipment. Naming an activity or its equipment does not mean you know how to do it safely; ask the operator about requirements.

## Practice

Fictional choices: Lakeside Cabin has a kitchen and hiking nearby, but no pool. Harbor Hotel has a pool and breakfast buffet. Pine Campground has tent sites and an RV area. Each location has different arrangements; no price is supplied.

1. Choose accommodation for a quiet walking trip. Name two features you want to check before booking.
2. Pick five packing words for a flight and hotel stay. Explain their purposes briefly.
3. Describe a trip using one booking verb, one accommodation word, one activity, and one attraction. Alone, say it out loud; with someone else, exchange plans.

**Possibilities:** “I'd like to reserve a cabin and go hiking.” “Does Harbor Hotel have a crib available?” “I'd pack my passport and charger.” Check whether your words fit the trip. Related lessons will turn selected vocabulary into booking, hotel, camping, and sightseeing exchanges.
