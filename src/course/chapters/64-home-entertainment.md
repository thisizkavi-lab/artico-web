# CH64 — Home Entertainment

**Outcome:** Choose something to watch or play, discuss controls and device problems, and negotiate a break or stopping time.  

## Study

A **series** contains related episodes; an **episode** is one installment. **Streaming** lets you play media through an internet connection. **Subtitles** display spoken words or a translation on the screen. A **remote** controls a device from a distance; a **controller** provides controls for a game. A **console** is a device for playing video games. **Headphones** let someone listen without using the room's speakers. A **smart speaker** can play audio and respond to supported voice commands.

### Decide together

For these practice scenes, the available choices are *Harbor Detectives*, episode 1, twenty-five minutes; *The Last Train*, a ninety-minute movie; and a twenty-minute soccer highlights program. *Harbor Detectives*, episode 2, is not available yet. The game is a two-player racing game with two controllers.

**A:** What do you feel like watching?  
**B:** I'd like something short. What about the soccer highlights?  
**A:** I'm not really in the mood for sports. Could we try the detective series?  
**B:** Sure. Let's watch the first episode.  
**A:** Do you want the subtitles on?  
**B:** Yes, please. I find them helpful.  

**I'm in the mood for...** describes a current preference; **I'm not in the mood for...** declines that type of activity. **I don't like sports** describes a broader preference. **Let's try it** agrees to give something a chance rather than promises you will like it.

**A:** Shall we watch the next episode?  
**B:** It's not available yet.  
**A:** Okay. Would you rather watch the movie or stop here?  
**B:** Let's stop here. I don't want to start a ninety-minute movie now.  

### Change a control or report a problem

**Could you turn it up?** asks for more volume. **Could you turn it down?** asks for less. **Turn the subtitles on** enables them; **turn them off** disables them. Name what you want changed if **it** could refer to several things.

**A:** I can't hear the dialogue very well. Could you turn the volume up a little?  
**B:** Sure. Is that better?  
**A:** Yes, thanks. Could we turn the subtitles on too?  
**B:** Okay.  

For a device problem, describe the symptom: **The screen is frozen**, **The controller isn't responding**, or **There's no sound**. **Frozen** here means the screen or application has stopped responding, not that it is cold.

**A:** My tablet's frozen. The picture hasn't changed, and it isn't responding when I tap the screen.  
**B:** Did it start during the video?  
**A:** Yes, a moment ago. I don't know why. Could you help me find the device's support instructions?  

**Do we have another controller?** requests an alternative. **Is the console plugged in?** asks about a connection; it is not the same as knowing the cause of a fault.

### Play, pause and negotiate

**A:** I'm gonna win this time.  
**B:** We'll see!  
**A:** Damn, you got me again!  
**B:** Want another race?  
**A:** In a minute. I'm grabbing something to eat. I'll be right back.  

**Gonna** represents **going to** in casual language. **You got me** here means you beat or caught the speaker in the game. **Damn** can express annoyance or surprise; it is an impolite expression that some listeners find offensive. It can be an active choice among people comfortable using it. **Ah, you got me again!** expresses the same immediate reaction without that word. Neither phrase has to be directed angrily at the other player.

For another scene, a household has agreed to stop playing at eight. It is now eight, and one player asks for five more minutes.

**A:** It's eight. We agreed to stop now.  
**B:** Could I have five more minutes to finish this race?  
**A:** Okay, until eight oh five. Then we're stopping.  
**B:** Deal.  

A different reply is **No, we need to stop now**. **Deal** accepts the stated arrangement. Asking for more time does not itself extend the agreement.

## Practice

Alone, play both speakers. Use the available titles, episode information and stopping-time facts; no video or recording is required.

1. You want something short, but your partner does not want sports. Suggest a choice, negotiate and agree on one available program.
2. Ask for episode 2. Give the unavailable reply and offer the movie or stopping. Decline the movie because of its length.
3. Ask for louder dialogue and subtitles. Reply and check whether the change helps.
4. Lose a race and react. Then ask for a break. Choose language that fits the imagined relationship.
5. Ask for five more minutes at eight. Rehearse both agreement until eight oh five and refusal. Respond to the actual answer.

**Sample possibilities:** “Let's try the first detective episode.” / “Episode two isn't available yet. Would you rather watch the movie or stop?” / “Could you turn the volume up a little?” / “Ah, you got me again! I'll be right back.” / “Okay, eight oh five, then we stop.” / “All right. I'll stop now.”  

Return later with the models covered and reverse the preferences. Check whether you negotiated an activity and responded to your partner rather than reciting every option.
