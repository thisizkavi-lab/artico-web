# CH10 — Saying Goodbye

**Your goal:** End an encounter, acknowledge the time together, and make clear whether future contact is a plan or a general wish.  

## Study: Signal that you are leaving

An ending can begin before the final **Bye**. These expressions let the other person know the interaction is coming to a close:

- **I need to get going.** You need to leave.
- **I'm going to head out.** You are about to leave.
- **I have to go.** A clear statement that you need to stop.
- **I need to end the call now.** Explicitly close a call.

**After visiting a friend**

**Jamie:** I should get going. It was great to see you.  
**Sky:** You too. Thanks for coming over.  
**Jamie:** Thanks for having me. See you soon.  
**Sky:** Bye. Take care.  

**It was great to see you** acknowledges the encounter. **Take care** gives a friendly closing wish. You can answer **You too** when the same wish applies to both people.

## Study: Choose a closing

| Expression | A possible use |
| --- | --- |
| **Bye. / Goodbye.** | End contact. |
| **See you later.** | A closing anticipating later contact, without necessarily fixing a time. |
| **See you on Tuesday.** | Refer to an understood Tuesday meeting. |
| **Talk to you soon.** | Anticipate another conversation. |
| **Good talking with you.** | Acknowledge the conversation. |
| **Have a good evening.** | Wish someone a pleasant evening as you leave. |
| **Good night.** | Close contact at night or before sleep. |

**Good evening** can open an evening encounter; **Good night** generally has a different job. Chapter 1 introduced that contrast.

At a meeting with a new contact, **It was a pleasure meeting you** is an available choice. A shorter **Nice meeting you** can do similar work. **Thank you for your time** is useful after someone has made time to help or meet you. Professional contact does not require every one of these phrases.

**A project conversation ends**

**Sky:** That answers my questions. Thank you for your time.  
**Jamie:** You're welcome. I'll send the document this afternoon.  
**Sky:** Great. Good talking with you.  
**Jamie:** You too. Goodbye.  

The document promise belongs to this scene. The closing itself does not require a promise.

## Study: Future contact can be specific or open

**We should do this again sometime** expresses interest in another meeting, but it is not a scheduled appointment. **See you Friday at six** refers to a more specific arrangement.

**Jamie:** This was fun. Let's do it again.  
**Sky:** I'd like that. I'll text you next week.  
**Jamie:** Sounds good. Bye.  

Here Sky offers a next step. If you want a definite time, **When are you free?** can begin scheduling in Chapter 12. If you do not want to promise future contact, you can simply close with **It was good talking with you. Take care**.

**Have a safe trip** wishes someone well when they are traveling. If you are not traveling too, **Thanks** may fit better than automatically saying **You too**.

## Optional: Familiar closings

**Catch you later** and **See ya** can close a familiar exchange. **I gotta go** is an informal written representation of **I've got to go**. **I'm out** can announce your departure in a casual scene. Choose the style you intend; none is a required way to sound American.

## Practice: Bring the encounter to an end

On your own, speak both people's lines. Try a supported ending first, then hide the models and change the relationship.

1. End a visit to a friend's home. Include a departure signal, one acknowledgment, and a goodbye. Let the friend respond.
2. End a brief work call after getting an answer. You do not need to promise another meeting. Support: **That answers my question**, **Thanks for your help**, **Good talking with you**.
3. A friend says **We should get lunch again sometime**. You would like to, but no date is fixed. Reply without pretending there is already an appointment.
4. You have agreed to meet Friday at six. Close with that plan clear. Your partner answers **See you then**.
5. Someone wishes you **Have a safe trip**. They are staying home. Give a reply that fits.
6. Optional: you are leaving a casual game night. Try **I gotta go. Catch you later**, then give the friend's response.

### Sample possibilities

1. **I need to get going. Thanks for having me. — You're welcome. Take care. — You too. Bye.**
2. **That answers my question. Thanks for your help. — Happy to help. — Good talking with you. Goodbye.**
3. **I'd like that. Let's find a day next week.**
4. **See you Friday at six. — See you then.**
5. **Thanks. Take care.**
6. **I gotta go. Catch you later. — Okay. See ya.**

**Check:** Could the other person tell you were ending the exchange? Was future contact confirmed or still open? Did your reply fit the wish or information actually given?
