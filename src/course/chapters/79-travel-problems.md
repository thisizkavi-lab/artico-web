# CH79 — Travel Problems

**Outcome:** Report a missing item or travel disruption, ask about options, and explain a health-related change of plan.  

## Study

A **missing** item cannot be found; **stolen** means taken without permission. Say what you know: “I can't find my passport” does not establish theft. A **claim number** or **reference number** identifies a report. **Delayed** and **canceled** describe different travel problems.

### Missing belongings

**Traveler:** My suitcase didn't arrive on the flight.  
**Agent:** Which flight were you on?  
**Traveler:** Flight 246 from Denver. It's a dark green suitcase with a yellow tag.  
**Agent:** Do you have the baggage receipt?  
**Traveler:** Yes. Could you explain how to make a report?  
**Agent:** I'll take the details and give you a reference number.  
**Traveler:** How can I check for updates?  

For a passport: “My passport was in my bag, and the bag is missing.” If you witnessed theft, say “Someone took my bag.” Ask “Where can I report this?” and “Who can advise me about replacing my passport?” Replacement procedures depend on the issuing authority and your location; the dialogue supplies no guarantee of immediate travel.

If someone returns an item: “That's mine—thank you for bringing it back.” If ownership is uncertain: “Could you describe it? I want to make sure it's the right bag.”

### Disruptions and charges

**Traveler:** My flight was canceled. What options are available?  
**Agent:** I'll check the next departures for your booking.  
**Traveler:** I need to arrive today. Is another route possible?  
**Agent:** I'll look, but I can't confirm that yet.  
**Traveler:** Please also explain the refund options if I don't take another flight.  

This teaches a request for options, not a universal refund or compensation rule. For a train: “The departure board says canceled. Is there a replacement service?” For a closed gate: “I understand boarding has closed. Who can help me with the next step?”

A **baggage fee** is a baggage charge. Ask “What is this additional fee for?” **Strike** refers to a work stoppage. **Staffing shortage** means too few staff are available. A notice naming either cause does not tell you everything about rebooking or payment.

### Illness changes the plan

**Traveler:** I'm sick and may need to change my return date.  
**Insurance representative:** What happened, and where are you now?  
**Traveler:** I'm in Harbor City. I saw a doctor today, and I need to discuss whether I can travel.  
**Representative:** I'll explain the information we need to review your claim.  
**Traveler:** What documents do you require? Please send the conditions in writing.  

Avoid assuming a doctor's note automatically establishes coverage. You may hear *tummy bug* for a stomach illness. You can describe what you're experiencing with “I've been vomiting” or “I have an upset stomach,” without choosing a diagnosis. “I hurt my back” describes an event; “I'm not fit to fly” is a claim you may need a clinician to assess. Ask “Who can advise me whether I should travel?”

With a companion: “I'm upset about this. I need a minute, then we'll work out the options.” Frustration can be real; you need not perform cheerful acceptance.

## Practice

Fictional case: Flight 246 from Denver; green suitcase, yellow tag, baggage receipt available. Later, the return flight is canceled. Separately, use an illness role with no coverage decision made.

1. Report the missing suitcase. Partner asks for its description and flight. Supply the details and ask how to get updates.
2. Partner says the next route is not confirmed. Explain that you need to arrive today and ask about an alternative or refund option without claiming entitlement.
3. Explain the illness-related concern to an insurer. Partner asks for documents. Ask what is required rather than saying coverage is guaranteed.

Solo: read the staff questions and answer, then retell one case without the card. Change one known fact on the next attempt.

**Possibilities:** “It's dark green with a yellow tag.” “What happens if I don't take the alternative flight?” “Could you send the document requirements?” Check that you distinguish observed facts, uncertainties, and the specific help requested. Health-reporting support follows in Chapters 80–87.
