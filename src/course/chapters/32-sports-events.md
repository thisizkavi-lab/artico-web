# CH32 — Sports Events

**Your goal:** Ask about event tickets and seating, make a viewing plan, and react while watching a game.  

## Study

### Check availability before deciding

**Tickets go on sale Friday** means they become available to buy on Friday in this context. It does not necessarily mean a discount. **Sold out** means no tickets remain in the stated offer. **A few seats left** indicates limited availability, without promising they will remain available.

**Friend:** Want to go to the Tigers game?  
**You:** Maybe. When do tickets go on sale?  
**Friend:** Friday at noon.  
**You:** Let's check the price before we decide.  

**When is the game?**, **Are any seats available?**, and **Is there a student discount?** ask different questions. **A season ticket** or **season-ticket package** covers a specified series of events; ask which games and conditions the offer includes. **Final** names the deciding stage of a competition; **tournament** is an organized competition involving multiple games or contests.

### Choose seats and confirm the charge

This fictional ticket card is already available for purchase:

| Detail | Practice information |
| --- | --- |
| Event | River City Tigers versus Harbor Hawks, Saturday at 4 p.m. |
| Reserved seats | $25 each; C14 and C15 are available together. |
| General admission | $18 each; does not assign a particular seat in this offer. |
| Order fee | $2 for the whole order. |
| Access information | Step-free entry at the West Gate; wheelchair viewing spaces in Section C; ask staff about space and companion-seat availability. |

**Customer:** Are there two reserved seats together for Saturday?  
**Employee:** Yes. C14 and C15 are available at twenty-five dollars each.  
**Customer:** What's the total, including the order fee?  
**Employee:** Fifty-two dollars for the pair.  
**Customer:** Okay. I'd like those two. Could you confirm the section and start time?  
**Employee:** Section C. The game starts at four.  

**Reserved seating** assigns seats under the offer. **General admission** gives entry without the same assigned-seat arrangement. Details vary, so ask **Does that ticket guarantee a seat?** if the offer is unclear. **I'd like those two** makes the choice after the details have been explained; **I'll think about it** leaves the purchase unconfirmed.

For an access need: **I need a wheelchair viewing space and a seat for my companion. What's available?** Or **Which entrance has a step-free route to Section C?** Describe the actual feature you need. An accessibility label alone may not answer it.

If the event is sold out, you can ask **Is there an official waitlist?** or choose another event. No waitlist or discount is supplied in this card; those answers remain open.

### Arrange to watch elsewhere

In a separate fictional café, staff have confirmed that they will show the Tigers–Hawks game at 4 p.m. Tables are available from 3:30.

**Customer:** Are you showing the Tigers game on Saturday?  
**Staff:** Yes, at four.  
**Customer:** Could we get a table at three thirty? There are three of us.  
**Staff:** Yes, we can reserve one for three thirty.  
**Customer:** Great. So three people at three thirty, and the game starts at four.  

**Show the game** means display the broadcast here, rather than stage the contest in the café. **Who are you rooting for?** asks which side you support. **I'm rooting for the Tigers**, **I'm just watching**, and **I don't follow either team** give different honest answers. A **fan** supports or enjoys a team, player or activity; **follow a team** means keep up with it.

### React to what happens

**Come on, Tigers!**, **Let's go!**, **Nice shot!**, and **Keep going!** encourage a team or player. **That was close** reacts to a near success or a tense moment. **We won** and **We lost** can express a fan's identification with the team, even when the fan did not play.

After a lost lead in an invented game:

**Fan A:** Damn, we blew that lead.  
**Fan B:** Yeah. We were two points ahead, and now we're behind.  
**Fan A:** There's still time. Come on, Tigers!  

**Blow a lead** means lose an advantage. **Damn** is a swear word expressing frustration here; listeners differ in how they receive it. **That's frustrating** gives another voice for the same feeling. Use a choice that fits your intended effect and company. Swearing is not required to sound involved.

**I don't think that was a foul** disputes a call. **Maybe—I didn't see it clearly** preserves uncertainty. You can disagree about a moment without inventing knowledge of a rule or insisting that everyone share your team loyalty.

## Practice

1. Use the ticket card. Ask for two seats together, check the total fee, then choose the seats or decline. Staff answer using C14 and C15.
2. Ask about a wheelchair viewing space and companion seating. Staff say **I'll check availability**. Ask which entrance to use; that answer is supplied in the card. Keep the space request unconfirmed.
3. Use the café scene to arrange a table for three at 3:30. Confirm the table time and game time separately.
4. You support the Tigers. Answer **Who are you rooting for?** Then change your role: you do not follow either team. Give an honest response.
5. Your team was ahead and has fallen behind. React with the frustrated or less forceful option, then add encouragement. A friend disagrees about a foul you did not see clearly; respond without claiming certainty.

**Possible responses:** “Are those two seats together? Is the total fifty-two dollars? I'll take them.” / “Could you check the space and companion seat? Which entrance is step-free?” / “Three people at three thirty, and the game at four—that's right?” / “I'm rooting for the Tigers” or “I'm just watching.” / “That's frustrating. There's still time—let's go!” / “Maybe. I didn't see it clearly.”  

**Solo route:** speak both sides using the supplied facts, then cover the examples. Change your purchase decision or team preference later. **Check:** Did the price, seating need and plan become clear? Did you distinguish availability from confirmation, and your reaction from a fact you could actually establish?
