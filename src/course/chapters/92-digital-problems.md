# CH92 — Digital Problems

**Outcome:** Report a digital failure, separate observations from guesses, and ask support about the next step.  

## Study

**Frozen** means not responding in the described situation. **Crash** means software stops working unexpectedly. **Laggy** describes delayed response. **Down** can describe an unavailable service. **Error message** is displayed information about a failure. These labels do not explain every cause.

### Give a useful report

**User:** The booking page keeps closing when I select a date.  
**Support:** Which device and browser are you using? What message appears?  
**User:** A laptop, using Harbor Browser. It says “Unable to complete request.”  
**Support:** When did it start?  
**User:** About ten minutes ago. It happened three times.  
**Support:** Are other pages loading?  
**User:** Yes. I don't know whether it's the site or my device.  

Harbor Browser and the error are fictional. Include the task, visible behavior, timing, and what you already tried. “The page won't load” is an observation. “Their server is overloaded” is a possible cause, not a fact established by a busy ticket sale.

### Ask before repeating or changing something

**Support:** Would you like to try another approved browser for this service?  
**User:** Before I do, will that affect the form I already filled in?  
**Support:** Let's check whether that information has been saved.  
**User:** Also, I clicked Pay once. Can you confirm whether a payment went through?  

Don't treat every failure as proof that an order was not submitted. “I don't want to submit it twice” states the concern. “Please check the existing order first” requests a sequence. This example does not claim that changing browsers fixes the issue or that a payment has occurred.

You may hear “Try restarting” or “Check the connection.” Ask “Do you mean the app, the device, or the router?” if the object is unclear. “I've already tried that” supplies useful history. Actual troubleshooting steps depend on the product and saved work; this lesson does not provide a universal reset procedure.

### Connection during a call

**Participant:** Your audio keeps cutting out.  
**Speaker:** Is it my audio or the whole call?  
**Participant:** I can see you, but parts of your sentences are missing.  
**Speaker:** I'll put the key point in the chat. Can we confirm it there?  

**Cutting out / breaking up** describes interrupted sound. A text summary is one communication option, not a repair to the network itself.

### Account and security concerns

**User:** I got a sign-in alert I don't recognize.  
**Support:** Tell us what you noticed through our official support channel.  
**User:** I want to check whether someone accessed my account. I won't send my password in this conversation.  

**Multi-factor authentication / MFA** uses more than one factor to verify access. Exact setup and recovery steps depend on the service. Ask “Where are the official setup instructions?” and “What are my recovery options?” Do not assume a single menu sequence applies everywhere.

If financial details may have been exposed: “I may have entered my card information on a fake page. I need to discuss next steps with my card issuer.” Verify the contact channel. The FTC's guidance supports using known genuine contacts; neither a friend nor this model can promise the money will be recovered or the account is now secure.

## Practice

Fictional case: laptop, Harbor Browser, booking page closes on date selection, error “Unable to complete request,” three occurrences in ten minutes, other pages load; Pay clicked once, payment status unknown.

1. Give a support report. Partner asks the questions in the first model. Include observed facts and uncertainty.
2. Partner suggests another browser. Ask about saved data and the existing payment before resubmitting.
3. In a call, partner misses part of your explanation. Offer a chat summary and confirm the key point.
4. Report an unfamiliar sign-in alert through a fictional support exchange. Ask for official recovery information; do not use real credentials.

Solo: read support prompts and respond, then hide the case and summarize the problem. Later change the error rather than assuming the same cause.

**Possibilities:** “Other pages work, so I'm not sure what's wrong.” “Please check whether the payment was submitted.” “Where can I find your official recovery instructions?” Check that the failure, uncertainty, history, and requested next step are clear.  

*Source note: [FTC phishing guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams) informs verified contact and compromised-information scenarios; troubleshooting models are invented questions, not repair instructions.*
