# CH59 — Meeting the Neighbors

**Outcome:** Introduce yourself as a neighbor, respond to an invitation, and exchange a useful local question.  

## Study

**I've just moved in next door** explains why you are introducing yourself. **I live upstairs** or **I'm in apartment six** locates your home. A **neighbor** lives nearby; a **housewarming** is a gathering to mark a new home.

### Introduce yourself

**New neighbor:** Hi, I'm Tessa. I've just moved in next door.  
**Neighbor:** Nice to meet you. I'm Ben. Welcome to the neighborhood.  
**Tessa:** Thanks. I wanted to introduce myself.  
**Ben:** How's the move going?  
**Tessa:** Pretty well. We still have a lot to unpack.  

The greeting and name exchange reuse Chapters 1–2. The new information is your relationship as a neighbor and a shared local context. **I just came by to say hello** gives a reason for a brief visit. If the timing is inconvenient: **I don't want to keep you. I'll see you around** closes without forcing a longer conversation.

### Invite and respond

**Ben:** We're having a small housewarming on Saturday. Would you like to come?  
**Tessa:** Thanks. What time?  
**Ben:** Around six.  
**Tessa:** I'd like to, but I'm not sure whether I'll be free. Could I let you know tomorrow?  
**Ben:** Of course.  
**Tessa:** Great. Thanks for asking.  

**Around six** is an approximate time. **I'd love to come** accepts with enthusiasm; **I can't make it, but thanks for inviting me** declines. **Maybe** or **I'll let you know** leaves the plan unconfirmed. If you want a brief meeting instead: **Would you like to come over for coffee sometime?** suggests it without fixing a date.

You can set a boundary: **Thanks, but I prefer to keep evenings quiet** or **I won't join this time**. A reason can help, but you do not need to invent one.

### Ask for local information

**Tessa:** Do you know a plumber who works in this area?  
**Ben:** I know someone I've used before. I can send you the business details.  
**Tessa:** That would help. Thank you.  
**Ben:** Are you interested in the neighborhood group chat?  
**Tessa:** What do people use it for?  
**Ben:** Mostly local updates and questions.  
**Tessa:** Could you tell me how to request an invitation?  
**Ben:** Sure.  

**Do you know a...?** asks for a contact or suggestion, not proof that the person is qualified or available. **What is it used for?** checks a group's purpose before joining. **I'd rather not join, but thanks** is a complete decline.

Other questions include **Where can I find the building's information?**, **Who handles maintenance requests?**, and **Is there a notice board for residents?** When a neighbor does not know: **No problem. I'll ask the manager** keeps the uncertainty visible.

### A minor shared-space concern

**Neighbor:** I think this box may be blocking the hallway. Is it yours?  
**Resident:** Yes. I didn't realize it was in the way. I'll move it.  
**Neighbor:** Thanks.  
**Resident:** Thanks for letting me know.  

**Is it yours?** checks responsibility rather than assuming. **It's in the way** explains the practical concern. You can also say **I'm not sure whose it is. Let's ask before moving it** when ownership is unknown.

## Practice

Read the other neighbor's turns and answer, then switch roles if alone.

1. Introduce yourself as the person who moved into apartment 6. Give a short moving update.
2. A Saturday invitation is offered. Ask the time, then accept, decline, or say you will confirm tomorrow.
3. Ask about a local repair contact. The neighbor offers a group chat instead; ask what it is for before deciding.
4. A neighbor asks whether a hallway box is yours. It is not yours and you do not know who owns it. Respond without accusing another person.

**Sample possibilities:** “Hi, I'm Tessa. I've moved into apartment six. We're still unpacking.” / “What time? I'd like to come, but could I confirm tomorrow?” / “What is the group chat used for?” / “It isn't mine. I'm not sure whose it is.”  

Repeat later with another invitation or local question. Check that the other person could distinguish your introduction, confirmed plans and unanswered questions.
