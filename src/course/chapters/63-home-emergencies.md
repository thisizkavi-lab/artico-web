# CH63 — Home Emergencies

**Outcome:** Describe an urgent household problem, give its location and timing, and clarify the help being arranged.  

## Study

Start with what is happening: **There's water coming through the ceiling**, **The toilet is overflowing**, **A window is broken**, **We have no hot water**, or **The heating won't come on**. **Overflowing** means liquid is spilling over the edge of a container or fixture. **Leaking** means liquid is escaping through an opening or damaged area. A **dripping faucet** releases drops; a **blocked sink** does not drain properly.

**The power is out** reports that the electricity supply is not working. A **power outage** is an interruption of electricity supply. **The power is back on** reports its return. **Only the kitchen lights are out** describes a smaller observed problem; it does not establish the cause.

For heating equipment, **The heating system isn't working** is useful when you do not know its type. A **boiler** heats water for a heating system. You do not need the correct technical name to explain **The rooms are getting cold**.

### Report facts and uncertainty

**Resident:** The kitchen lights went out about ten minutes ago.  
**Building contact:** Are the lights working in the other rooms?  
**Resident:** The living-room light is still on. I don't know what's causing it.  
**Contact:** I'll record those details and ask the maintenance team to contact you.  
**Resident:** Could you tell me who will contact me and how soon I should expect an update?  

**Went out** describes the change; **are out** describes the current state. **I don't know whether the whole building is affected** is more accurate than claiming a building-wide problem from one room's lights.

### Ask for urgent help

For this fictional reporting scene, the resident is in apartment 3 at 18 Cedar Street. Water began coming through the kitchen ceiling five minutes ago and is still coming through. The resident does not know the source.

**Resident:** I need urgent help. Water is coming through my kitchen ceiling.  
**Service contact:** What's the address?  
**Resident:** Eighteen Cedar Street, apartment three.  
**Contact:** When did it start?  
**Resident:** About five minutes ago. It's still happening. I don't know where the water is coming from.  
**Contact:** I'll pass this to the person handling urgent maintenance reports.  
**Resident:** Please make clear that it's continuing. Can they speak to me about what to do next?  
**Contact:** I'll ask them to contact you directly.  

Use **urgent** when you need prompt attention. **It's getting worse** reports a change; **It's still happening** reports continuation. **What should I do while waiting for professional help?** requests situation-specific guidance. The reporting conversation does not establish that the problem has been fixed.

### Clarify a service visit

In a separate fictional scene, a repair business can inspect a dripping kitchen faucet Thursday between ten and noon. Its $85 service-call fee covers the visit and inspection; repair work would be quoted separately. The faucet has been dripping for three days.

**Customer:** My kitchen faucet has been dripping for three days. When could someone inspect it?  
**Staff:** Thursday between ten and noon.  
**Customer:** What is the service-call fee?  
**Staff:** Eighty-five dollars for the visit and inspection. Repairs would be quoted separately.  
**Customer:** So that fee doesn't include a repair?  
**Staff:** That's right.  
**Customer:** Okay. Please confirm the appointment window and the fee in the booking details.  

Other useful questions are **What information do you need?**, **Can you confirm you've received the report?**, **Is this an arrival window or an exact appointment time?**, and **Who should I contact if I don't receive an update?** Ask about an inspection or proposed work without claiming you know the repair method.

## Practice

Speak both roles alone, using the scene facts for the contact's questions. A partner can change one question after the supported round.

1. Report the kitchen lights going out ten minutes ago. Explain that the living-room light still works and that you do not know the cause.
2. Report the continuing ceiling leak. Answer **Where?**, **When did it start?**, and **Is it still happening?** Request contact with the person handling urgent reports.
3. Book the faucet inspection. Check the Thursday arrival window, the $85 fee and whether repairs are included.
4. Change the urgent problem to an overflowing bathroom toilet at the same address. It began two minutes ago and is still overflowing. Keep the location and timeline specific.

**Sample possibilities:** “Only the kitchen lights are out. The living-room light still works.” / “Water is coming through the kitchen ceiling in apartment three at eighteen Cedar Street. It started five minutes ago and is still happening.” / “Does the eighty-five-dollar fee include repairs?” / “It's the bathroom toilet. It started overflowing two minutes ago.”  

Return later with the models covered. Check whether you communicated the observed problem, exact location, timing and help requested without turning a guess into a diagnosis.
