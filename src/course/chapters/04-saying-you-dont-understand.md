# CH04 — When Something Isn't Clear

**Your goal:** Ask about the part you missed, clarify what someone means, and check that you've understood.

## Study: When you didn't catch something

You may understand most of a sentence but miss one detail—a time, name, number, or place. Ask about that part instead of making the other person repeat everything.

**Maya:** The workshop starts at ten thirty in Room 214.  
**Sam:** Sorry, I didn't catch the room number. Was that 214 or 240?  
**Maya:** Room 214.  
**Sam:** Okay, Room 214. Thanks. I'll see you there.  
**Maya:** See you then.

When you miss only one detail, ask about that detail. Offering the options you heard—'Was that 214 or 240?'—makes it easy for the other person to correct you. A quick 'Sorry?' can work when you need someone to repeat a short part. 'What?' can sound surprised or abrupt, depending on your tone and relationship.

## Study: When you heard it but don't understand

Sometimes you hear every word but don't know what an expression means. Ask about the word or phrase itself. Then say what you think it means to check.

**Alex:** You'll need to turn in the form by Friday.  
**Jordan:** Sorry, what does 'turn in' mean here?  
**Alex:** It just means submit it—give the completed form to the office.  
**Jordan:** Oh, so I can drop it off at the front desk?  
**Alex:** Exactly. You can hand it to the receptionist.  
**Jordan:** Got it. I'll drop it off tomorrow.  
**Alex:** Perfect. Thanks.

Turn in means submit something, such as a form or assignment. Drop it off means take something to a place and leave it there. The two can overlap in this situation, but turn in focuses on submitting, while drop off focuses on delivery.

## Study: When you need a different explanation

When an explanation is too fast or unclear, say where you got lost. You can ask for a slower pace, a repeat of one step, or a demonstration. Then repeat the steps in your own words.

**Maya:** To submit the request, open the portal, sign in, choose 'New request', attach the PDF, and click Submit.  
**Jordan:** Sorry, could you slow down a little? I got as far as 'New request', but I lost you after that.  
**Maya:** Of course. After you choose 'New request', click 'Choose file' and select the PDF.  
**Jordan:** I'm not sure I follow—where do I find the file?  
**Maya:** It should be in your Downloads folder.  
**Jordan:** Oh, I see. Then I click Submit?  
**Maya:** Exactly. You'll get a confirmation email.  
**Jordan:** So I don't need to email the PDF separately?  
**Maya:** That's right. Uploading it here is enough.  
**Jordan:** Got it. Thanks for walking me through it.

Slow down asks for a slower pace. Go over that again asks for repetition. Walk me through it asks for a step-by-step explanation. They solve related but different problems. Once it makes sense, confirm the steps in your own words and move on.

## Practice: Repair the exchange

Try each situation aloud. Don't aim for one exact sentence. Identify what is unclear, ask for the right kind of help, then confirm the answer.

1. Someone says, 'The train leaves at six forty from platform three.' You heard the time but missed the platform. Ask only about the platform. They say, 'Platform three.' Confirm and continue.
2. Someone says, 'The meeting has been pushed back to Friday.' You don't know what pushed back means. Ask about it. They explain, 'It means moved to a later time.' Check your understanding.
3. A coworker explains, 'Open the file, choose Export, then save it as a PDF.' They speak too quickly. Ask them to slow down or repeat the last step. Confirm the sequence.
4. Someone says, 'I'll send it over after lunch.' You don't know what send it over means. Ask for clarification. They explain, 'I'll email it to you.' Respond naturally.
5. Choose a conversation from this chapter and change the details. Try again without looking at the model.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Sorry, what was the platform number? — Platform three. — Okay, platform three. Thanks.**
2. **What do you mean by pushed back? — It means moved to a later time. — Oh, so it's happening later than planned? — Exactly.**
3. **Could you slow down a little? — Sure. Open the file, choose Export, and save it as a PDF. — Got it: Export, then save as a PDF.**
4. **What do you mean by send it over? — I'll email it to you. — Oh, okay. That works.**

**Check:** Did you ask about the exact problem? Did you ask for the right kind of help? Did you confirm what you understood and continue?
