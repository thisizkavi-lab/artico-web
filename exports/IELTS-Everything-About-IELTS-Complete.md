## 01. What IELTS measures: language and task

*Part 1.1 · Test Architecture & Conditions · 8 min · Academic and General Training · Source section: 1.1*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-what-ielts-measures)

IELTS measures communicative English proficiency across Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking. Every failed question stems from either a language deficit, a task management failure, or both.

**Why it matters:** If you mistake a language weakness for a technique problem, you will practice the wrong repair and your score will remain stagnant.

**What you will do:** Differentiate language problems from task problems, analyze a concrete failure specimen, and classify test errors accurately.



#### Diagnosing the Underlying Cause of Lost Marks

**Language Problem**

> The candidate cannot understand the recording due to unrecognized connected speech, or cannot write an essay because of limited vocabulary and unstable sentence structures.

**Why it fails:** Practicing superficial test strategies will not resolve missing vocabulary or faulty syntax.

**Task Problem**

> The candidate possesses strong English skills but exceeds the word limit, ignores a bullet point in the prompt, or spends 35 minutes on Reading Passage 1.

**Why it works:** Targeted procedural discipline and strict time allocation instantly eliminate task errors.

> **Watch out · Combined Breakdown Case**
>
> Both problems often occur together. A candidate may misread a qualifying adjective like "predominantly" (language problem) and subsequently write an absolute claim that contradicts the passage (task problem).



#### Four-Step Error Classification Routine

1. **Inspect the failed test item** — Why: Locates the exact question number and reveals your recorded answer.
2. **Audit language comprehension** — Why: Verifies whether you understood all words, qualifiers, and grammatical links in the text or audio script.
3. **Audit task compliance** — Why: Checks whether your answer observed all instruction limits, word counts, and format rules.
4. **Assign the targeted repair** — Why: Chooses vocabulary retrieval drills for language gaps, or pacing drills for task failures.

> **How to · Balancing Language Input with Task Rehearsal**
>
> With a Band 5.5 baseline and a Band 7.0 target, spend 70 percent of study time building core language. Allocate the remaining 30 percent to task familiarization and timed execution.

> **Watch out · Knowledge Versus Execution Under Pressure**
>
> Knowing a strategy does not prove that you can use it. Understanding a lesson does not prove that you can perform under strict time pressure. Performance evidence on fresh material is the only valid test.



#### Error Diagnostic Check

A capable candidate scores Band 6.0 in Writing because their 380-word essay lacks a conclusion after running out of time. What is the primary breakdown?

1. Task problem: poor time allocation and lack of paragraph planning. **(Correct)**
   - **Explanation:** The candidate has sufficient English but failed to control time, resulting in an incomplete response structure.

2. Language problem: insufficient lexical resource to write quickly. 
   - **Explanation:** Writing 380 words proves high lexical output. The failure was structural and procedural.

3. Assessment error: examiners penalize essays longer than 250 words. 
   - **Explanation:** There is no upper word limit penalty in IELTS. The penalty resulted from an omitted conclusion.



#### Understanding Test Demands

- [ ] I can determine whether a mistake resulted from unknown English or poor task execution.
- [ ] I recognize that memorizing tips cannot compensate for inadequate grammar or vocabulary.
- [ ] I understand that timed performance evidence is distinct from untimed comprehension.

## 02. Choose the right test: Academic and General Training

*Part 1.1 · Test Architecture & Conditions · 8 min · Academic and General Training · Source section: 1.2*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-choose-correct-test)

IELTS is offered in two primary versions: Academic and General Training. The receiving university, employer, or immigration authority determines which version you must submit.

**Why it matters:** Submitting a General Training result for university admission will lead to automatic rejection. Preparing with the wrong reading materials distorts your test readiness.

**What you will do:** Compare the two test versions across all four skills, verify six essential institutional requirements, and audit your target profile.



#### Academic versus General Training Comparison

**Academic**

- Reading: 3 long academic texts sourced from journals, books, and research reports.
- Writing Task 1: 150-word objective factual report on a chart, table, graph, map, or process.
- Writing Task 2: 250-word formal discursive essay on an academic or societal issue.
- Listening & Speaking: Shared identical format with General Training.
- Target audience: Higher education admissions and professional registration in medicine, engineering, and law.

**General Training**

- Reading: 3 sections featuring everyday notices, workplace handbooks, and general interest articles.
- Writing Task 1: 150-word letter written in a personal, semi-formal, or formal register.
- Writing Task 2: 250-word discursive essay on a topic of general personal or social interest.
- Listening & Speaking: Shared identical format with Academic.
- Target audience: Migration, secondary education, vocational training, and employment visas.

**Shared:** Listening and Speaking are identical across both versions, and core language and reasoning skills transfer directly.

Related lesson: [Open General Training Task 1 Letter units](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=w-gt-purpose-reader-register)



#### Six Items to Verify with Your Receiving Organization

1. **Verify accepted version** — Why: Confirms whether the institution requires Academic or General Training.
2. **Check overall band requirement** — Why: Records the exact required overall band score, such as Band 7.0.
3. **Check individual skill minimums** — Why: Verifies the minimum score for each sub-test, such as no sub-score below 6.5.
4. **Check delivery arrangements** — Why: Confirms whether the institution accepts computer-delivered, paper, or remote scores.
5. **Verify application deadlines** — Why: Ensures your test session occurs at least six weeks before the institutional deadline.
6. **Check route-specific rules** — Why: Confirms whether immigration authorities mandate specific test center credentials.

> **How to · Documenting Institutional Requirements**
>
> Create an official requirement dossier before beginning preparation. Record the specific test title, required overall band, individual section minimums, acceptable test centers, and delivery mode. Keep this record in your preparation folder to guide your target scores.

> **IELTS rule · IELTS for UKVI and Life Skills**
>
> IELTS for UKVI has identical test questions and scoring to standard IELTS, but uses administrative video logging approved by UK Visas and Immigration. IELTS Life Skills is a completely different, shorter speaking and listening test for family and settlement visas. Do not mistake IELTS Life Skills for the four-skill test.

Source: [Official IELTS test types](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types)

**Reflection / workbook prompt:** Record your target institution, required test version, overall band score, and individual sub-skill minimums.



#### Test Selection Audit

- [ ] I have confirmed the exact IELTS version demanded by my target organization.
- [ ] I have recorded the individual skill minimums, not merely the overall band score.
- [ ] I know whether my application requires a UKVI-accredited test session.

## 03. Test format, timing, and task weights

*Part 1.1 · Test Architecture & Conditions · 8 min · Academic and General Training · Source section: 1.3*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-format-and-timing)

The IELTS test assesses four skills under strict standardized timing. Listening, Reading, and Writing take place together in a single 2 hour 45 minute sitting without scheduled breaks.

**Why it matters:** Writing Task 2 carries twice the scoring weight of Task 1. Mismanaging your 60-minute writing period can destroy an otherwise strong examination performance.

**What you will do:** Review standard time allocations across all components, understand task weighting, and master the ideal time distribution for test day.



#### Standard Test Component Timing and Structure

| Skill | Structure | Standard Time Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listening | 4 recorded parts; 40 questions total. Audio is played once only. | Approximately 30 minutes, plus answer entry or review time. |
| Academic Reading | 3 long texts; 40 questions total. | 60 minutes total, including direct answer entry. No extra transfer time. |
| General Training Reading | 3 sections (multiple short and longer texts); 40 questions total. | 60 minutes total, including direct answer entry. No extra transfer time. |
| Writing | 2 tasks: Task 1 (150 words min) and Task 2 (250 words min). | 60 minutes shared between both tasks. Candidates manage their own time. |
| Speaking | 3 parts: familiar interview, individual long turn, and abstract discussion. | 11 to 14 minutes face-to-face or via high-definition video call. |

> **IELTS rule · Task 2 Carries Double Weight**
>
> Writing Task 2 contributes twice as much to your overall Writing score as Task 1. Allocate approximately 40 minutes to Task 2 and 20 minutes to Task 1.

> **Watch out · Task 1 Cannot Be Omitted**
>
> One strong Task 2 essay cannot replace a missing or incomplete Task 1 response. Leaving Task 1 unfinished severely penalizes Task Achievement and pulls down your overall Writing band regardless of Task 2 quality.



#### Standard Examination Time Allocation Plan

1. **Reading pacing: 20 minutes per passage** — Why: Spend 17 minutes reading and answering, and 3 minutes transferring answers directly to the sheet or screen.
2. **Writing Task 1 pacing: 20 minutes total** — Why: Spend 3 to 5 minutes analyzing data and planning, 12 minutes writing, and 3 minutes checking numbers and grammar.
3. **Writing Task 2 pacing: 40 minutes total** — Why: Spend 5 to 7 minutes analyzing the prompt and structuring main ideas, 30 minutes writing, and 3 to 5 minutes editing.

> **How to · Speaking Component Scheduling**
>
> The Speaking test can occur on the same day as the written components or within seven days before or after. Check your official confirmation slip for the exact venue and reporting time.

Source: [Official Academic Test Format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test)



#### Format and Timing Review

- [ ] I remember that Reading provides no additional transfer time at the end of the hour.
- [ ] I understand that Writing Task 2 is worth twice as many marks as Writing Task 1.
- [ ] I have practiced completing Reading and Writing sections in single unbroken sittings.

## 04. Delivery modes in 2026: computer and Writing on Paper

*Part 1.1 · Test Architecture & Conditions · 8 min · Academic and General Training · Source section: 1.4*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-delivery-modes-2026)

IELTS delivery has transitioned toward computer-delivered testing across global venues. From mid-2026, fully paper-based testing is being phased out in many markets, while hybrid delivery options remain available in select regions.

**Why it matters:** Using an old paper answer-transfer strategy on a computer test will cause you to run out of time during Listening and Reading.

**What you will do:** Compare full computer, hybrid Writing on Paper, and IELTS Online delivery, and practice six vital digital interface actions.

> **IELTS rule · Writing on Paper Delivery Option**
>
> Selected markets offer a "Writing on Paper" option within computer-delivered IELTS. Candidates read test passages and essay prompts on a screen, but write their Task 1 and Task 2 answers by hand on official paper sheets.

Source: [IELTS Writing on Paper details](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-on-computer-writing-on-paper)



#### Comparison of Current IELTS Delivery Options

| Feature | IELTS on Computer | Computer with Writing on Paper | IELTS Online (At Home) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listening setup | Headphones provided; type answers or click options directly. | Headphones provided; type answers or click options directly. | Own computer and private quiet room required. |
| Reading interface | Split-screen display; passage on left, questions on right. | Split-screen display; passage on left, questions on right. | Split-screen display on candidate computer monitor. |
| Writing input | Typed on keyboard; live automatic word count displayed. | Handwritten with pencil on official paper answer booklets. | Typed on keyboard; live automatic word count displayed. |
| Results release | Available online within 1 to 5 calendar days. | Available online within 1 to 5 calendar days. | Available in 3 to 6 days; restricted institutional acceptance. |
| Answer transfer | 2 minutes at end of Listening to review entries. | 2 minutes at end of Listening to review entries. | 2 minutes at end of Listening to review entries. |



#### Six Digital Interface Actions to Rehearse Before Test Day

1. **Build on-screen reading stamina** — Why: Helps you read 900-word academic articles on a computer monitor without eye strain.
2. **Develop typing speed and accuracy** — Why: Ensures you can type at least 30 clean words per minute on a physical external keyboard.
3. **Master digital navigation** — Why: Allows rapid movement through the bottom question navigation palette and review flags.
4. **Practice split-screen text highlighting** — Why: Enables quick selection of text anchors and insertion of digital notes.
5. **Perform the pre-test audio check** — Why: Verifies headphone operation and allows volume adjustment before the test starts.
6. **Enter answers directly** — Why: Prevents rushing because computer tests provide only 2 minutes to review Listening answers.

> **Watch out · Verify Acceptance of IELTS Online**
>
> IELTS Online is taken remotely from home, but many universities, medical boards, and immigration departments do not accept remote at-home results. Always verify acceptance with your receiving institution before booking.



#### Delivery Mode Preparedness

- [ ] I know whether my local test center offers computer delivery or Writing on Paper.
- [ ] I have practiced reading long academic passages on a computer display.
- [ ] I understand that computer-delivered Listening provides only 2 minutes to check answers.

## 05. Band scores, rounding, and raw marks

*Part 1.1 · Test Architecture & Conditions · 10 min · Academic and General Training · Source sections: 1.5, 1.6*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-scores-and-raw-marks)

IELTS reports results on a 9-band scale. Candidates receive an individual score from 1.0 to 9.0 for each of the four skills. They also receive an overall band calculated as the arithmetic average of all four components, rounded to the nearest whole or half band.

**Why it matters:** A high Listening score cannot compensate for an unmet Writing minimum. Knowing the exact raw-mark benchmarks tells you how many correct answers you need on test day.

**What you will do:** Master the official band rounding rule, examine raw mark conversion benchmarks, and understand the General Training raw mark differential.



#### Overall Band Score Rounding Rule

**Diagram caption:** The arithmetic average of the four skill scores rounds to the nearest whole or half band. A fraction of .25 rounds up to .5; a fraction of .75 rounds up to the next whole band.

**Diagram description:** Diagram showing IELTS rounding rules: 6.25 rounds up to 6.5, 6.75 rounds up to 7.0, and 6.125 rounds down to 6.0.

*Illustration identifier: `diag-score-rounding`. Markdown preserves the diagram's explanatory text, not its rendered SVG geometry.*



#### Official Overall Band Calculation and Rounding Examples

| Listening | Reading | Writing | Speaking | Average | Final Band | Official Rounding Logic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.5 | 6.5 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.25 | 6.5 | Fraction ends in .25; rounds up to the next half band. |
| 7.0 | 6.5 | 6.5 | 7.0 | 6.75 | 7.0 | Fraction ends in .75; rounds up to the next whole band. |
| 6.5 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.125 | 6.0 | Fraction is below .25; rounds down to the nearest whole band. |
| 7.5 | 7.0 | 6.5 | 7.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 | Exact whole number; remains unchanged. |



#### Raw Marks Required for Key Bands (Out of 40)

**Diagram caption:** Listening and Academic Reading require roughly 30 correct answers out of 40 for Band 7.0. General Training Reading requires 35 correct answers for Band 7.0 due to simpler texts.

**Diagram description:** Line chart comparing raw marks needed for Bands 5 to 8 across Listening, Academic Reading, and General Training Reading.

*Illustration identifier: `diag-raw-to-band`. Markdown preserves the diagram's explanatory text, not its rendered SVG geometry.*



#### Official Indicative Raw Mark Benchmarks (Out of 40)

| Band Score | Listening Raw Marks | Academic Reading Raw Marks | General Training Reading Raw Marks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Band 5.0 | 16 / 40 | 15 / 40 | 23 / 40 |
| Band 6.0 | 23 / 40 | 23 / 40 | 30 / 40 |
| Band 7.0 | 30 / 40 | 30 / 40 | 35 / 40 |
| Band 8.0 | 35 / 40 | 35 / 40 | 38 / 40 |

> **IELTS rule · General Training Reading Raw Mark Gap**
>
> General Training Reading texts use everyday workplace materials. Therefore, candidates must score 35 out of 40 for Band 7.0, compared to 30 out of 40 in Academic Reading. The margin for error is significantly tighter in General Training.

> **How to · Zero Negative Marking: Never Leave Blanks**
>
> Incorrect answers do not deduct marks from your correct responses. When uncertain, make your best-supported inference before moving to the next item. Never leave an answer space blank.

> **Watch out · Spelling Penalties and Proportion Fallacy**
>
> A spelling mistake loses that specific question mark; it does not trigger an automatic overall band deduction. Furthermore, never convert a short 10-question practice exercise into an estimated band score by simple proportion. Band conversions are calibrated across full 40-item tests.



#### Scoring and Raw Mark Mastery

- [ ] I can calculate my overall band score from the four component scores using the rounding rule.
- [ ] I know how many correct answers out of 40 I need to reach my target band in Listening and Reading.
- [ ] I understand why General Training Reading requires 5 more raw marks for Band 7.0 than Academic Reading.

## 06. Core assessment terms

*Part 1.1 · Test Architecture & Conditions · 9 min · Academic and General Training · Source section: 1.7*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-assessment-terms)

Effective preparation requires shared understanding of technical assessment terms. These 17 foundational terms define the criteria, evidence types, and operational concepts used by IELTS examiners.

**Why it matters:** If you misunderstand technical terms like Coherence, Cohesion, or Scope, you will focus on the wrong features in your writing and speaking responses.

**What you will do:** Study the precise definitions and concrete IELTS application examples for all 17 foundational assessment terms.



#### The 17 Foundational Assessment Terms and Their IELTS Application

| Term | Technical Definition | IELTS Application Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task | The exact work specified by the prompt instructions. | Summarizing main features of a chart or writing a 250-word balanced opinion essay. |
| Scope | The boundary conditions of an argument: group, frequency, time, and degree. | Recognizing that "some urban residents" cannot be paraphrased as "all citizens". |
| Evidence | Observable facts, audio excerpts, text lines, or data supporting a claim. | Quoting the exact text phrase that validates a True / False / Not Given question. |
| Paraphrase | Expressing identical meaning using different words and grammatical structures. | Transforming "the price increased significantly" into "there was a marked rise in cost". |
| Gist | The general overall meaning of an entire paragraph or spoken section. | Identifying that a paragraph discusses funding challenges without memorizing each figure. |
| Inference | A logical deduction supported by text facts when the exact statement is absent. | Concluding that a species is endangered because its natural forest habitat was destroyed. |
| Criterion | One official assessment area against which candidate performance is scored. | Task Achievement, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, or Grammatical Range. |
| Coherence | The logical arrangement, progression, and clarity of ideas across a response. | Structuring an essay so that each paragraph explores one clear central topic. |
| Cohesion | The specific linguistic devices and reference words that bind sentences together. | Using pronouns, conjunctions, and discourse markers without over-mechanical repetition. |
| Lexical Resource | The range, precision, and collocation control of candidate vocabulary. | Using precise topic vocabulary rather than repetitive common synonyms. |
| Collocation | Words that naturally and conventionally occur together in native speech and text. | Saying "conduct research" or "take an exam" rather than "make an exam". |
| Register | The level of formality suited to the communicative purpose and audience. | Using objective formal style in Task 2 and reserve informal idiom for Speaking Part 1. |
| Intelligibility | The ease with which a listener can understand spoken words and intended message. | Clear pronunciation, word stress, and natural rhythm that require minimal listener effort. |
| Baseline | Documented evidence of current performance before targeted intervention. | Scoring 24/40 on an unassisted practice test to establish current skill level. |
| Transfer | The successful application of a learned skill to a completely fresh task. | Using the overview-selection method on an unfamiliar pie chart without coaching. |
| Diagnostic | Analysis that pinpoints the exact operational reason for a test failure. | Determining whether a reading error was caused by unknown vocabulary or poor scanning. |
| Distractor | Plausible but incorrect information intended to test candidate precision. | A speaker mentioning "Room 14" before immediately correcting the location to "Room 18". |



#### Vague Descriptions versus Technical Precision

**Vague Impressionistic Description**

> "The essay sounded nice and used some advanced words, but the ideas seemed a bit messy."

**Why it fails:** Uses subjective impressions that cannot guide revisions or identify specific criterion criteria.

**Criterion-Referenced Diagnostic Analysis**

> "The essay demonstrates good Lexical Resource with topic-specific vocabulary. However, it lacks Coherence because body paragraphs omit topic sentences."

**Why it works:** Uses official assessment terms to pinpoint the exact structural weakness requiring repair.

> **How to · Using Assessment Terms in Your Error Log**
>
> Always use technical assessment terms in your error records. Writing "overlooked scope qualifier" or "failed collocation" identifies an exact linguistic fault. Vague labels like "careless mistake" prevent targeted skill improvement.

> **IELTS rule · Anchored in Official Public Band Descriptors**
>
> These 17 definitions align directly with the official public band descriptors published by Cambridge English and IDP. Master these concepts to evaluate your own practice responses accurately.



#### Assessment Term Self-Check

- [ ] I can explain the difference between Coherence (flow of ideas) and Cohesion (connective words).
- [ ] I understand why Lexical Resource rewards precise word choice over obscure synonyms.
- [ ] I know how to identify distractors before locking in an answer in Listening and Reading.
- [ ] I can locate the scope qualifiers in any Reading or Task 2 prompt.

## 07. Use one 9-step preparation cycle

*Part 1.2 · Universal Study, Vocabulary & Grammar System · 10 min · Academic and General Training · Source section: 2.1*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-preparation-cycle)

Effective test preparation follows an intentional nine-step progression divided into three phases: build skills, test control, and repair and transfer. Completing random tests without diagnosis produces fatigue without score improvement.

**Why it matters:** A skill is truly mastered only when it functions reliably on fresh, unseen test material under examination conditions.

**What you will do:** Study the three preparation phases, understand the nine steps, and apply the strict transfer check rule.



#### The Nine-Step Preparation Cycle

**Diagram caption:** Phase A builds foundational competence with worked examples and assisted tasks. Phase B measures independent test performance under time pressure. Phase C isolates weaknesses and tests repairs on fresh material.

**Diagram description:** Flowchart showing Phase A (understand, observe, explain, apply with support), Phase B (timed attempt, review evidence), and Phase C (isolate weakness, smallest repair, transfer check).

*Illustration identifier: `diag-9step-cycle`. Markdown preserves the diagram's explanatory text, not its rendered SVG geometry.*



#### The Nine Consecutive Steps in the Cycle

1. **Step 1: Understand the task** — Why: Prevents procedural errors and misread constraints.
2. **Step 2: Observe a worked example** — Why: Demonstrates how a skilled writer handles the prompt.
3. **Step 3: Explain the method aloud** — Why: Verifies conceptual understanding before writing.
4. **Step 4: Apply with full support** — Why: Builds muscle memory using notes and reference models.
5. **Step 5: First fresh timed attempt** — Why: Exposes actual operational performance under time limits.
6. **Step 6: Review objective evidence** — Why: Compares your actual output against official answer keys.
7. **Step 7: Isolate one specific weakness** — Why: Pinpoints the single cognitive breakdown that cost marks.
8. **Step 8: Practise the smallest useful repair** — Why: Corrects the exact linguistic or task defect.
9. **Step 9: Transfer check on fresh material** — Why: Proves the repair functions on completely new tasks.

> **How to · Pacing the Transition from Learning to Testing**
>
> Spend adequate time in Phase A before attempting timed tasks. Rushing into timed practice tests before mastering the method produces anxiety and embeds error patterns. Consolidate your technique under untimed conditions first.

> **Watch out · The Transfer Rule**
>
> If Step 9 fails on fresh material, do not advance to a new topic. Loop back to Step 5 and re-diagnose. A skill is mastered only when it transfers cleanly to an unfamiliar task.



#### Learning Evidence versus Performance Evidence

| Dimension | Phase A: Assisted Learning (Steps 1–4) | Phase B & C: Independent Performance (Steps 5–9) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing condition | Untimed; pause audio or check dictionaries whenever helpful. | Strict examination timing; continuous execution without pauses. |
| Assistance level | Hints, templates, and teacher corrections permitted. | Zero external assistance; simulates actual examination environment. |
| Primary objective | Acquire procedural competence and build language patterns. | Test operational reliability and expose points of failure under stress. |
| Evidence provided | Learning evidence: reveals what you can achieve with support. | Performance evidence: reveals what you can achieve independently. |



#### Preparation Cycle Implementation

- [ ] I explain the task method before attempting the exercise.
- [ ] I keep assisted learning sessions separate from timed baseline evaluations.
- [ ] I isolate one micro-skill at a time during Step 8 repairs.
- [ ] I always verify repairs with fresh, unseen test material before moving forward.

## 08. Diagnose before adding volume and log errors

*Part 1.2 · Universal Study, Vocabulary & Grammar System · 10 min · Academic and General Training · Source sections: 2.2, 2.3*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-diagnose-and-error-log)

A practice test score identifies a result; it does not reveal the operational cause of failure. Doing more practice tests without diagnosing root causes only entrenches bad habits. You must inspect the actual failed operation behind every lost mark.

**Why it matters:** Recording vague complaints like "I am bad at Reading" provides zero guidance. Documenting exact operational breakdowns enables immediate, measurable repairs.

**What you will do:** Identify the eleven primary causes of test errors, implement an eight-field error log, and apply the preservation rule.



#### The Eleven Primary Root Causes of Test Errors

1. **1. Missed instruction** — Why: Overlooking word limits, such as writing three words when instructed "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS".
2. **2. Wrong information located** — Why: Scanning for the wrong keyword and searching an irrelevant paragraph.
3. **3. Misunderstood evidence** — Why: Locating the correct sentence but misinterpreting its core grammatical meaning.
4. **4. Overlooked qualifier** — Why: Failing to notice limiting words like "rarely", "predominantly", or "allegedly".
5. **5. Unknown vocabulary** — Why: Encountering a critical keyword that is absent from your active lexicon.
6. **6. Unrecognized connected speech** — Why: Failing to catch weak forms, elision, or linking sounds in the audio recording.
7. **7. Incorrect answer entry** — Why: Misspelling an answer, transposing digits, or recording the answer in the wrong slot.
8. **8. Underdeveloped idea** — Why: Stating a claim in Writing Task 2 without explaining the causal mechanism or evidence.
9. **9. Uncovered prompt element** — Why: Addressing only one part of a multi-part essay prompt or omitting an overview in Task 1.

Paraphrasing is essential in IELTS, but reckless synonym replacement destroys precision. Swapping words merely to avoid repetition frequently introduces factual inaccuracies and grammatical errors.

**Why it matters:** Using an inappropriate synonym changes the scope of an argument or creates an unnatural collocation, directly weakening your Lexical Resource control.

**What you will do:** Apply the Five-Gate Synonym Verification Filter, inspect six common false synonym traps, and learn when to repeat the original word.



#### The Five-Gate Synonym Verification Filter

#### The Five Verification Gates Explained

1. **Gate 1: Meaning** — Why: Checks whether the substitute word shares the exact core definition in this specific context.
2. **Gate 2: Scope** — Why: Verifies whether the substitute applies to the same group, time, intensity, and geographical boundary.
3. **Gate 3: Grammar** — Why: Ensures the substitute fits the same grammatical structure, countability, and clause pattern.
4. **Gate 4: Collocation** — Why: Confirms that the substitute partners naturally with neighboring nouns, verbs, and prepositions.
5. **Gate 5: Register** — Why: Verifies that the substitute is appropriate for formal academic writing or objective reporting.



#### Six High-Frequency Synonym Traps and Their Scope Distinctions

| Proposed Replacement | Why the Substitution Fails | Correct Controlled Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residents → Citizens | Residents live in a location; they may be foreign workers or students without citizenship. | Use "residents" for local municipal services; use "citizens" for legal rights. |

> Repeat a precise word when any replacement would distort meaning or create unnatural phrasing. Examiners value accuracy and precision far above flawed variety.

## 11. Grammatical control and clear relationships

*Part 1.2 · Universal Study, Vocabulary & Grammar System · 10 min · Academic and General Training · Source sections: 2.8, 2.9*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-grammar-and-clear-meaning)

Grammar is not an ornamental checklist of complex structures. In IELTS, grammar functions as a precision tool to express logical relationships between ideas clearly on the first reading.

**Why it matters:** Attempting convoluted, error-filled sentences reduces your Grammatical Range and Accuracy score. Examiners look for a high proportion of error-free sentences.

**What you will do:** Review the top six recurring error targets, execute the 5-step grammar repair sequence, and balance active and passive voice.

> **IELTS rule · Meaning Over Fake Complexity**
>
> Do not force artificial complexity into every sentence. A convoluted, error-filled sentence reduces your Grammatical Range and Accuracy score. Balance simple, compound, and complex sentences with consistent control.



#### Top Six Recurring Grammar Error Targets

1. **1. Subject-verb agreement** — Why: Ensures the verb agrees with the true subject, especially when prepositional phrases intervene.
2. **2. Definite and indefinite articles** — Why: Controls the use of "the" with specific nouns, and "a/an" with singular countable nouns.
3. **3. Countable and uncountable nouns** — Why: Recognizes nouns that cannot take plural forms (information, advice, research, equipment).
4. **4. Tense consistency** — Why: Matches verb tenses to the chronological time frames specified by prompts and data axes.
5. **5. Prepositional collocations** — Why: Pairs verbs and adjectives with their conventional prepositions (participate in, contribute to).
6. **6. Comma splices and boundaries** — Why: Avoids joining two independent clauses with only a comma without a conjunction.



#### The Five-Step Grammar Repair Sequence

1. **Step 1: Identify intended meaning** — Why: States precisely what relationship between ideas you intended to express.
2. **Step 2: Correct the original sentence** — Why: Rewrites the flawed sentence so that it communicates the message cleanly.
3. **Step 3: Articulate the grammatical rule** — Why: Writes down the exact structural principle governing the correction.
4. **Step 4: Generate a new sentence** — Why: Constructs a fresh sentence on a different topic using the identical grammatical pattern.
5. **Step 5: Verify on fresh work** — Why: Checks your next written or spoken practice for that specific error pattern.



#### Overloaded Pseudo-Complexity versus Controlled Precision

**Overloaded Clause Chain**

> "Due to the fact that globalization which is increasing rapidly happens, therefore many local cultures are losing their heritage because of economic reasons."

**Why it fails:** Uses redundant connectives ("due to the fact", "therefore") and clumsy relative clauses that obscure meaning.

**Controlled Complex Sentence**

> "As globalization accelerates, many regional communities struggle to preserve their cultural heritage against commercial pressures."

**Why it works:** Uses an introductory adverbial clause ("As globalization accelerates") to state cause and effect cleanly.

> **How to · Developing the Habit of Proofreading**
>
> Reserve three to five minutes at the end of every writing task specifically for sentence-level editing. Systematically check subject-verb agreement, noun countability, and article usage. Catching recurring slips protects your proportion of error-free sentences.

> **How to · Active versus Passive Voice Selection**
>
> Use active voice when the agent performing the action is significant. Use passive voice in process diagrams or scientific contexts where the process or result matters more than the actor.



#### Grammatical Control Review

- [ ] Every subject matches its corresponding verb in number and person.
- [ ] Uncountable nouns appear without plural endings or improper articles.
- [ ] Tenses reflect the actual timeframe indicated in the task.
- [ ] Sentences use subordination to show cause, condition, or contrast naturally.

## 16. 19 recurring grammar repairs and sentence editing

*Part 1.3 · Reference Language Banks · 10 min · Academic and General Training · Source sections: 10.8, 10.9*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-lang-grammar-repairs-editing)

High band performance in Grammatical Range and Accuracy requires eliminating frequent structural errors. This reference presents 19 recurrent grammar problems and an 8-step sentence-level editing procedure to audit your work.

**Why it matters:** Examiners look for a high proportion of completely error-free sentences for Band 7.0 and above. Eliminating recurring slips directly raises your score.

**What you will do:** Review the 19 high-frequency grammar repairs, master the 8-step sentence-editing sequence, and analyze an annotated revision specimen.



#### Nineteen Recurring Grammar Repairs and Controlled Forms

Inspect common learner mistakes alongside their controlled grammatical decisions.

| Problem | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| “More easier” | Use “easier” |
| “Most hard” when a superlative is intended | Use “hardest” or “most difficult,” according to the adjective |
| “Most of people” | Usually “most people”; use “most of the people” for a defined group |
| Missing article before a singular role | “I'm a teacher” |
| Missing article in a score phrase | “A Band 9 score” |
| Unclear family-member construction | “There are five people in my family” |
| Unnatural verb for completing the exam | “I took the IELTS test last year” is a standard option; “sat” also occurs in suitable usage |
| Finished event used for continuing residence | “I have lived here for three years” when the residence continues |
| Adjective used where an adverb is needed | “Acted rashly”; “rose gradually” |
| Noun phrase with a trend adjective | “A gradual rise,” not “a gradually rise” |
| Confused result preposition | “Rose to 30” gives the endpoint; “rose by 10” gives the change |
| Incorrect plural | “Phenomena” is the plural of “phenomenon” |
| Incorrect plural in a work schedule | “Two days off,” not “two day offs” |
| Missing plural or tense ending in speech | Check the actual recording; practise the ending in phrases |
| “Exceed of” | “Exceed the limit” |
| Missing collocation preposition | “Detrimental to”; “prone to”; “irrespective of”; “awareness of” |
| Independent sentences joined with a comma alone | Use a full stop, suitable conjunction, or another correct construction |
| Unclear pronoun | Repeat or specify the noun so the reference is unambiguous |
| Confused apostrophe | Distinguish a possessive form from a plural; check the actual intended owner |

> **How to · Learn the Structural Rule Behind Each Repair**
>
> Understand why each repair is required. Memorizing one corrected sentence does not fix every occurrence of the underlying pattern. Focus on the grammatical principle.



#### The Eight-Step Sentence-Level Editing Procedure

1. **1. State intended meaning plainly** — Why: Clarifies what relationship you want to convey.
2. **2. Identify subject and main verb** — Why: Confirms that the clause contains a complete core structure.
3. **3. Verify time and number** — Why: Checks tense consistency and subject-verb agreement.
4. **4. Check articles and noun forms** — Why: Validates countability, plurals, and definite articles.
5. **5. Audit prepositions and collocations** — Why: Verifies natural word combinations.
6. **6. Check clause connections** — Why: Ensures conjunctions connect clauses without comma splices.
7. **7. Remove redundant wording** — Why: Eliminates filler and wordiness that cloud meaning.
8. **8. Confirm no meaning was lost** — Why: Confirms that the edited sentence retains necessary precision.



#### Eight-Step Sentence Editing Walkthrough

*Original editing specimen · progressive repair from draft to controlled sentence*

**Flawed Draft (1)**

> Flawed draft: In recent years, more easier access to the internet have resulted in that most of people spends less time on reading books.

**Annotation:** Contains multiple comparative, agreement, preposition, and quantifier errors.

**Core Proposition (2)**

> Step 1 & 2: Identify core meaning: Increased internet access has reduced the time people spend reading books.

**Annotation:** Simplifies the core proposition into a single clear active clause.

**Agreement & Quantifiers (3)**

> Step 3 & 4: Correct agreement and quantifiers: "easier" replaces "more easier"; "has resulted" matches singular subject; "most people" replaces "most of people".

**Annotation:** Fixes double comparative, verb number, and non-specific quantifier.

**Gerund Pattern (4)**

> Step 5 & 6: Refine prepositions and clauses: "spending less time on reading" becomes "spending less time reading books".

**Annotation:** Applies natural verb-gerund pattern without redundant prepositions.

**Controlled Revision (5)**

> Final edited sentence: In recent years, easier access to digital media has led most people to spend less time reading books.

**Annotation:** Polished, completely error-free complex sentence preserving original scope.

> **How to · Systematic Three-Minute Proofreading Window**
>
> Dedicate the final three minutes of every writing session exclusively to the 8-step editing checklist. Scanning specifically for subject-verb agreement, noun plurals, and article omission catches four to five careless slips per essay.

> **Watch out · Preserve Necessary Precision**
>
> When editing a dense sentence, first simplify the proposition. Then restore any necessary condition, comparison, or qualification. Do not remove useful precision merely to make a sentence shorter.



#### Sentence Editing Checklist

- [ ] I check for double comparatives like "more easier" before submitting an essay.
- [ ] I know when to use "most people" versus "most of the people".
- [ ] I follow the 8-step procedure during my 3-minute writing review window.

## 17. Targets, evidence types, and error categories

*Part 1.4 · Diagnosis, Plan & Quality Control · 10 min · Academic and General Training · Source sections: 11.1, 11.2, 11.3, 11.4, 11.5*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-targets-evidence-cycle)

Setting an effective IELTS study target requires defining measurable behaviors, not merely a numerical band score. You must distinguish assisted learning evidence from independent performance evidence, and classify failures using practical error categories.

**Why it matters:** Confusing supported practice with independent exam readiness leads to false confidence and unexpected score drops on test day.

**What you will do:** Establish behavior-based targets, differentiate learning from performance evidence, and execute an 8-step diagnosis-and-repair cycle.



#### Learning Evidence versus Performance Evidence

| Evidence Type | Conditions & Support Level | What It Proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning evidence | Untimed; audio pausing allowed, reference dictionaries consulted, teacher feedback provided. | Demonstrates what you can understand and construct with assistance. |
| Performance evidence | Strictly timed; unfamiliar material, zero preview, unassisted execution under actual delivery format. | Demonstrates what you can reliably deliver under examination pressure. |
| Retention evidence | Completing a previously seen practice test or re-answering known items. | Demonstrates memory recall; does not prove ability to solve fresh tasks. |



#### The Eight-Step Diagnosis and Repair Cycle

1. **1. Attempt a relevant task** — Why: Generates observable baseline work under test conditions.
2. **2. Preserve original work** — Why: Ensures unedited attempts remain available for inspection.
3. **3. Identify exact failure** — Why: Pinpoints specific questions or paragraphs where marks were lost.
4. **4. Locate supporting evidence** — Why: Examines the audio script, text line, or official scoring rubric.
5. **5. Classify failed operation** — Why: Assigns the error to one of nine practical failure categories.
6. **6. Execute focused repair** — Why: Practices the smallest corrective drill targeted at that operation.
7. **7. Attempt fresh material** — Why: Tests the repair on an unseen task requiring the same skill.
8. **8. Verify transfer** — Why: Confirms the improvement functions without coaching or notes.

> **How to · The Smallest Supported Repair**
>
> Do not overhaul your entire study plan after one low score. Diagnose the specific operational fault and apply the smallest targeted drill supported by the evidence.



#### Nine Practical Error Categories and Targeted Repairs

| Category | What Failed | Targeted Actionable Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instructions | Response violated task conditions or word counts. | Decode limits and required answer form before reading or writing. |
| Language recognition | A word, sound, or sentence relation was missed. | Work with short contextual audio or text and verify meaning. |
| Location or tracking | The relevant passage section or audio speaker was lost. | Practice tracking text anchors, maps, sequences, or speakers. |
| Decision rule | Evidence was located but classified incorrectly. | Practice specific task decision logic with written explanations. |
| Content development | A point lacked explanation, mechanism, or evidence. | Plan and fully explain one main point before adding another. |
| Organization | Ideas existed but progression or relationships were unclear. | Assign each paragraph one clear functional role. |
| Language production | Intended meaning was clear but grammar or vocabulary failed. | Correct the recurring error pattern and apply on fresh sentences. |
| Answer entry | Spelling, number, label, or transfer format was wrong. | Add a dedicated checking routine during transfer periods. |
| Attention or pacing | Focus or available time expired at a specific point. | Practice section pacing and calm recovery drills. |



#### Vague Blame versus Concrete Error Record

**Vague Reaction**

> "My Listening concentration was terrible today. I missed several numbers in Section 1."

**Why it fails:** Blames general concentration without identifying what cognitive cue was missed.

**Actionable Diagnostic Entry**

> "I heard the initial appointment time (2:30) but missed the correction after the pivot word "actually" (3:15). Repair: track replacement information, not merely the first number mentioned."

**Why it works:** Identifies the precise distractor mechanism and defines a clear rule for future tasks.

**Reflection / workbook prompt:** Behavioural Study Target: State one specific operational target and how you will verify it on fresh unassisted material.

- [ ] My study targets include observable behaviors as well as band scores.
- [ ] I separate assisted learning scores from independent performance scores.
- [ ] I classify recurring errors using the nine practical categories.

## 18. Diagnostic patterns, effort balance, and input-to-output

*Part 1.4 · Diagnosis, Plan & Quality Control · 10 min · Academic and General Training · Source sections: 11.6, 11.7, 11.8, 11.9, 11.10*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-patterns-practice-input)

Diagnosing test performance requires recognizing skill-specific patterns without turning them into rigid rules. Balance focused micro-drills with complete mock tests, allocate effort based on demonstrated gaps, and bridge input to output systematically.

**Why it matters:** Spending equal time on all four skills when Writing is Band 5.5 and Reading is Band 8.0 wastes valuable study hours. Allocating effort to your weakest skill produces the fastest overall band improvement.

**What you will do:** Analyze seven diagnostic error patterns, balance micro-practice with full tests, and link sustainable input with active output.



#### Seven Cross-Skill Diagnostic Patterns and Their True Causes

| Skill & Observed Pattern | Common False Assumption | Actual Operational Diagnosis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listening: errors in discussions | Needs more general podcasts | Speaker confusion or inability to process options while tracking audio. |
| Reading: one question type repeatedly wrong | Candidate is bad at Reading | Method for that specific family (such as headings or True/False/Not Given) is misunderstood. |
| Reading: accurate untimed, incomplete timed | Needs to read faster | Spends excessive time searching blindly or continually changing answers. |
| Writing: strong vocabulary, weak score | Needs more advanced C2 words | Fails task coverage or prompt analysis; ideas are superficial. |
| Writing: relevant points, low band | Needs more ideas | Points lack causal mechanisms or supporting examples. |
| Speaking: fluent on familiar, halts on unfamiliar | Candidate lacks personality | Vocabulary retrieval friction or lack of content-planning routines. |
| Speaking: rapid speech with unclear endings | Needs to talk faster | Phonological slip: missing word endings and blurred syllable stress. |

> **Watch out · Avoid Subjective Diagnostic Stereotypes**
>
> A learner's nationality, native language, profession, or personality is not a diagnostic explanation. Always inspect the objective evidence in the candidate's actual recording or essay.



#### Focused Practice versus Complete Performance

| Dimension | Focused Practice (Micro-Drills) | Complete Performance (Full Simulation) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | Isolates one specific operation (such as writing a Task 1 overview or tracking map directions). | Integrates all operations across a complete section or full examination. |
| Feedback speed | Immediate review after completing 3 to 5 items. | Reviewed after completing a full 40-item paper or 60-minute task. |

1. **1. Read or listen for core meaning** — Why: Ensures complete comprehension of authentic English input.
2. **2. Notice a useful combination** — Why: Identifies natural collocations and structural transitions in context.
3. **3. Verify meaning and pattern** — Why: Confirms definition and grammar in a reliable learner dictionary.
4. **4. Summarize in your own words** — Why: Forces active processing without copying original phrases.
5. **5. Discuss or write on related topic** — Why: Transfers the newly acquired language into productive output.
6. **6. Check accuracy** — Why: Audits grammar, prepositions, and spelling in your response.

> **How to · Four Directions of Vocabulary Retrieval**
>
> Review vocabulary across multiple pathways: word to meaning, meaning to word, word to original phrase, and phrase to connected spoken or written response. Group items by topic or grammar rather than random alphabetical lists.

- [ ] I combine focused micro-drills with complete timed practice simulations.

## 20. Retain the method, reject the false rule

*Part 1.4 · Diagnosis, Plan & Quality Control · 12 min · Academic and General Training · Source sections: 12.1, 12.2, 12.3, A.1, A.2, A.3, A.4*

[Open this lesson in Artico](https://thisizkavi-lab.github.io/artico-web/#/application/tests/ielts/overview/study?unit=ov-claims-checklists-sources)

IELTS preparation is clouded by rigid internet myths and false rules. High scores come from applying core communicative principles, using evidence-based methods, and rejecting arbitrary dogmas that restrict your natural language.

**Why it matters:** Believing false rules like "never repeat a word" or "every essay must have four paragraphs" distorts your writing and creates artificial errors under examination conditions.

**What you will do:** Review eight core preparation principles, consult the complete 29-claim myth buster, audit final skill checklists, and explore official references.



#### Eight Core Principles Across the Entire Course

1. **1. Understand the exact task** — Why: Decodes word limits, question anatomy, and prompt conditions before acting.
2. **2. Base decisions on evidence** — Why: Locates exact passage lines, audio cues, or data points before choosing an answer.
3. **3. Develop ideas with clear support** — Why: Explains causal mechanisms and concrete reasons rather than listing points.
4. **4. Choose language for meaning and context** — Why: Prioritizes precision, collocations, and natural register over obscure synonyms.
5. **5. Build grammatical range with control** — Why: Balances simple, compound, and complex sentences without error-filled convolution.
6. **6. Practice the failed operation directly** — Why: Isolates the exact micro-skill that caused marks to be lost.
7. **7. Verify improvements on fresh material** — Why: Ensures learned skills transfer to unseen tests under timed conditions.
8. **8. Follow current official delivery instructions** — Why: Aligns preparation with modern computer and Writing on Paper formats.

> **Tip · Option: Coaching Choice**

> **Watch out · Trap: A Method Is Not a Band Guarantee**
>
> A method is useful only when it helps you produce an accurate, well-supported response. No examination strategy can replace active grammatical control and precise vocabulary.
