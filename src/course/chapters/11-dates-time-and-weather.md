# CH11 — Dates, Time, and Weather

**Your goal:** Read and say a date or time clearly, describe how often something happens, and recognize a useful set of weather words.  

This is a foundation chapter. Choose the words your next task needs; you do not have to memorize every item before continuing.

## Study: Calendar words

A **day** is one calendar day; a **week** has seven days. A **weekday** usually means Monday through Friday, while a **weekend** usually refers to Saturday and Sunday. Someone's working days can be different. A **month** is one of the twelve named parts of a year. A **date** identifies a calendar day.

**Days:** Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday.  

**Months:** January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December.  

**Yesterday** is the day before today; **tomorrow** is the day after. Their calendar dates change with the day you are speaking. A written message with a date can be clearer when someone may read it later.

### Write a date; say a date

This course uses **April 7, 2027** in its full written examples. You can say **April seventh, twenty twenty-seven**. Another possible spoken form is **the seventh of April**.

| Written day | Spoken form | Written day | Spoken form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | first | 8th | eighth |
| 2nd | second | 9th | ninth |
| 3rd | third | 12th | twelfth |
| 4th | fourth | 20th | twentieth |
| 5th | fifth | 21st | twenty-first |
| 6th | sixth | 22nd | twenty-second |
| 7th | seventh | 31st | thirty-first |

On a form labeled **MM/DD/YYYY**, **04/07/2027** means April 7, 2027. **MM** identifies the month, **DD** the day, and **YYYY** the four-digit year. Do not assume every form or country uses that order. If a bare **04/07** is unclear, ask **Do you mean April seventh or July fourth?** Writing the month in words removes that particular ambiguity.

Years can be spoken in different ways. Useful examples are **1900 — nineteen hundred**, **1901 — nineteen oh one**, **2000 — two thousand**, **2001 — two thousand one**, and **2033 — twenty thirty-three**. These are choices, not a rule rejecting every alternative.

## Study: Time and duration

| Written time | A clear way to say it |
| --- | --- |
| 1:00 | one o'clock |
| 1:05 | one oh five |
| 1:15 | one fifteen / a quarter after one |
| 1:30 | one thirty / half past one |
| 1:45 | one forty-five / a quarter to two |
| 2:00 | two o'clock |

**A quarter** is fifteen minutes here. **Half past one** means thirty minutes after one. If someone says a shortened or unfamiliar time expression, confirm the number rather than guess.

**6 a.m.** is six in the morning; **6 p.m.** is six in the evening. For twelve o'clock, **noon** and **midnight** are useful clear words. With midnight, confirm which night or date is intended when it matters.

**Clock time:** **The class starts at six p.m.**  
**Duration:** **The class lasts an hour.**  

A **minute** has sixty seconds; an **hour** has sixty minutes. **Half an hour** is thirty minutes. **At six** identifies a time; **for an hour** describes a duration; **by six** can set a deadline no later than six.

### How often?

- **Once a week:** one time in a week.
- **Twice a week:** two times in a week.
- **Three times a month:** three occasions in a month.
- **Every day / every week / every month:** a repeated interval.

**How often do you practice? — Twice a week.**

## Study: Weather vocabulary

| Word | Short meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **sunny / cloudy / overcast** | Sun is visible / there are clouds / the sky is covered by clouds. |
| **warm / chilly / freezing** | Fairly hot / rather cold / extremely cold in a conversational description. |
| **windy / foggy / snowy** | With wind / with fog that limits visibility / with snow. |
| **icy** | With ice, such as an icy road. |
| **pouring** | Raining heavily. |

**It's freezing** can describe how cold someone feels; it does not always report an exact freezing-point measurement. Temperatures need a scale: **degrees Fahrenheit (°F)** or **degrees Celsius (°C)**. Ask **Is that Fahrenheit or Celsius?** if the scale is not clear.

Words you may encounter in weather reports include **storm**, **flood** (water covering normally dry land), **heat wave** (a period of unusually hot weather), **drought** (a prolonged shortage of water or rainfall), **hurricane** (a powerful rotating tropical storm system), and **blizzard** (strong winds with falling or blowing snow and very poor visibility). Chapter 13 develops weather conversation and forecast meaning.

## Practice: Read, say, and clarify

Use this fictional notice: **Art class: April 7, 2027, at 6:15 p.m. Duration: 90 minutes. Frequency: twice a week. Form date format: MM/DD/YYYY. Weather note: cloudy, 58°F.**

1. Say the date and start time aloud. Then write the date in the form's numeric format.
2. Answer **How long is the class?** and **How often does it meet?**
3. Someone says **It starts at six fifteen in the morning**. Correct the part that conflicts with the notice.
4. A message gives **04/07** without a format. Ask a question that could resolve the ambiguity.
5. Describe the supplied weather without inventing rain or snow. Identify the temperature scale.
6. Hide the notice. Create a short notice for an invented class, including a spelled-out date, a.m. or p.m., duration, and frequency. Read it back as someone checking the details.

### Sample possibilities

1. **April seventh, twenty twenty-seven, at six fifteen p.m.** Numeric date: **04/07/2027**.
2. **An hour and a half. Twice a week.** Ninety minutes is one hour plus thirty minutes.
3. **It's six fifteen in the evening, not the morning.**
4. **Do you mean April seventh or July fourth?**
5. **It's cloudy and fifty-eight degrees Fahrenheit.**

**Check:** Can someone identify the intended date and part of the day? Did you distinguish start time, duration, and frequency? Could you request clarification when the notation was unclear?
