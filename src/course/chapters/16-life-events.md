# CH16 — Life Events

**Your goal:** Recognize the news someone shares and respond with celebration, a good wish, acknowledgment or sympathy.  

## Study: Match the response to the news

Some events are occasions for celebration. Others are difficult, mixed, or private. The speaker's own account helps you choose a response rather than assume how they feel.

| News or occasion | Possible response |
| --- | --- |
| A birthday | **Happy birthday!** |
| An anniversary | **Happy anniversary!** |
| A graduation or promotion shared happily | **Congratulations! / Congrats!** |
| A new baby shared happily | **Congratulations on your new baby.** |
| A new home | **Congratulations on the move. How are you settling in?** |
| Retirement | **Wishing you all the best in your retirement.** |
| A death or bereavement | **I'm so sorry for your loss.** |

**Congrats** is a shortened informal form of **congratulations**. **Congratulations on...** names the achievement or event: **on your promotion**, **on graduating**, **on your new home**.

## Study: Celebrate without taking over

**Good news at work**

**Tess:** I got the promotion!  
**Omar:** Congratulations! That's great news.  
**Tess:** Thanks. I'm excited, but there's a lot to learn.  
**Omar:** I hope the first week goes well.  

Tess has mixed feelings even while sharing good news. Omar responds to both the achievement and the new challenge. **I'm proud of you** expresses personal pride; **You worked hard for this** recognizes effort when you know that is true.

**A birthday exchange**

**Omar:** Happy birthday! Any plans for today?  
**Tess:** Thanks. I'm having dinner with a couple of friends.  
**Omar:** That sounds nice. Have a great evening.  

The follow-up gives Tess room to say how she will celebrate. You do not need to ask someone's age or list the gifts they received.

## Study: Milestones and good wishes

**At a new home**

**Tess:** Welcome! We're still unpacking.  
**Omar:** It's good to see your new place. How are you settling in?  
**Tess:** Pretty well. The neighbors have been friendly.  
**Omar:** I'm glad to hear that.  

**Settling in** means becoming familiar and comfortable in a new situation. A new home may be owned, rented or shared; the greeting does not need an ownership assumption.

For a wedding, **Congratulations to you both** addresses the couple. A short **Here's to Alex and River!** can begin a toast—a spoken good wish to people gathered for the occasion. A toast does not require this chapter to specify a particular drink.

**A colleague's last day before retirement**

**Omar:** Wishing you all the best in your retirement.  
**Tess:** Thank you. I'll miss working with everyone.  
**Omar:** We'll miss you too. I hope we stay in touch.  

A good wish can acknowledge change without pretending the person has only one feeling.

## Study: Difficult or uncertain news

Sympathy uses **sorry** differently from an apology, as Chapter 9 explained.

**Tess:** My aunt died last weekend.  
**Omar:** I'm so sorry to hear that.  
**Tess:** Thank you. I don't really want to talk about it yet.  
**Omar:** Of course. We don't have to talk about it.  

For news such as a divorce or leaving a job, start with **How are you feeling about it?** if the feeling is unclear. If someone says **I'm relieved**, **I'm glad things are getting better for you** may fit. If they say **It's been really hard**, acknowledgment and support may fit better than congratulations. Chapter 19 develops offers of help.

## Practice: Respond to the person's account

Read each complete situation. On your own, say the news, your response, and the person's reply.

1. **I passed my course! I'm so happy.** Celebrate. The speaker replies **Thanks. It was a lot of work**. Acknowledge the effort.
2. It is your friend's birthday. Wish them well and ask about plans. Their plan is dinner with friends.
3. A colleague says **I'm retiring next month. I'm excited, but I'll miss the team**. Give a good wish and acknowledge the second feeling.
4. **My grandfather died. I don't want to discuss it right now.** Express sympathy and respect the stated preference.
5. **The divorce is final.** No feeling is stated. Ask how they feel. Rehearse two continuations: **I'm relieved** and **I'm having a hard time**. Support: **I'm glad things are getting better**, **I'm sorry it's been so hard**.
6. Hide Study. Choose a life event and give a response that fits an invented speaker's feelings, rather than only the event label.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Congratulations! — Thanks. It was a lot of work. — You worked hard for this.**
2. **Happy birthday! Any plans for today? — Dinner with friends. — Have a great evening.**
3. **Wishing you all the best. We'll miss you too.**
4. **I'm so sorry for your loss. We don't have to talk about it.**
5. **How are you feeling about it?** followed by the fitting acknowledgment above.

**Check:** Did your response fit the actual news and feeling? Did you avoid turning sympathy into an apology? Could you acknowledge both celebration and uncertainty without demanding private details?
