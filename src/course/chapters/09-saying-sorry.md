# CH09 — Saying Sorry

**Your goal:** Acknowledge your own mistake, respond to an apology, and distinguish an apology from sympathy or a request for repetition.  

## Study: Say what happened

**Sorry** can acknowledge a small accident. **I'm sorry** can begin a fuller apology. Naming the action helps the other person know what you are taking responsibility for.

- **Sorry I stepped on your foot.** Acknowledge the accident.
- **I'm sorry I kept you waiting.** Acknowledge the delay.
- **That was my mistake.** Take responsibility.
- **I should have told you sooner.** Recognize what you failed to do.
- **I'll send the correct file now.** Offer a concrete next step.

**A missed message**

**Cam:** You said you'd send me the address. I waited outside.  
**Jules:** I'm sorry. I forgot to send it. That was my mistake.  
**Cam:** Thanks for saying that. Please send it before I leave next time.  
**Jules:** I will. Here's the address now.  

The explanation identifies the problem; it does not erase the effect. A next step is useful when there is something to fix. You do not have to make a promise you cannot keep.

## Study: Informal responsibility and stronger apologies

**My bad** is an informal acknowledgment that a mistake was yours. **Oops, sorry** can respond to an immediate small accident.

**Jules:** You put the wrong time in the message.  
**Cam:** My bad. It should say seven, not six. I'll correct it.  

Here the informal wording accompanies a specific correction. If the other person is seriously hurt or the problem remains unresolved, a brief **My bad** alone may not communicate the attention you intend. You can use **I owe you an apology** or **I'm really sorry I did that** and explain the action.

Avoid relying only on **I'm sorry you feel that way** when you mean to take responsibility for something you did. That wording focuses on their feeling rather than naming your action. Compare **I'm sorry I shared your message without asking**.

## Study: Receive an apology

| Response | What you can communicate |
| --- | --- |
| **It's okay. / No problem.** | The incident is not a problem for you, or you are reassuring them. |
| **Thanks for apologizing.** | You appreciate the acknowledgment. |
| **I appreciate it, but I'm still upset.** | You accept the words while describing a remaining feeling. |
| **Please ask me next time.** | You state what you want in future. |
| **I need a little time.** | You are not ready to continue immediately. |

You do not have to say **It's okay** if it is not okay for you. **No big deal** presents the incident as relatively unimportant; choose it when that is what you mean.

**Cam:** Sorry I borrowed your charger without asking.  
**Jules:** Thanks for apologizing. Please ask me next time.  
**Cam:** Okay. I will.  

## Study: Sympathy is a different use

**I'm sorry to hear that** can express concern about someone else's bad news. It need not mean you caused the event.

**Jules:** My grandfather died last week.  
**Cam:** I'm so sorry for your loss.  
**Jules:** Thank you. I appreciate that.  

For other difficult news, **I'm sorry you're having such a hard time** offers acknowledgment. Chapter 19 develops support. **Sorry?** can also ask for repetition, as in Chapter 4; that is another function of the same word.

## Practice: Match the function

Speak both roles when practicing alone. First use Study; then cover it and change the event or response.

1. You accidentally bump into a friend. Give a short apology. They are not bothered. Supply their reassuring reply.
2. You sent the wrong meeting time: six instead of seven. Acknowledge your mistake and correct the information.
3. Someone borrowed your headphones without asking. They apologize. Your role wants them to ask next time and does not want to say everything is fine. Respond.
4. A friend says **My dog died yesterday**. Express sympathy. The friend answers **Thank you**. Do not speak as though you caused the death.
5. A person speaks too quietly. Use **Sorry?** or a fuller Chapter 4 repair phrase. Explain why this is not an apology for wrongdoing.
6. Hide Study. Rehearse a four-turn exchange about a forgotten message, using a concrete next step and a response that fits the recipient's feelings.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Oops, sorry! — No problem.**
2. **My bad. It should say seven. I'll correct the message.**
3. **Thanks for apologizing. Please ask me next time.**
4. **I'm so sorry to hear that.**
5. **Sorry, I didn't catch that. Could you say it again?** You are requesting information.

**Check:** Was it clear whether you were apologizing, showing sympathy or asking for repetition? Did an apology identify the action? Did the recipient have room to state an honest response?
