# CH42 — Sending and Receiving

**Outcome:** Ask about mailing or collecting an item, clarify delivery options, and report a delivery problem.  

## Study

A **letter** can be placed in an **envelope**. A **package** contains an item being sent. A **shipping label** gives delivery information. **Postage** is the charge for mailing. **Tracking** provides shipment-status information; a **tracking number** identifies the shipment for that purpose.

### At the post office

For this fictional shipping quote, both options include tracking. Basic costs $12 with estimated Friday delivery; Faster costs $19 with estimated Thursday delivery. Neither date is guaranteed in this quote.

**Customer:** I'd like to mail this package to Denver.  
**Clerk:** How soon does it need to arrive?  
**Customer:** By Friday, if possible. What are the options?  
**Clerk:** Basic is twelve dollars, with estimated delivery Friday. Faster is nineteen dollars, with estimated delivery Thursday.  
**Customer:** Does the quoted price include tracking?  
**Clerk:** Yes, both options include tracking.  
**Customer:** Is either delivery date guaranteed?  
**Clerk:** No. Those are estimated dates.  
**Customer:** Okay. I'd prefer the faster option, but I understand Thursday is an estimate.  

An **estimate** is an expected result; a **guarantee** is a specific promise with stated conditions. Ask which applies rather than assuming every quoted delivery date has the same meaning. USPS uses service names including **First-Class Mail** and **Priority Mail**; their names do not tell you all the current eligibility, price or delivery conditions.

For a simple purchase, say **I'd like some stamps, please**. For international mail: **How much would it cost to send this to Japan?** Follow-up questions include **What information do you need from me?** and **Are there any forms I need to complete?**

### Collecting or receiving an item

**Customer:** I'm here to pick up a package. I received this notice.  
**Clerk:** May I see the notice?  
**Customer:** Here it is. What identification do you need?  
**Clerk:** I'll explain the requirements for this pickup.  

A **delivery notice** tells you something about an attempted or available delivery. **ID** means identification. The exact pickup requirements belong to the service you are using.

**Driver:** Delivery for Alex Reed?  
**Resident:** Yes, that's me.  
**Driver:** Could you sign here?  
**Resident:** Sure. Is this the only package today?  
**Driver:** Yes, just this one.  

If it is not for you: **That person doesn't live here** or **I think you have the wrong address** explains the issue. **This is apartment four, not fourteen** clarifies an address mismatch.

### Arranging a courier and reporting problems

**Do you offer pickup from this address?** asks whether collection is available. **How soon could it be delivered?** asks about timing. **What is included in the quoted price?** asks about scope.

For a missing or delayed item: **The tracking says delivered, but I haven't received it** states two facts without claiming you know where the package is. **The box arrived damaged** describes its condition. A useful next question is **How can I request a review?**

**Customer:** My package hasn't arrived. The tracking hasn't changed since Monday.  
**Agent:** Could you give me the tracking number?  
**Customer:** It's the number on this receipt. What happens next?  
**Agent:** I can check the shipment status and explain the next steps.  

## Practice

Alone, read the clerk or agent's question, answer aloud, and then rehearse both roles. These are practice scenarios; no real shipment is required.

1. You want a package delivered by Friday. Ask about the two quote options, give their price/tracking/date replies, and clarify whether either date is guaranteed. Choose an option or explain why you need to ask about another service.
2. You have a pickup notice but do not know the identification requirements. Explain why you came and ask what you need.
3. A driver arrives at apartment 14, but you live in apartment 4 and the addressee is unfamiliar. Clarify the address.
4. Tracking shows **delivered**, but you have not received the package. State the discrepancy and ask how to request a review.

**Sample possibilities:** “What are my options for delivery by Friday? Is that date guaranteed?” — “Basic is twelve dollars; Faster is nineteen. Both include tracking, but neither date is guaranteed.” / “I'm here to pick up a package. What ID do you need?” / “This is apartment four. I think you have the wrong address.” / “It says delivered, but I haven't received it. How can I request a review?”  

Try again with a new destination or problem. Check that you stated what was sent or received and asked about the information you still needed.
