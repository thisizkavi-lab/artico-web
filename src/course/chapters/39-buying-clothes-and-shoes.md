# CH39 — Buying Clothes and Shoes

**Outcome:** Ask about an item, size and availability; describe fit; and decide whether to buy.  

## Study

Useful clothing names include **shirt**, **T-shirt**, **jacket**, **coat**, **dress**, **skirt**, **shorts**, **jeans**, **pants**, **sweater**, and **suit**. Pants cover the lower body with separate sections for the legs; you may also encounter **trousers**. Footwear includes **sneakers**, **boots**, **sandals**, and **dress shoes**.

Point to a specific item or name it: **Do you have this shirt in blue?** and **Do you have these shoes in a size nine?** ask for another version. **This** fits one shirt; **these** fits a pair of shoes or pants.

### Trying an item

**Customer:** Do you have this jacket in a medium?  
**Employee:** We do. Would you like to try it on?  
**Customer:** Yes, please. Where are the fitting rooms?  
**Employee:** Just over there.  
**Customer:** Thanks. The sleeves feel a little short. Do you have a large?  
**Employee:** I'll check. Would you like the same color?  
**Customer:** Yes, the dark green.  

**Try it on** means put it on to see how it looks or fits. **A size up** means the next larger size; **a size down** means the next smaller size in the store's labeling system. Give the actual size label when possible rather than assuming every item uses the same fit or numbering.

### Describing fit

- **It's too tight around the shoulders.**
- **The pants are too loose at the waist.**
- **The sleeves are too long.**
- **These shoes feel narrow.**
- **They fit, but I don't like the color.**
- **This one fits better.**

**Too** identifies a problem for your purpose, not simply a noticeable feature. **A little loose** may be a description; **too loose** means you want a different fit.

**Customer:** Could I try these in a nine?  
**Employee:** Sure. How do they feel?  
**Customer:** They're tight at the front. Do you have a nine and a half?  
**Employee:** Not in this color, but we have black.  
**Customer:** I'd rather have blue. Could you check whether another store has them?  
**Employee:** I can ask.  
**Customer:** Thanks. If not, I'll keep looking.  

### Availability and the decision

**Your website shows it in stock** describes information you saw. Follow with **Could you check?**, since the employee may need to verify it. **Is this on sale?** asks about the current price. A request such as **What is your return policy?** belongs before a purchase when you need that information; Chapter 40 practices returns in more detail.

You can accept: **I'll take this one.** Decline: **It doesn't fit the way I want.** Or delay: **I'm going to think about it. Thanks for your help.** You do not owe the employee a positive opinion about every item.

## Practice

Use these fictional stock facts: green jacket—medium and large; blue sneakers—size 9 only; black sneakers—9 and 9½. Read both roles alone or switch with a partner.

1. Request the medium jacket, then explain that the sleeves are too short and ask for a larger size.
2. Request blue sneakers in 9½. The employee offers black. Accept the alternative or explain that you prefer blue.
3. Ask about the return policy before deciding. The employee says **I'll show you our written policy.** Ask where to find it or thank them.

**Sample possibilities:** “Do you have this in a medium?” — “Yes.” — “The sleeves are too short. Could I try a large?” / “I'd prefer blue. I'll keep looking, thanks.” / “What's your return policy?” — “I'll show you.” — “Great, thank you.”  

Try again later with a different item and fit problem. Check that you named the item, requested a usable size or alternative, and made your decision clear.
