# CH47 — College and Further Study

**Outcome:** Compare study options, ask about a program, navigate the first days, and seek practical or social support.  

## Study

**College** and **university** can name places of higher education; use the institution's own name. A **program** is an organized course of study. An **adviser** or **advisor** helps with educational choices; a **department** organizes a subject area. Useful subject names include **engineering**, **business**, **biology**, **chemistry**, **law**, **design**, **economics**, and **social sciences**.

### Compare programs

Use this fictional Harbor College information:

| Program | Format | Additional facts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design certificate | six months; Tuesday evenings | classes start September 9; information session August 12 |
| Engineering degree | four-year program; daytime classes | admissions office explains entry requirements and costs |
| Introductory photography course | eight Saturdays | no qualification awarded in this practice course |

These are invented course conditions. A **certificate** and a **degree** are different named outcomes; the institution should explain what each qualification represents. A short course may develop a skill without awarding either.

**Prospective student:** I'm interested in the design certificate. How long does it take?  
**Adviser:** Six months. Classes meet on Tuesday evenings.  
**Student:** Does it include practical work?  
**Adviser:** Yes, students create projects.  
**Student:** What are the entry requirements, and what is the total cost?  
**Adviser:** The admissions office can explain those details.  
**Student:** Is there an information session?  
**Adviser:** August twelfth. You can ask your remaining questions there.  

**Entry requirements** are conditions for entering a program. **Tuition** concerns instruction charges; **fees** may refer to other listed charges. **What is the total cost?** asks for the full relevant amount rather than assuming one advertised figure includes everything.

Other questions are **How do I apply?**, **Can I study part-time?**, **Is there a work placement?**, and **What qualification will I receive?** A **work placement** is an organized period of practical work; ask how a particular program uses that term.

### Find your way and meet people

**Student:** Excuse me, where is the biology department?  
**Staff member:** In West Hall.  
**Student:** I'm new here. Could you tell me how to get there?  
**Staff member:** Use the campus directory at the entrance; I can show you the building.  
**Student:** Thank you.  

You can use **I'm here for the business class** to identify a class, or **Is this the room for the lecture?** to check before entering. No unseen map is required to practice these questions.

**A:** Hi, I'm Noor. Are you in the design program?  
**B:** Yeah. I'm Luis. How are you finding it?  
**A:** I like the projects. There's a lot to learn, though.  
**B:** Same here. Want to get coffee after class?  
**A:** I'd like that. What time are you free?  

**How are you finding it?** asks about your experience. **There's a lot to learn** can express challenge without saying the program is a bad choice. The coffee invitation uses Chapter 7's invitation skill; a confirmed time uses Chapter 12.

### Ask for help early enough to discuss the issue

**Student:** I'm enjoying the classes, but I'm having trouble keeping up.  
**Adviser:** Which part is difficult?  
**Student:** Finishing the weekly assignments. Who can I speak to about study support?  
**Adviser:** We can look at the support options for your program.  
**Student:** I also need information about financial help. Where should I ask?  

Useful concerns are **I'm thinking about changing programs**, **I'm having trouble making friends**, **I'm feeling homesick**, and **I need advice about housing**. **Homesick** means missing home and familiar people or places. Saying **Who can I talk to?** asks for a route to help rather than requiring you to solve the problem in English first.

### Clubs and activities

**Student:** Is anyone going to the club fair?  
**Classmate:** I am. I want to check out the photography club. Want to come?  
**Student:** Maybe. Do beginners join?  
**Classmate:** We can ask at their table.  
**Student:** Okay, let's do that.  

**I'm in** accepts an activity. **It's not for me, but thanks** declines it. **I'd like to know more first** postpones a decision without pretending to be enthusiastic.

## Practice

Speak both roles alone, using the information table and supplied adviser responses.

1. Compare the certificate and photography course. Ask about duration and whether a qualification is awarded. Choose the option suited to an evening-study role and explain your choice.
2. You are attending an information session. Ask about entry requirements, total cost and how to apply. If the answer is **The admissions team can explain that**, ask how to contact them.
3. Meet a classmate. Introduce yourself, discuss one enjoyable or challenging part of study, and accept or decline coffee.
4. Your assignments are taking too long. Explain the difficulty and ask about study support. Add a financial-help or housing question if you want another branch.

**Sample possibilities:** “The certificate meets on Tuesday evenings, so it fits my schedule. What qualification does it award?” / “How can I contact admissions?” / “I like the projects, but there's a lot to learn. I'd like coffee. What time?” / “I'm having trouble finishing the assignments. Who can I talk to about study support?”  

Return later with another study goal. Check that you could compare options, ask for missing facts and seek the appropriate help without assuming every college follows the practice conditions.
