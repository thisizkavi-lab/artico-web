# CH62 — Pets

**Outcome:** Ask about adopting an animal, describe a pet's observed problem, and ask for clear care information.  

## Study

A **pet** is an animal kept as a companion. **Adopt** means take an animal into your care through an adoption arrangement. A **shelter** or **rescue organization** helps animals find homes. A **breed** is a type within a species, such as a particular type of dog; it is not a description of everything an individual animal will do.

### Discuss a possible adoption

Start with your situation: **I'd like to adopt a cat**, **We already have another dog**, **There are children in our household**, or **I'm away from home during the day**. Ask what is known about the particular animal.

The fictional rescue has a cat named Luna. Staff have observed Luna living calmly with one other adult cat. They have no information about her experience with children. The visitor works from home and already has an adult cat.

**Visitor:** I'd like to ask about Luna. We already have an adult cat at home.  
**Staff:** Luna has lived calmly with another adult cat here.  
**Visitor:** What else do you know about how she gets along with other animals?  
**Staff:** That's the situation we've observed. We'd need to discuss your cat and household too.  
**Visitor:** Do you know how she responds to children?  
**Staff:** We don't have that information.  
**Visitor:** Okay. I work from home. Could you explain the adoption process and the support you provide?  

**Gets along with** means has a reasonably friendly or peaceful relationship with someone or another animal. An observed example supplies information; it does not guarantee behavior in every future home. **What have you observed?** is more specific than asking only **Is she friendly?**

### Describe a problem to a veterinary clinic

A **vet**, or **veterinarian**, provides medical care for animals. Useful observations include **She's eating less than usual**, **He's limping**, **My cat keeps vomiting**, **My rabbit is losing fur**, and **My puppy is much quieter than usual**. **Paw** means an animal's foot. **Limping** means walking unevenly because a leg or foot is difficult to use. Report what you see and when it changed rather than supplying a diagnosis you do not know.

**Caller:** I'd like to speak to someone about my dog. He's limping and seems to avoid putting weight on his left front paw.  
**Clinic staff:** When did you first notice it?  
**Caller:** This morning. I don't know what caused it.  
**Staff:** Has anything else changed?  
**Caller:** He's eating less than usual. Could you ask the vet how urgently he needs to be seen?  
**Staff:** I'll pass those details to the vet and ask them to speak with you.  
**Caller:** Thank you. My number is on his record. Could you confirm you have it?  

If the cause is uncertain, **I think he hurt his paw, but I'm not sure** distinguishes a guess from an observation. **I don't know** is useful information. **Could you explain what that test is for?** asks about a suggested test; **Does he need an X-ray?** asks a question rather than announcing the decision.

### Ask about care and records

Useful care terms are **vaccination**, giving a vaccine; **flea treatment**, treatment intended to address fleas; **eye drops**, liquid medicine put into the eyes as directed; and **grooming**, caring for an animal's coat and related cleanliness. Ask a veterinary professional which care applies to the particular animal and how to follow the directions.

A **microchip** is a small identification device with an identifying number. It is not a GPS tracker. A **vaccination record** documents vaccinations. **Pet passport** is the name of a travel document used in some systems; travel documents and entry requirements depend on the trip.

**Owner:** Could you go over Luna's care and vaccination records with me?  
**Staff:** Yes. Which part would you like to discuss first?  
**Owner:** This treatment entry. What is it for, and who should I ask about the next steps?  
**Staff:** Let's check the record together and identify what the veterinary clinic needs to explain.  
**Owner:** I'd also like to check the microchip information. Who can confirm the identification and registration details?  

For travel, ask **Which documents would this particular trip require, and where can I confirm the current requirements?** Do not treat one animal's care record as every animal's checklist.

## Practice

Alone, read each staff prompt and answer from the supplied facts, then speak both roles.

1. You work from home and have an adult cat. Ask about Luna's observed behavior and adoption support. Reply as staff using only the rescue's stated information.
2. Your dog began limping this morning, avoids putting weight on the left front paw, and is eating less. Answer **When did you notice it?** and **Has anything else changed?** Ask about the urgency of veterinary assessment.
3. You do not understand a treatment entry. Ask its purpose and who can explain the directions. Ask separately about microchip identification.

**Sample possibilities:** “We already have an adult cat. What have you observed about Luna with other cats?” / “We don't have information about her experience with children.” / “I noticed the limp this morning. He's also eating less. Could you ask the vet how urgently he needs to be seen?” / “What is this treatment for?”  

Later, change the problem to a cat vomiting since last night. State the animal, observation and starting time without diagnosing it. Check whether you kept observed facts, uncertainty and requests for professional help distinct.
