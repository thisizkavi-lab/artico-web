# CH12 — Making Arrangements

**Your goal:** Find a time that works, confirm the important details, and change an arrangement when necessary.  

## Study: Move from an idea to a plan

An invitation such as **Let's get lunch** does not yet say when or where. Use scheduling questions to make the plan usable:

- **When are you free?** Ask about availability.
- **Does Friday work for you?** Check a proposed day.
- **What time works for you?** Ask for a suitable time.
- **Could we make it six instead?** Suggest a different time.
- **Where should we meet?** Agree on a place.

**Arranging dinner**

**Drew:** Are you free for dinner on Friday?  
**Sasha:** Yes, but I can't get there before six.  
**Drew:** Could we make it six fifteen?  
**Sasha:** That works for me. Where should we meet?  
**Drew:** Outside the restaurant.  
**Sasha:** Okay. Friday at six fifteen, outside the restaurant.  

Sasha repeats the final details after the discussion. **That works for me** accepts this proposed arrangement. **I might be free** would leave availability uncertain.

## Study: Be precise enough

Useful support from Chapter 11:

- **At 6:15 p.m.** gives a time.
- **On Friday / on July 23** gives a day or date.
- **In the morning / in the evening** identifies a part of the day.
- **For an hour** gives a duration.
- **By six** can identify a deadline rather than a starting time.

**Drew:** Is the pickup at eleven forty-five?  
**Sasha:** Yes, eleven forty-five a.m., by the front entrance.  
**Drew:** Got it. I'll be there.  

If a numerical date could be interpreted in more than one way, spell out the month or ask **Do you mean July third?** For an online meeting across locations, **Which time zone is that?** can prevent a misunderstanding. You do not have to calculate a conversion in your head; confirm what time the meeting is for each person.

**Next Friday** can also need clarification when people interpret it differently. **Do you mean Friday the tenth?** checks the actual date rather than relying on the phrase alone.

## Study: Change or cancel

| Need | Expression |
| --- | --- |
| Move the time | **Could we move it to seven?** |
| Choose another day | **Thursday doesn't work for me. How about Saturday?** |
| Report a delay | **I'm running about ten minutes late.** |
| Cancel this plan | **I can't make it tonight. I'm sorry.** |
| Keep an answer provisional | **I'll check and let you know tomorrow.** |

**A delay message**

**Sasha:** I'm running about ten minutes late. Can you wait?  
**Drew:** I can wait until six thirty.  
**Sasha:** Thanks. I expect to be there by then.  
**Drew:** Okay. Let me know if that changes.  

**About** marks an estimate. The other person may have a limit; do not assume everyone can wait indefinitely. A cancellation can be complete without an alternative. Offer a new day if you want to reschedule.

## Practice: Reach a real agreement

Use this fictional availability table. The café is available from 4 to 9 p.m. in this scene.

| Day | Your availability | Your friend's availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tuesday | After 6 p.m. | 4–7 p.m. |
| Wednesday | Unavailable | After 6 p.m. |
| Friday | 5–8 p.m. | After 7 p.m. |

1. Find a time for a thirty-minute café visit on Tuesday. Suggest it, accept it as the friend, and confirm time and place. Support: **How about six fifteen?**, **That works**, **At the café**.
2. Your friend proposes Wednesday. Decline that day and offer a Friday time that fits both schedules.
3. You have agreed on Friday at 7:15 p.m. Change the response: your friend now needs to finish by 7:30. Either shorten the visit or propose a different plan. Support: **We could meet for fifteen minutes**, **Let's choose another day**.
4. Send a short text saying you expect to arrive ten minutes late. Rehearse a reply from someone who can wait, then from someone who cannot. Support: **I can wait**, **I need to leave at seven thirty**, **Let's reschedule**.
5. Hide the table. Make a new arrangement using invented availability. Confirm day or date, time, place and whether the answer is definite.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Does six fifteen Tuesday work? — Yes. — Great. Tuesday at six fifteen p.m., at the café.**
2. **Wednesday doesn't work for me. Could we meet Friday at seven fifteen? — That works.**
3. **We could meet for fifteen minutes** or **Let's choose another day when we have more time**.
4. **I'm running about ten minutes late. Can you wait? — I need to leave at seven thirty. — Okay. Let's reschedule.**

**Check:** Is there an actual overlap in availability? Did the final confirmation reflect changes made during the conversation? Could someone follow the plan without guessing the time zone, date or location?
