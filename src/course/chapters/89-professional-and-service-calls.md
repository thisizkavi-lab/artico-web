# CH89 — Professional and Service Calls

**Outcome:** Identify yourself and your purpose, manage a transfer or message, and confirm what happens after a call.  

## Study

**I'm calling about…** states the subject. **I'm calling regarding…** is another, more formal-sounding choice. **Could I speak with…?** requests a person. Both “Hi” and “Hello” can open a professional call; give enough identity and purpose for the task rather than relying on an elaborate greeting.

### Start and get to the right person

**Receptionist:** Cedar Design, this is Morgan.  
**Caller:** Hi, this is Lee Park from Harbor Print. Could I speak with Dana about the poster order?  
**Receptionist:** I'll transfer you. May I put you on hold?  
**Caller:** Yes, thank you.  
**Receptionist:** Thanks for waiting. Dana isn't available. Can I take a message?  
**Caller:** Please ask Dana to call me about the delivery date.  

**On hold** means waiting on the connection. A **transfer** changes whom you are connected to. If waiting is difficult: “I can't hold right now. Is there a callback option?” This gives the employee an alternative instead of assuming you must remain available.

### Leave enough information

Fictional message card: Lee Park, Harbor Print; order HP204; needs delivery-date confirmation before noon; callback 202-555-0146.

**Employee:** Could you spell your name and give a callback number?  
**Caller:** Lee Park—P-A-R-K. The number is 202-555-0146.  
**Employee:** And what's the message?  
**Caller:** Please confirm the delivery date for order HP204. I'd appreciate a reply before noon if possible.  
**Employee:** I'll pass that on.  
**Caller:** Thank you. Could you read back the order reference?  

A request for a deadline does not guarantee a response. “It's urgent for our planning” gives a reason. “They know where to reach me” may work when contact information is genuinely known; a concrete callback detail is useful when it is not.

### Customer service and a missing answer

**Agent:** How can I help?  
**Customer:** I'm calling about an order marked delivered. I haven't received it.  
**Agent:** I'll check the record. What's the reference?  
**Customer:** HP204. I've already contacted support once.  
**Agent:** We need to investigate and get back to you.  
**Customer:** When should I expect an update, and how will you contact me?  

“Could I speak with someone who can review this?” requests another level of help. “I need a clear next step” is firmer than merely repeating thanks. Explain the problem without inventing an outcome or using hostility as the only way to be heard.

### End with the arrangement

**Agent:** Is there anything else?  
**Caller:** No, that's all. So you'll send an update by email after checking the order?  
**Agent:** Yes, I'll confirm the arrangement in the message.  
**Caller:** Thanks for your help. Goodbye.  

Other endings: “I appreciate your time.” “I'll wait for that confirmation.” “Please contact me if you need more details.” Choose a closing that matches what actually happened; don't say the issue is resolved if it is still open.

## Practice

1. Use the message card to ask for Dana. Partner says “Dana isn't available.” Leave your identity, reference, request, and callback details.
2. Partner reads HP240 instead of HP204. Correct the reference and request confirmation.
3. Use the missing-order case. Partner says an investigation is needed. Ask about timing and the channel for an update, then close without pretending the parcel has been found.

Solo: read receptionist or agent turns and speak the caller turns; later hide the card and reconstruct the message. All contact details are fictional and need not be called.

**Possibilities:** “It's HP204—two, zero, four.” “When will I hear from you?” “Thank you; the issue is still open, so I'll look for the update.” Check whether the recipient knows who called, why, how to respond, and what remains unresolved.
