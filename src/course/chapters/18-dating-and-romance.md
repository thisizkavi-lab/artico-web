# CH18 — Dating and Romance

**Your goal:** Make or clarify a date invitation, express interest or refusal, discuss expectations, and close the encounter honestly.  

## Study: Invite and clarify

**Would you like to go out with me?** can make romantic interest clear. A coffee invitation alone can be ambiguous: it might mean a date, friendship or another purpose. Ask when you need to know.

**Alex:** Would you like to get coffee with me Saturday?  
**River:** Do you mean as a date?  
**Alex:** Yes, if you're interested.  
**River:** I'd like that. Saturday afternoon works for me.  
**Alex:** Great. How about two at the café?  

**I'd like that** accepts the idea. **I'd love to** expresses stronger enthusiasm. **I was hoping you'd ask** can express playful interest when that is what you want to convey. Clear time and place still matter; Chapter 12 supplies scheduling support.

**We should meet up sometime** shows an interest in meeting, but does not confirm a date or romantic intention. **Maybe. Let me check** is also unconfirmed. A possible reply is **Sure. Let me know**, rather than treating it as a definite yes.

## Study: Decline the right thing

| Expression | What the words communicate |
| --- | --- |
| **I can't on Saturday. How about Sunday?** | The proposed time does not work, but you offer another. |
| **Thanks, but I'm not interested in a date.** | A clear refusal of the romantic invitation. |
| **I like you as a friend.** | You describe a friendship intention. |
| **I'm already seeing someone.** | You are currently dating someone. |
| **I'm not dating right now.** | You are not participating in dating at present. |
| **No. I don't want to go out.** | A direct refusal. |

**Alex:** Would you like to go out Friday?  
**River:** Thanks, but I'm not interested in a date.  
**Alex:** Okay. Thanks for being clear.  

A refusal does not need an excuse or an alternative day. **I already have plans** only explains availability; it may leave romantic interest unclear. If you mean no to dating the person, a clearer refusal can express that meaning. If someone declines, acknowledge the answer rather than continue as though it were an invitation to negotiate.

## Study: Talk on the date

Begin with a short greeting from Chapter 1. A compliment such as **You look great** is optional, and **Thank you** is enough to accept it.

**At the café**

**Alex:** It's good to see you. I'm a little nervous.  
**River:** Me too. What do you like to do outside work?  
**Alex:** I like hiking and live music. How about you?  
**River:** I like cooking. I've been trying new recipes lately.  
**Alex:** What do you like making?  

**I'm a little nervous** names a feeling without requiring the other person to solve it. A follow-up can come from an interest they actually mention.

You can ask about expectations:

- **Are you looking for a relationship?** Ask about their goal.
- **I'm looking for something serious.** Describe interest in a committed relationship.
- **I'm keeping things casual for now.** Describe a less committed approach; explain what casual means to you if it matters.
- **I'm not sure yet.** Give an uncertain answer.
- **I'd rather not discuss my past relationships tonight.** Keep a topic private.

**Casual**, **serious**, and **exclusive** can need a conversation about expectations. **We're not exclusive** means you have not agreed to date only each other; it does not define every other expectation automatically.

## Study: Express affection or a boundary

**River:** I'd like to kiss you. Is that okay?  
**Alex:** Not right now.  
**River:** Okay.  

You can also answer **Yes, I'd like that** or **No, I don't want to**. **I'd like to take things slowly** describes a preference for the pace. These words give each person a way to express what they want in the moment.

## Study: End with the intention clear

**Alex:** Thanks for today. I had a good time. I'd like to see you again.  
**River:** I had a nice time too, but I'm not feeling a romantic connection.  
**Alex:** Okay. Thanks for telling me. Take care.  

If both want another date, **I'd like that. Let's find a day next week** begins the next plan. **Maybe, I'll let you know** leaves it open. Avoid inventing an enthusiastic promise merely to finish the conversation smoothly.

**How are you getting home? — I'm taking the bus** can discuss departure. **I need to head out** is a closing signal. Chapter 10 supplies other endings.

## Practice: Choose your actual intention

All roles below are invented adults. Rehearse both people alone, or alternate roles with a partner.

1. Invite someone to coffee. They ask whether it is a date. Your role means a date, and they are interested. Clarify, accept and propose Saturday at two.
2. Change the response: they are busy Saturday but interested Sunday. Rehearse an alternative. Then change the meaning again: they do not want a date. Give a clear refusal and acknowledgment.
3. On a date, ask about interests. Your partner likes cooking and music. Follow up on one. Supplied answer: **I like making pasta** or **I like jazz**.
4. Your role is unsure about a relationship goal. Answer **Are you looking for something serious?** without giving a false certainty.
5. Someone asks to kiss you. Your role wants to wait. Say so; give the other person's acknowledgment.
6. At the end, one person wants another date and the other does not. Rehearse the complete exchange with a clear answer rather than a fake plan.
7. Hide Study. Make a four-turn invitation or ending in which the reply differs from the first model.

### Sample possibilities

- 1: **Yes, I mean as a date. — I'd like that. — How about Saturday at two?**
- 2: **I can't Saturday. How about Sunday?** versus **I'm not interested in a date. — Okay.**
- 3: **What do you like cooking? — I like making pasta.**
- 4: **I'm not sure yet. I'm still figuring that out.**
- 5: **Not right now. I'd like to take things slowly. — Okay.**
- 6: **I'd like to see you again. — Thanks, but I'm not feeling a romantic connection. — Okay. Take care.**

**Check:** Was interest, uncertainty or refusal clear? Did the next turn respond to the answer given? Did you distinguish availability, relationship expectations and personal boundaries?
