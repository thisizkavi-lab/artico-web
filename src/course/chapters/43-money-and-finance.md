# CH43 — Money and Finance

**Outcome:** Ask about an account or payment, describe a card problem, and clarify a money transaction.  

## Study

| Term | Useful meaning |
| --- | --- |
| checking account | an account used for everyday money transactions |
| savings account | an account used to hold money for saving |
| deposit | put money into an account |
| withdraw | take money out of an account |
| transfer | move money between accounts or send it through a service |
| fee | a charge for a service or action |
| balance | the amount recorded in an account |
| statement | a record of account activity over a period |

A **debit card** uses money in a linked bank or credit-union account. A **credit card** lets you borrow money and repay it later. Their appearance may be similar, but the basic source of money differs.

### Asking about an account

For this fictional account discussion, Maple Everyday Checking has a $5 monthly fee and no ongoing minimum-balance condition. Its required-document list is not supplied; the customer still needs to request it.

**Customer:** I'd like to ask about opening a checking account.  
**Representative:** What would you like to know?  
**Customer:** What documents do I need, and is there a monthly fee?  
**Representative:** There's a five-dollar monthly fee. I'll give you the required-document list.  
**Customer:** Is there a minimum balance?  
**Representative:** No ongoing minimum balance for this account.  
**Customer:** So there's a five-dollar monthly fee regardless of that balance?  
**Representative:** That's right.  
**Customer:** Thanks. I'd still like to review the document requirements before deciding.  

**Minimum balance** means a stated lowest amount relevant to an account's conditions. A condition may affect a fee or another feature; ask which. Useful online-access questions are **How can I access the account online?** and **Where can I find the instructions?**

### Deposit or withdrawal

**Customer:** I'd like to deposit this check.  
**Representative:** Which account would you like it deposited into?  
**Customer:** My checking account. When will the money be available?  
**Representative:** I'll check the availability information for this deposit.  

**I'd like to withdraw one hundred dollars** states a requested amount. **Could I have smaller bills?** asks about the denominations. Neither phrase establishes that a transaction is available or free; the response supplies those details.

### When a card or transaction is a problem

**Customer:** I can't find my debit card. Could you explain how to block it?  
**Agent:** I'll connect you with the team that handles card reports.  
**Customer:** Thank you. I also see a transaction I don't recognize.  
**Agent:** Tell the team about that so they can explain the review process.  

**I don't recognize this charge** describes uncertainty. **I didn't make this purchase** states what you know about your own action. **The ATM kept my card** describes a machine incident. Ask **What information do you need?** and **How can I follow up?** rather than guessing the institution's process.

### Currency and paying together

An **exchange rate** expresses the value of one currency in another. **What rate are you using?**, **Is there a fee?**, and **How much will I receive in total?** help you clarify a proposed exchange.

For practice only, a service quotes a final total of $96 after its stated charges in exchange for the customer's other currency.

**Customer:** Is ninety-six dollars the amount I'll receive after all charges?  
**Clerk:** Yes, that's the final quoted amount.  
**Customer:** Okay. Could I have a mix of smaller and larger bills?  

With friends, **Let's split it equally** proposes equal shares. **I'll cover mine** means pay your own share. **It's on me** offers to pay. You can decline an arrangement: **I'd rather pay separately**.

**A:** The total is sixty dollars. Should we split it equally?  
**B:** There are three of us, so twenty each.  
**A:** I can pay the bill. How would you like to send your share?  
**B:** Let's choose a payment method that works for both of us.  

## Practice

Use the models' representative turns as prompts when alone. Practice requests and clarification, not a real financial transaction.

1. Ask about Maple Everyday Checking's fee and balance condition. Give the representative's replies from the scene facts. Then request the document information that is still missing.
2. Ask to deposit a check and find out when the funds will be available.
3. Your card is missing and you see a purchase you did not make. State both facts and ask about the reporting process.
4. Three people share a fictional $60 bill. Propose equal shares, or explain that you prefer separate payment.

**Sample possibilities:** “Is there a monthly fee?” — “Five dollars a month, with no ongoing minimum-balance condition.” — “Could you give me the required-document list?” / “When will the deposited money be available?” / “My card is missing, and I didn't make this purchase. How do I report both issues?” / “Twenty each?” — “I'd rather pay separately.”  

Return later with a different amount or problem. Check whether you distinguished a requested action, a known fact and an unanswered question. The account's own terms supply the financial conditions.
