# CH58 — Moving

**Outcome:** Coordinate packing and moving, clarify where belongings go, and discuss timing or a moving-service question.  

## Study

**Move out** means leave a home; **move in** means begin living in another. **Pack** puts belongings into containers; **unpack** takes them out. **Load** puts items into a vehicle; **unload** takes them out. A **moving truck** carries belongings. **Fragile** marks something easily damaged.

### Ask for supplies or help

**A:** We're moving next Saturday. Do you have any spare boxes?  
**B:** I have three. What size do you need?  
**A:** Small ones for books would help.  
**B:** You can have these two.  
**A:** Thanks. Could I pick them up tomorrow evening?  
**B:** Yes, after six works.  

**Spare** means additional or not currently needed. **Could you help us pack?** asks for assistance; **Could you help on Saturday morning?** specifies time. To decline: **I can't help with the move, but I can lend you some boxes** offers a different kind of help.

### Coordinate the day

For this fictional move: collect the keys at 9 a.m.; movers arrive at 10; kitchen boxes go to the kitchen; books go to the living room; a box marked **Fragile—glasses** needs its label noticed. The destination address is **18 Cedar Street, apartment 3**.

**Mover:** Where would you like these boxes?  
**Resident:** The ones marked Kitchen go in the kitchen. Books go in the living room.  
**Mover:** What about this one?  
**Resident:** That has glasses in it. It's marked fragile. Could you confirm you saw the label?  
**Mover:** Yes, I've got it.  
**Resident:** Thanks. This box isn't labeled. I'll check before you move it to a room.  

**Marked** or **labeled** describes words placed on an object. **This one** works when the specific box is clear. If not, describe it: **The small box beside the door**.

### Clarify access and timing

**Resident:** I want to confirm tomorrow's move. What time will you arrive?  
**Mover:** Ten in the morning.  
**Resident:** The address is eighteen Cedar Street, apartment three. Is there any access information you need?  
**Mover:** Tell us about the building entrance and where to ask about parking.  
**Resident:** I'll check the building's information and send it to you. What does the quoted price include?  
**Mover:** I'll send the itemized details for you to review.  

**Itemized** means divided into listed items or charges. **Does the quote include packing?** and **How are extra charges explained?** ask about a particular service. **I haven't finished packing yet** reports progress honestly; **Most things are packed, but the kitchen isn't finished** gives more useful detail.

### When plans change

**Resident:** We may need a later start because the key pickup has changed. Is an eleven o'clock arrival possible?  
**Mover:** I need to check the schedule.  
**Resident:** Okay. Could you let me know whether that's confirmed?  

**May need** introduces uncertainty. **Is it confirmed?** distinguishes a request from an agreed change. Afterward, **The boxes are here, but we haven't unpacked** separates arrival from completion.

## Practice

Take both parts alone, or switch roles. Use the timetable and box labels above; no physical lifting is needed.

1. Ask for spare boxes and arrange pickup after six tomorrow evening.
2. Explain where the kitchen and book boxes go. An unlabeled box appears; say you need to check it first.
3. Confirm the moving time and address. Ask whether the quote includes packing.
4. Key pickup changes to 10:30. Request an 11 a.m. start, then ask whether it has been confirmed.

**Sample possibilities:** “Do you have spare boxes? Could I pick them up after six?” / “Kitchen boxes go in the kitchen. I need to check this unlabeled one.” / “Could you confirm ten o'clock at eighteen Cedar Street, apartment three?” / “Would eleven be possible? Please let me know whether the change is confirmed.”  

Return later with a different schedule or room assignment. Check that your instructions identify the object, destination and any unresolved timing rather than relying on vague “over there” directions.
