# CH31 — Sports and Recreation

**Your goal:** Join or decline a game, ask about a lesson or facility, and use clear language during a team activity.  

## Study

### Name the activity

Choose the sports relevant to you. These brief meanings identify activities; they do not teach their rules.

| Sport | Identifying meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **baseball** | A bat-and-ball team game involving running around bases. |
| **basketball** | A team game aiming to score in the other team's basket. |
| **American football** | A team game carrying, throwing and kicking a ball toward scoring areas. |
| **soccer** | A team game aiming to kick the ball into a goal. |
| **tennis / badminton** | Racket sports using a ball / a shuttlecock. |
| **swimming** | Moving through water by using your body. |
| **track and field** | Running, jumping and throwing events. |
| **ice hockey** | A team game on ice using skates, sticks and a puck. |
| **boxing** | A combat sport using gloved fists. |
| **golf** | A game using clubs to hit a ball into holes. |
| **cricket** | A bat-and-ball team game using small sets of upright posts called wickets. |
| **rugby** | A team game using an oval ball that players carry, pass and kick. |

For running, jumping and throwing events, **track and field** names the activity clearly. **Athletics** can also refer more broadly to sports. If **football** is unclear, ask **Do you mean soccer or American football?** The speaker's intended sport matters more than guessing from their nationality.

A **team** plays together; an **opponent** plays against you. **Practice** develops skills; a **game** or **match** is a contest. A **court** is the playing area for sports such as tennis or basketball; a **field** is used for sports such as soccer or baseball. A swimming **pool** is a contained area of water. A **recreation center** provides places or programs for leisure activities.

### Join, decline or ask about the plan

**Jordan:** We're playing basketball after class. Want to join us?  
**Casey:** Yeah, I'm up for it. Where are you playing?  
**Jordan:** Court four at six.  
**Casey:** Great. I'll meet you there.  

**I'm up for it** means you are willing or interested. **Count me in** agrees to participate. You may hear or write **Wanna join?** for **Want to join?** in familiar conversation. The full form remains useful too.

**Jordan:** Are you coming to practice tonight?  
**Casey:** I can't play today, but I'd like to watch.  
**Jordan:** Sure. See you there.  

Other responses include **Not this time**, **I don't know how to play. Could you explain?**, and **What time does it start?** Asking for information does not itself confirm attendance. **My ankle hurts, so I'm sitting this one out** gives a reason if it fits your role. **Sit this one out** means not take part in this activity or round.

### Ask about lessons and facilities

Fictional recreation-center information: beginner tennis lessons Saturday at 10 a.m.; experienced-player lessons at 11; swimming sessions from 3 to 5 p.m., booked at the desk; basketball practice Tuesday at 6 p.m. on Court 4. The badminton court is Court 2, past the desk and through the first door on the left.

**Visitor:** Do you offer tennis lessons?  
**Employee:** Yes. Beginners come on Saturday at ten. The experienced group starts at eleven.  
**Visitor:** I'm a beginner. How do I sign up?  
**Employee:** We can take your details at the desk.  
**Visitor:** Before I book, what does the lesson cost?  
**Employee:** I'll get the current price information for you.  

**Do I need to bring a racket?**, **Is equipment rental available?**, and **Are there open sessions?** check arrangements not supplied by a sport's name. A **beginner** is starting to learn; **experienced** means having previous experience. Being experienced does not necessarily mean being a professional.

### Communicate during a game

In this basketball exchange, a teammate is free to receive the ball:

**Casey:** Over here! Pass it to me!  
**Jordan:** Here!  
**Casey:** I've got it.  
**Jordan:** Shoot!  
**Casey:** Missed it.  
**Jordan:** That was close. Let's try again.  

**Pass** sends the ball to a teammate; **shoot** attempts to score. Short calls can fit a fast-moving game. **Someone's behind you!** alerts a teammate to an opponent in the stated scene. **My turn?** checks whose turn it is; **Whose ball is it?** asks which side has possession or the next play. The exact meaning depends on the sport and moment.

## Practice

1. A friend invites you to the Tuesday basketball practice. Ask where and when, then accept or decline. Your partner uses the center information.
2. You want beginner tennis. Ask about the group and how to sign up. Then ask about the unknown cost instead of inventing a price.
3. Ask where the badminton court is. The employee gives the supplied directions. Repeat them to check: **Past the desk, first door on the left?**
4. In the basketball scene, call for a pass and report a missed shot. Change the next cue: you did not hear your teammate. Say **What did you say?** and have them repeat **Pass it to me!**
5. Choose three sports you want to talk about. Cover their meanings and describe one. Then say whether you want to play, watch or learn it.

**Possible responses:** “Which court, and what time? — Court four at six. — Count me in.” / “I'd like the beginner tennis lesson. What does it cost?” / “So Court Two is past the desk, through the first door on the left?” / “I missed it. Let's try again.” / “I'd like to learn tennis.”  

**Solo route:** say both roles without performing a physical game. On a later round, change your availability or the sport. **Check:** Did you communicate your intention, confirm the activity's details, and make calls the teammate could understand? Did you keep an unanswered price or booking question separate from a confirmed arrangement?
