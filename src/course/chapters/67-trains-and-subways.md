# CH67 — Trains and Subways

**Outcome:** Choose a train, ask about tickets and transfers, and understand a service change.  

## Study

A **platform** is the area beside a train. A **track number** identifies the track used by a service. **Local** and **express** are service labels; confirm whether a train stops where you need it. **Inbound / outbound** may describe direction relative to a center; use the destination as an additional check. A **car** is one section of a train.

### Buy the right trip

**Traveler:** I'd like a round-trip ticket to Harbor City for Saturday.  
**Agent:** Returning the same day?  
**Traveler:** Yes. Does the ticket include a seat reservation?  
**Agent:** For this train, it does. Which departure do you want?  
**Traveler:** The 10:15. Is there an earlier one?  
**Agent:** There's a 9:00, but it's sold out.  
**Traveler:** I'll take the 10:15, then.  

Other useful questions are “How much is a one-way ticket?” “Can I reserve a seat?” and “Does this ticket work on the subway too?” The answer depends on the ticket and service. If a discount applies, ask what document you need to show. A reservation and a ticket are related but are not interchangeable words.

### Confirm the route

**Rider:** Does this train stop at Market Street?  
**Employee:** No, this is the express. Take the local on the other side.  
**Rider:** Do I need to transfer?  
**Employee:** No. The local goes directly there.  
**Rider:** How many stops is it?  
**Employee:** Four.  

“Which platform is the Harbor City train leaving from?” asks where to wait. “Has it left yet?” asks whether departure has already happened. “When is it due?” asks the expected arrival time. Use “Could you write the station name down?” when a name is unfamiliar.

### Changes and announcements

These fictional notices illustrate the language:

- “The 10:15 train is delayed by twenty minutes.” It is expected later; that is different from canceled.
- “Track change: passengers for Harbor City should go to Track 4.” Your boarding location has changed.
- “Weekend service is suspended between Oak and River. Shuttle buses will operate.” Trains are not running on that section; replacement transportation is announced.
- “Stand clear of the closing doors.” Move away from the doors.
- “Watch the gap between the train and the platform.” Pay attention to the space as you move between them.

Ask “What are my options now?” or “Where do I catch the shuttle?” rather than assuming the old route still works.

### On board

**Employee:** Tickets, please.  
**Traveler:** Here's mine. Where's the cafe car?  
**Employee:** Two cars ahead.  
**Traveler:** Thanks. Is anyone sitting here?  
**Passenger:** No, go ahead.  
**Traveler:** Do you mind if I use this outlet?  
**Passenger:** Not at all.  

An **outlet** supplies electricity. “Not at all” here grants the request; it does not refuse it.

## Practice

Fictional board: 9:00 Harbor City, sold out; 10:15 Harbor City, Track 2, now 10:35; local subway to Market Street, four stops, no transfer. A new notice moves the Harbor City departure to Track 4.

1. Ask for the morning round trip. Partner gives the board information. Choose and confirm your departure.
2. Explain the changed time and track to a companion who still expects Track 2 at 10:15.
3. Ask whether the express reaches Market Street. Partner says it does not, and gives the local route. Confirm it.

Solo: rehearse both roles, then hide the explanations and report only the travel details. Return later with a changed destination.

**Possibilities:** “It's now leaving at 10:35 from Track 4.” “So the local goes straight to Market Street?” Check that your response preserves the destination, direction, time, and change—not a specific string of words.
