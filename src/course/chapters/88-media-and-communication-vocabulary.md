# CH88 — Media and Communication Vocabulary

**Outcome:** Name a communication channel, device, online action, or reading item and explain what you need.  

## Study

Choose terms for your own task first. A word may name a thing, an action, or both: “Send a message” and “Message me” use *message* differently.

### Calls and messages

You **call**, **text**, **email**, or **make a video call**. A **voicemail** is a recorded message left for someone. To **take a message** is to record information for another person. To **put someone on hold** is to pause their connection while they wait; to **transfer a call** is to connect it elsewhere. **Speakerphone** lets a call play through a speaker. **Reply** answers a message; **forward** sends received content onward.

### Devices and connection

A **desktop** usually stays at a desk; a **laptop** is a portable computer; a **tablet** has a flat touch screen. **Phone / cell phone / smartphone** can describe a handheld communication device, with *smartphone* emphasizing its computing functions. A **smartwatch** is a watch with digital functions.

**Keyboard** has keys for entering information; **mouse** controls a pointer; **mouse pad** is a surface used beneath it. A **webcam** is a camera used with a computer or similar device. **Charging cable** connects a device for charging; **battery level** reports remaining charge. **Signal** describes reception. A **router** directs network traffic; **Wi-Fi** describes wireless networking; an **internet provider** supplies internet service. A **hotspot** provides a connection point for devices.

### Accounts and actions

An **account** is a user's access or record with a service. **Settings** control options; a **menu** presents choices. A **password** is a secret used in access. A **link** points to another online location. **Click** commonly describes selecting with a pointer; **tap** selecting by touch. You **browse** pages, **download** data to your device, **upload** data from it, and **stream** content as it is delivered. **Cookies** are data a website stores through a browser; they are not all the same as passwords or computer viruses.

### Social media and reading

A **post** is published content. A **follower** follows an account; **like**, **share**, and **comment** name actions or responses. **DM** means direct message. **Livestream** is a live online broadcast. **Scroll** moves through content. **Block** restricts interaction under a platform's rules; **troll** can describe someone deliberately provoking others. **Hashtag** is a label beginning with #; **selfie** is a self-taken photo. **Influencer** describes someone whose online presence can influence an audience. **Viral** describes content spreading widely, not a literal medical virus.

A **podcast** is an episode-based media program. **Headline** titles a news item; an **article** is a piece of writing. **Magazine**, **newspaper**, **book**, and **e-reader** describe publications or a device for reading them. A **subscription** gives ongoing access or delivery under stated terms. An **emoji** is a small graphic symbol; an **abbreviation** is a shortened form.

## Practice

Fictional tasks: send a photo privately; join a video appointment; read a newspaper article; send a received email to a colleague.

1. Choose the relevant channel and action for two tasks. Explain the choice.
2. Hide the definitions. Contrast upload/download, reply/forward, or article/headline using your own example.
3. Describe one device or connection need in a short sentence. Alone, say it; with someone else, let them identify the need.

**Possibilities:** “I'll send a direct message with the photo.” “I need a webcam for the video call.” “I'll forward the email.” Check that direction, recipient, and action are clear. Actual account, security, or platform instructions belong to the specific service; the following lessons develop the communication tasks.
