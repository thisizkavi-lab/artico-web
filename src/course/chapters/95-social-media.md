# CH95 — Social Media

**You can:** talk about online content, choose a response to contact, and explain a simple content idea.  

## Study

### What do you want to do with the post?

To **follow** an account is to receive or find its updates through the platform. To **share** is to pass content on. A **comment** responds where other people can see it; a **DM**, or direct message, goes through a private messaging channel. Private here describes the channel, not a guarantee that the recipient will keep the information secret.

> **Jules:** Did you see my photos from the street festival?  
> **Carmen:** I saw the one with the dancers. Can you send me the other pictures?  
> **Jules:** Sure. I'll DM them to you.  
> **Carmen:** Thanks. I'd rather not put the one with me on my profile.  
> **Jules:** Okay. Do you want to choose a different one?  
> **Carmen:** Yes, the picture of the food stands.  

“The photo came out great” praises the result. “Could you leave me out of the post?” sets a boundary. You can like the picture and still prefer not to be included. If you are planning to post a picture of someone, “Are you okay with me posting this?” lets them give a real answer.

### Reach, reaction, and interests

A post **goes viral** when it spreads widely and quickly. There is no single number in this expression that guarantees it is viral. **Followers** follow an account; **likes** are reactions to a particular item. **Reach** describes how many people content gets to. These are different things: having many followers does not mean every follower sees or likes every post.

> **Noah:** That dog video really took off.  
> **Tess:** I know. People keep sharing it.  
> **Noah:** Do you follow the account?  
> **Tess:** No, but I liked that one video.  

“Took off” here means suddenly became popular. “I follow them for the recipes” explains a reason. “I'm not into their travel posts” expresses a preference without making it a rule for everyone.

### Unwanted contact and disagreement

Someone may criticize a post, tease a friend, or repeatedly provoke people. **Trolling** refers to deliberate provocation; a single disagreement does not prove that intention.

You can reply, ask a question, decline, or stop engaging. “What do you mean by that?” seeks clarification. “I don't agree, but I see your point” can continue a discussion. “Please stop messaging me” sets a direct boundary. “I'm going to block that account” describes a choice; blocking and reporting functions depend on the platform.

You do not have to keep answering to make the exchange polite. Equally, disagreement alone does not require ending it. Choose the action that serves your purpose.

### A business content idea

> **Owner:** What could we post about the new classes?  
> **Assistant:** A short video could show what happens in a class.  
> **Owner:** I don't want students on camera without asking them.  
> **Assistant:** We could film you explaining an activity, then invite questions in the comments.  
> **Owner:** Let's try that. What would the caption say?  
> **Assistant:** “Curious about our Saturday art class? Here's a quick look at one activity. Message us if you'd like the schedule.”  

This is a proposed communication plan, not a promise of audience growth. An **influencer** has an online audience they can influence; a **livestream** broadcasts as it happens; a **podcast** presents a series of episodes. Choose the format for the information you want to share.

## Practice

### 1. Talk about content

Fictional facts: you liked a cooking clip and shared it with your sister. You do not follow its creator. Answer these partner questions: “Do you follow that account?” and “Why did you share the video?”

Possibility: “No, I just liked that clip. I shared it because my sister wants to learn that recipe.” Do not change the facts to make yourself a follower.

### 2. Change the social choice

Partner: “Can I post this picture of us?” First, you agree but want them to ask before tagging your account. “Sure. Could you check with me before tagging me?” A **tag** links or identifies an account in a post.

Second, you do not want the picture posted. Say that clearly. “I'd rather you didn't post that one” or “Please don't post that picture” can both fit. Partner: “How about the landscape photo?” You are happy with that alternative; answer accordingly.

### 3. Propose and respond

Fictional bookstore: staff can film a short book introduction or host a live question session. The manager wants a format people can watch later, and no one is available to answer live questions. Suggest the recorded introduction and explain why. Partner: “What should we ask people to do?” Suggest commenting with a book question or requesting the event schedule.

**Solo route:** answer the supplied cues, then cover the study models and try the opposite picture preference. Write one usable caption for the bookstore. Check that your message, audience, and requested action are clear; neither agreement nor refusal is compulsory.
