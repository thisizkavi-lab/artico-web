# CH82 — Booking a Health Appointment

**Outcome:** Request a medical or dental visit, describe the reason briefly, and confirm or change its arrangements.  

## Study

An **appointment** is an arranged visit. **New patient** means someone not previously registered or seen under that provider's arrangement. **Opening / available appointment** means a time being offered. A **callback** is a return call. **Triage** is assessment used to decide how care should be prioritized; asking for an appointment does not by itself establish urgency or guarantee a place.

### Request the visit

**Caller:** I'd like to make an appointment with Dr. Diaz.  
**Receptionist:** Have you been seen here before?  
**Caller:** No. Are you accepting new patients?  
**Receptionist:** I'll check. What's the reason for the visit?  
**Caller:** I've had a cough for a week. I'd like to speak with someone about it.  
**Receptionist:** Let me ask the nurse about the next step.  
**Caller:** What information do you need from me?  

Say “I'd like a dental checkup,” “I'd like an appointment for my daughter,” or “I need to discuss a problem with my medicine.” A brief symptom report is often more useful than choosing a diagnosis: “I've had a cough” rather than claiming a chest infection you have not been told you have.

### Confirm the practical details

**Receptionist:** We can offer Thursday at 3:30 with Dr. Diaz.  
**Caller:** Is that at the Main Street office?  
**Receptionist:** Yes. This visit is in person.  
**Caller:** What should I bring, and when should I arrive?  
**Receptionist:** I'll send the instructions with the confirmation.  
**Caller:** Could you also tell me how to arrange an interpreter?  

**In person** means attending physically; a **video visit** takes place through a video connection. Ask “Is it a phone appointment or a video appointment?” if “remote” is unclear. “Could you write the time and address in the confirmation?” is a practical repair request. Bring and arrival instructions belong to the specific clinic.

### Change or cancel

**Caller:** I have an appointment Thursday at 3:30, but I need to reschedule it.  
**Receptionist:** Could I have your name?  
**Caller:** Alex Park. Is Friday available?  
**Receptionist:** At this office, we have Friday at ten.  
**Caller:** That works. Please confirm that Thursday's appointment is canceled.  

**Reschedule** changes the time; **cancel** ends the arrangement. Ask about applicable charges rather than assuming a universal policy.

### Make urgency clear without promising access

**Caller:** I have severe tooth pain and need advice about being seen soon.  
**Receptionist:** I'll ask the clinical team to assess your request.  
**Caller:** When should I expect a callback, and what should I do if I need help sooner?  

If the service says “We're not accepting new patients,” ask “Can you tell me where to look for another provider?” Avoid interpreting a waiting list or callback as a guarantee of care at a specific time. Immediate emergency reporting is covered in Chapter 84; these appointment models are not emergency triage instructions.

## Practice

Fictional schedule: Thursday 3:30, Main Street office, in person; Friday 10:00 available. Caller Alex Park wants to discuss a week-long cough. Interpreter arrangements and arrival instructions are not yet confirmed.

1. Request the visit. Partner asks whether you are a new patient and why you are calling. Answer with the role details.
2. Confirm the offered location and type of visit. Ask for the missing instructions or interpreter arrangement.
3. Reschedule from Thursday to Friday. Partner offers ten. Verify the old visit is canceled and the new one confirmed.
4. Change to a dental pain role. Ask for prompt clinical advice without asserting which priority you must receive.

Solo: read receptionist turns and answer, then state the confirmed arrangement with the schedule hidden.

**Possibilities:** “I'm a new patient.” “So Friday at ten replaces Thursday?” “Who can advise me about being seen sooner?” Check the visit's reason, date, time, place or channel, and unresolved arrangements.
