# CH70 — Taxis and Rides

**Outcome:** Arrange a ride, give a destination, confirm pickup details, and handle a change or missing item.  

## Study

**Cab** and **taxi** are related words for a hired vehicle with a driver. A **taxi stand** is a waiting area. A **pickup location** is where the ride starts; a **drop-off location** is where you leave the vehicle. **Rideshare** here means an app-arranged ride. Ask about the particular service's arrangements.

### Arrange the trip

**Customer:** Hi, I'd like to reserve a taxi for tomorrow morning.  
**Dispatcher:** Where should we pick you up?  
**Customer:** At the north entrance of Cedar Hotel, at 7:30.  
**Dispatcher:** Where are you going?  
**Customer:** The airport. There are four passengers and two large suitcases.  
**Dispatcher:** I'll check which vehicle is available.  
**Customer:** I'm also traveling with a service dog. Could you confirm the pickup arrangements?  
**Dispatcher:** I'll note that and confirm the details with you.  

A **service dog** is a dog trained to perform tasks related to a person's disability. Ask clearly for arrangements you need. “Could you repeat the pickup time?” helps verify a booking. The answer may be a confirmation, or “We don't have a vehicle available then.” You can ask “What's the earliest time you can offer?”

### Get into the right trip

**Passenger:** Are you the driver for Maya?  
**Driver:** Yes. You're going to Cedar Hotel?  
**Passenger:** That's right—the north entrance, please.  
**Driver:** Do you have any bags for the trunk?  
**Passenger:** Just this suitcase.  

**Trunk** is the storage area at the back of a car. “Can you take me to this address?” works when you can show the destination. “About how long will it take?” asks for an estimate, not a guarantee. “Does that estimate include tolls?” checks whether a charge is part of the quoted amount.

### Change, pay, and recover an item

**Passenger:** Could we make one extra stop at the pharmacy?  
**Driver:** Possibly. It may change the fare. Which pharmacy?  
**Passenger:** The one on Cedar Avenue. Could you tell me the additional cost first?  

At the end: “Could you drop me off at this entrance?” “Can I pay by card?” “Could I have a receipt?” **Keep the change** tells someone not to return the difference from a cash payment. It is a choice in an appropriate payment situation, not a compulsory phrase or tipping rule.

**Customer:** I think I left my laptop in a taxi this morning.  
**Agent:** What time was the trip, and where did it end?  
**Customer:** Around eight. I went from Cedar Hotel to the airport.  
**Agent:** Do you have the trip reference?  
**Customer:** Yes, it's on my receipt. What's the next step?  

“I can't find the pickup area” and “I've been waiting twenty minutes” report a practical problem. “I need an update, please” is firm without pretending that a reply time is guaranteed.

## Practice

Fictional trip: Maya, Cedar Hotel north entrance, tomorrow 7:30 a.m., airport, four passengers, two suitcases. Later problem: laptop missing after the ride.

1. Reserve the ride. Partner asks for pickup, destination, people, and bags. Confirm the essentials.
2. Partner says the driver is at the south entrance. Clarify your location and ask what to do.
3. Add a pharmacy stop. Partner says it may cost more. Ask for the amount before agreeing.
4. Report the missing laptop using the trip details.

Solo: read the dispatcher or driver turns and supply your own replies. Later change the pickup time and baggage.

**Possibilities:** “I'm at the north entrance, not the south entrance.” “What would the total fare be?” “I think I left a laptop in the car.” Check that the listener can identify the ride, requested change, and unresolved problem.
