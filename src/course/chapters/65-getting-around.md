# CH65 — Getting Around

**Outcome:** Name a way to travel, recognize a travel location, and describe a ticket or delay.  

## Study

Choose the words for your own trip first. These groups support the bus, train, airport, taxi, and directions lessons that follow.

### Ways to travel

- **Car / van / truck:** road vehicles. A van often carries people or goods; a **truck** carries goods or equipment. You may also hear *lorry* for this kind of vehicle.
- **Bike / bicycle:** a vehicle with pedals. **Motorcycle** has a motor; a **scooter** can refer to different small vehicles, so describe the kind you mean.
- **Taxi / cab:** a vehicle and driver hired for a trip. A **taxi stand** is a place where taxis wait.
- **Bus:** a road vehicle carrying passengers on a service. **Intercity bus** describes travel between cities; *coach* may name a kind of bus or a service.
- **Train:** a vehicle or connected cars that travel on rails. It is the broad word here.
- **Subway:** a city passenger-rail service that often runs underground; some sections can be above ground.
- **Light rail:** an urban passenger-rail service usually using smaller vehicles, sometimes along streets and sometimes on separate tracks. These rail labels can overlap. Local names differ; use the name on the signs.
- **Ferry:** a boat carrying passengers, and sometimes vehicles. **Plane** is short for airplane. A **helicopter** uses rotating blades.

### Places and people

A **bus stop** is where a bus picks up or drops off passengers. A **station** is a larger travel location. A **platform** is where passengers wait beside a train. A **parking lot** is a place to park vehicles. An **airport** serves planes; a **runway** is the strip used for takeoff and landing. A **port** serves ships. **Passengers** are people traveling in a vehicle. A **line** is people waiting their turn: “The ticket line is long.”

### Tickets, documents, and actions

A **one-way ticket** covers travel in one direction. A **round-trip ticket** covers the outward and return trip under its stated conditions. An **e-ticket** is an electronic ticket. A **driver's license** is a driving document. A **discount card** gives a specified discount; its terms depend on the service.

You **board / get on a bus, train, or plane**, then **get off** it. You **get in a car or taxi**, then **get out** of it. A plane **takes off** and **lands**. **Tap your card** describes touching it to a reader. Ask where and when to tap rather than assuming every system works alike. **Give someone a ride** means take them in your vehicle.

A **departure board** shows travel information. **Delayed** means later than scheduled; **canceled** means that scheduled service will not run.

## Practice

Use this fictional board: Bus 8 — Museum — 9:10 — on time. Train 24 — Harbor — 9:30 — delayed. Ferry B — Island — 10:00 — canceled.

1. Hide the definitions. Say which service is late, which will not run, and where Bus 8 goes.
2. Choose a one-way journey or a journey there and back. Name the ticket you would ask about.
3. Choose five words for a trip from home to an island. Explain what each contributes. Alone, say the route out loud; with a partner, explain it and exchange roles.

**Sample possibilities:** “The Harbor train is delayed.” “I'd like a round-trip ticket.” “I'll get on a bus at the bus stop, then take the ferry.” More than one route can make sense. Check that your words describe the intended vehicle, place, and travel status.
