# CH44 — At the Library

**Outcome:** Ask about joining a library, find or borrow material, and clarify a library service.  

## Study

To begin, **How do I get a library card?** asks about membership. **What identification do I need?** and **Can visitors apply?** ask about requirements. Library systems have their own conditions; the person you speak to can explain the relevant ones.

A **librarian** helps with library information and services. A **catalog** lists the collection. **Borrow** or **check out** means take a permitted item for a limited period. **Return** brings it back. **Renew** extends the loan when available. A **hold** is a request to reserve an item under the library's system. The **due date** is when a borrowed item is due back.

### Finding a book or another format

**Visitor:** I'm looking for a book about learning photography.  
**Librarian:** Would you prefer a beginner's guide?  
**Visitor:** Yes. Could you recommend one?  
**Librarian:** Let's check the catalog.  
**Visitor:** Is there an audiobook version, too?  
**Librarian:** I can look for that format.  

**E-book** means a digital book. **Audiobook** means a spoken recording of a book. **Do you have this in another format?** leaves the format open; **Do you have the e-book?** asks for one specifically.

### Borrowing and renewing

For practice, Cedar Library offers a two-week loan for the book in this scene. Its fictional renewal system may refuse renewal when another reader has requested the item.

**Reader:** I'd like to check out this book.  
**Staff member:** Do you have your card?  
**Reader:** Yes, here it is. When is it due?  
**Staff member:** Two weeks from today.  
**Reader:** Can I renew it if I need more time?  
**Staff member:** For this book, you can request a renewal unless another reader is waiting.  
**Reader:** Where can I check whether a renewal is available?  
**Staff member:** In your account or at this desk.  

If a book is unavailable, **Can I place a hold?** asks to reserve it. If renewal is refused: **Okay. Where should I return it?** responds to the actual result rather than assuming a longer loan.

### Other services

**Can I reserve a computer?**, **Do you have a quiet study area?**, **Do you offer classes?**, and **Where can I find older newspapers?** identify different needs. Describe the task if you do not know the service name: **I need somewhere to work on a school project**.

**Visitor:** Do you offer help with downloading e-books?  
**Staff member:** We do. What would you like help with?  
**Visitor:** I'm not sure which account I need to use.  
**Staff member:** Let's look at the library's instructions together.  

## Practice

Rehearse both roles alone, using the staff responses above. Then change one detail.

1. You are a visitor and do not have a card. Ask how to apply and which documents are needed. The staff member says **I'll show you our visitor requirements.** Request an explanation of anything unclear.
2. You want a beginner's photography guide in audio form. Ask for the subject and format.
3. At Cedar Library, your book cannot be renewed because someone else requested it. Respond and ask where to return it.
4. You need a quiet place and access to a computer. Ask about each rather than assuming one space provides both.

**Sample possibilities:** “Can visitors get a card? What documents do I need?” / “Do you have a beginner's photography guide as an audiobook?” / “Okay. Where should I return it?” / “Is there a quiet study area? Can I reserve a computer?”  

Later, replace the subject or service. Check whether you named the material, format or task and understood the library's response. Exact policy details belong to the library you use.
