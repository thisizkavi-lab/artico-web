# CH06 — Agreeing and Disagreeing

**Your goal:** Show agreement, express a different view with the strength you intend, and decide how to continue a disagreement.  

## Study: Agree with the idea

| Expression | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **I agree.** | States agreement. |
| **Exactly.** | Signals that the point matches your view. |
| **Absolutely. / I totally agree.** | Gives emphatic agreement. |
| **That's a good point.** | Acknowledges a useful point; you may still disagree about the conclusion. |
| **I couldn't agree more.** | Expresses very strong agreement, despite the negative-looking form. |

**Planning a workshop**

**Nico:** We need a room with more seats.  
**Harper:** I agree. Twelve people won't fit comfortably in the small room.  
**Nico:** And we should check the price first.  
**Harper:** Absolutely.  

You can share a feeling without repeating the whole sentence:

- **I love this song. — Me too. / So do I.**
- **I don't like crowded rooms. — Me neither. / Neither do I.**
- **I don't think this is a good plan. — I don't think so either.**

In the last exchange, both people have doubts about the plan. **I don't think so either** is not a general disagreement answer to every positive statement.

## Study: Understand without agreeing

**I hear you** and **I know what you mean** can acknowledge someone's experience or concern. Add your view if agreement is unclear:

**Nico:** I'm worried the bigger room costs too much.  
**Harper:** I hear you. But I still think we need the extra space.  

Harper recognizes the concern while maintaining a different conclusion. Listening signals from Chapter 3 do not always equal approval.

## Study: Disagree or qualify

| Expression | A possible intention |
| --- | --- |
| **I'm not sure about that.** | Express doubt. |
| **I see it differently.** | State a different view. |
| **I agree about the location, but not the price.** | Separate the parts you accept and reject. |
| **I'm not convinced.** | Say the argument has not persuaded you. |
| **No, that's not right.** | Correct or reject something directly. |

A reason can make the difference clear. Direct disagreement is available when you want it; disagreement does not have to start with an apology. Softer wording can still communicate a firm decision.

**Discussing a movie**

**Nico:** That was the best part of the movie.  
**Harper:** I see it differently. I thought the ending was better.  
**Nico:** Really? I thought the ending was too easy.  
**Harper:** Fair enough. We liked different things.  

**Fair enough** acknowledges a view or explanation. Here it does not mean Harper changed their taste. Informal **Nah, it wasn't for me** can reject a positive evaluation. Stronger **No way** can make rejection emphatic; surrounding words distinguish that from surprised disbelief.

## Study: Continue or close the disagreement

- **What makes you think that?** Ask for the reason.
- **Let's check the information.** Investigate a factual question.
- **We may have to agree to disagree.** Accept that the views remain different.
- **Let's leave it there.** End this discussion.

Agreeing to disagree can close a debate about taste. It does not itself establish which factual claim is true or solve a decision the group still needs to make.

## Practice: Respond to the actual point

Use the supplied roles. Practice both sides alone, then reverse your position.

1. Someone says **I love quiet cafés**. Your role feels the same. Give a short agreement.
2. Someone says **I don't like loud music while I'm working**. Your role also dislikes it. Give a negative agreement.
3. A group needs twelve seats. The small room has eight; the large room has twenty. Someone says **The small room is big enough**. Correct that claim and give the number.
4. A friend says **The movie was boring**. Your role liked the acting but found the ending slow. Give a mixed view. Support: **I liked the acting**, **the ending was slow**, **I agree about...**.
5. They repeat **No, the whole movie was terrible**. Either ask for a reason or close the taste disagreement. Supply the friend's reply: **I didn't care about the characters** or **Okay, let's talk about something else**.
6. Hide Study. Make one exchange showing understanding without agreement, using the expensive-room concern from the model.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Me too.**
2. **Me neither.**
3. **No, that's not right. It seats eight, and we have twelve people.**
4. **I liked the acting, but the ending was slow.**
5. **What makes you think that?** or **We may have to agree to disagree.**
6. **I hear you, but we still need more seats.**

**Check:** Did your reply agree or disagree with the intended idea? Did you distinguish a person's concern from their conclusion? Could you move the conversation forward without pretending you agreed?
