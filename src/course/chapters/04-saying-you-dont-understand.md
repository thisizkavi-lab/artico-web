# CH04 — Saying You Don't Understand

**Your goal:** Explain what you missed and ask for repetition, slower speech or a clearer explanation.  

## Study: Identify the problem

You do not have to pretend you understand. A useful repair tells the other person what help you need.

| Your problem | Something you can say |
| --- | --- |
| You did not hear the words | **Sorry, I didn't catch that.** |
| You heard a word but do not know its meaning | **What does “deadline” mean?** |
| You heard the sentence but the meaning is unclear | **I'm not sure what you mean.** |
| The speech is too fast for you | **Could you say that more slowly?** |
| You need the steps explained again | **Could you walk me through it again?** |
| You need one detail confirmed | **Did you say Tuesday or Thursday?** |

**Catch** here means hear or understand what was said. **Walk me through it** means explain the steps. You can also use the direct **I don't understand** and then ask a specific question. An explanation about your English ability is optional, not a required apology.

## Study: Ask, receive help, and confirm

**At a workshop**

**Staff:** Please turn in the form at the front desk by three.  
**Visitor:** Sorry, I didn't catch the time. Did you say three?  
**Staff:** Yes, three o'clock.  
**Visitor:** Okay, by three. Thank you.  

The visitor asks about the missed detail rather than making the staff repeat everything. **By three** means no later than three in this instruction. The visitor repeats the important information to check it.

**A different problem**

**Staff:** Please turn in the form at the front desk.  
**Visitor:** What does “turn in” mean?  
**Staff:** Give the completed form to the person at the desk.  
**Visitor:** Got it. I give it to the person at the desk.  

**Got it** means you now understand. Say it after the explanation actually helps. If it does not, try **I'm still not sure. Could you show me?**

## Study: Short repair questions

**Excuse me?**, **Sorry?**, **What was that?** and **What?** can ask someone to repeat. A short expression can also communicate surprise or challenge, depending on the encounter and delivery. A specific question often makes your intention clearer.

**Friends making a plan**

**Jo:** Let's meet behind the building.  
**Ren:** Wait, what? Behind it?  
**Jo:** Yes, by the back entrance.  
**Ren:** Oh, okay. The back entrance.  

Ren's surprise and need to check are both visible. You can instead choose **Could you say that again?** when that expresses your intention better. Do not assume one short word is rude in every context.

Useful requests beyond repetition:

- **Could you write it down?** Ask for written support.
- **How do you spell that?** Check a name or word.
- **Could you give me an example?** Ask for meaning in context.
- **Do you mean the blue folder?** Check your interpretation.

## Practice: Repair the exchange

The following instructions belong to fictional scenes. Read both roles aloud on your own. Start with the support table; then hide it and try a different problem.

1. A person says **Meet me at the front desk at six**. You missed the time. Ask only about that detail. They repeat **Six o'clock**. Confirm it.
2. You hear **The deadline is Friday** but do not understand **deadline**. Ask. The explanation is **The last day to send it**. Say what you now understand.
3. Someone gives three instructions too quickly: **Open the page, choose a time, and send the form**. Ask for slower speech. Rehearse their slower repetition, then confirm the three actions.
4. You are told **Bring that folder**. You have a blue folder and a red one. Ask whether they mean the blue folder. They answer **No, the red one**. Respond to the correction.
5. Hide the model. Pick one of the situations and repair it without saying only **I don't understand**. Make your request specific enough to help the other person help you.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Sorry, what time did you say? — Six o'clock. — Okay, at six.**
2. **What does “deadline” mean? — The last day to send it. — Okay, I need to send it by Friday.**
3. **Could you say that more slowly? — Open the page. Choose a time. Send the form. — Got it: open, choose, and send.**
4. **Do you mean the blue folder? — No, the red one. — Oh, the red one. Okay.**

**Check:** Did you distinguish hearing from meaning? Did your request produce useful help? Could you confirm the corrected detail rather than repeat the original misunderstanding?
