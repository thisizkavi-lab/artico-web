# CH61 — Home Improvements

**Outcome:** Report progress on a home project and ask a professional about the scope, cost and timing of work.  

## Study

**Home improvements** are changes intended to improve a home. **DIY**, short for **do-it-yourself**, describes doing the work yourself. You can discuss a project without knowing how to perform every part of it.

Useful project words include **paint the walls**, **put up shelves**, **remove wallpaper**, **hang wallpaper**, **install carpet**, **repair a fence**, and **assemble a bookcase**. **Assemble** means put parts together. A **coat of paint** is one layer. **Tiles** are pieces used to cover a surface; **grout** is material that fills spaces between tiles. These terms describe work rather than give instructions for doing it.

### Explain what is finished

**A:** How's the kitchen project going?  
**B:** We've finished removing the wallpaper. The walls still need painting.  
**A:** Are you painting them yourselves?  
**B:** We haven't decided. We're asking someone for a quote first.  
**A:** What about the shelves?  
**B:** They're not up yet. That's the next job.  

**We've finished...** identifies completed work. **We still need to...** identifies remaining work. **Not yet** means something has not happened up to now; it does not necessarily mean the project has been abandoned. For a small update: **The tiles are in place, but the grouting isn't finished** or **There's one more coat of paint to go**.

**How's it going?** here asks about a particular project. Give useful detail rather than treating it only as a greeting. A positive comment is **It's looking good**; a concern is **I'm happy with the color, but that corner still needs attention**.

### Request a quote

A **quote** states a proposed price for described work. Ask what it covers. **Materials** are the items used for the work; **labor** is the work people perform. **Supply the materials** means provide them.

**Caller:** Hi. We need someone to paint our kitchen walls. Could you give us a quote?  
**Professional:** Yes. I'd need to see the room and discuss what you want done.  
**Caller:** Could you come on Thursday afternoon?  
**Professional:** I have two thirty available.  
**Caller:** That works. We'd also like to discuss putting up two shelves.  
**Professional:** Okay. I'll look at both jobs during the visit.  

For a different task: **Could you quote for repairing this fence?** or **We'd like someone to assemble this bookcase**. Explain the result you want; ask about anything you cannot describe precisely.

### Clarify before deciding

For the following fictional quote, painting the kitchen walls costs $240 and includes paint and labor. Painting the ceiling and installing shelves are separate jobs. The professional offers a Monday morning start and expects the wall work to take one day.

**Customer:** Does the two-hundred-forty-dollar quote include the paint?  
**Professional:** Yes, paint and labor for the walls. The ceiling isn't included.  
**Customer:** And the shelves would be a separate quote?  
**Professional:** That's right.  
**Customer:** When could you start the wall work?  
**Professional:** Monday morning. I expect it to take one day.  
**Customer:** Could you send the scope and price in writing? We'd like to review them before deciding.  
**Professional:** Sure.  

An expected duration is an estimate, not a promise that every project will take that long. **That's above our budget** explains a limit. **Is there a less expensive option?** asks about changing the proposal. **We'd like to go ahead with the wall work** accepts that work; it does not automatically accept every separately discussed job.

## Practice

Speak both roles alone. For a partner version, one person reads the project or quote facts while the other asks questions.

1. A project has finished wallpaper removal. Painting and shelves remain. Answer **How's the project going?** with completed and remaining work.
2. Request a visit for painting and two shelves. Arrange Thursday at two thirty using the model's available time.
3. Use the $240 quote to ask whether paint, the ceiling and shelves are included. Give complete replies from the stated facts.
4. Your budget is $200. Explain the difference and ask about an alternative without claiming a new price has been agreed.

**Sample possibilities:** “We've removed the wallpaper, but we still need to paint the walls and put up the shelves.” / “Could you come Thursday at two thirty?” / “Paint is included, but the ceiling and shelves are separate.” / “That's above our two-hundred-dollar budget. Is there a less expensive option?”  

Later, cover the models and report a different project: a bookcase is assembled, but the shelves are not up. Check whether you separated completed work, proposed work and work you actually agreed to.
