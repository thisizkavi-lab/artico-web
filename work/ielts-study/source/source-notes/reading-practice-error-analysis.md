## Distilled practice loop

The instructor’s “four tires” are (1) a method for each question type, (2) practice tests, (3) analysis of errors, and (4) focused repair. Time is presented as the spare tire. Turn that metaphor into a measurable loop:

1. **Learn the task method.** IELTS uses varied Reading question types that assess different skills. Teach each family with a worked example, its decision rule, and evidence from the text. The official format supports teaching distinct task demands; it does not require one fixed proprietary strategy per type. ([IELTS Academic Reading format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading))
2. **Practise the method before chasing a score.** During early learning, use a small set untimed, with notes or a checklist if needed. Explain each decision and correct the procedure. Then remove prompts, use fresh items, and add timing progressively. The point is to separate strategy learning from readiness measurement.
3. **Use tests to diagnose, not just count.** After a set, record the question family, your answer, the supporting passage evidence, and why the selected response was wrong. Look across more than one task or set for recurring patterns.
4. **Name the error before choosing practice.** The video suggests spelling, unfamiliar vocabulary, a wrong question-type method, or difficulty locating and understanding evidence. Add other useful labels as needed, such as missed instructions, rushed comparison, inference, or time. Distinguish a search/location problem from a comprehension or answer-decision problem.
5. **Repair the leading weakness.** Use targeted mini-practice on the error pattern, then retest with new material. Resume full timed sets after the method is sufficiently reliable. If progress stalls, revisit the diagnosis instead of assigning more undirected tests.
6. **Use time as a constraint to prepare for, not a reason to skip learning.** Build from accurate, supported practice toward the 60-minute Reading test. Make a test-date decision using the target score, practice evidence, personal deadlines, and cost; no teacher or method can guarantee a score or a particular timeline.

For a learner-facing self-check, ask them to explain the method from memory, apply it to a new item, point to passage evidence, and identify why tempting alternatives fail. The fourth video explicitly defines mastery as being able to teach the steps without notes and then apply them automatically; retain this as a practical retrieval check, not the only valid definition of mastery. ([3:00–5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M_NP2v7rV8Y&t=180s))

## Keep the claims in proportion

- The official Academic Reading test has 40 questions in 60 minutes. IELTS describes the question types as testing different reading skills, including detailed and main-point understanding; it also describes skimming and scanning followed by closer reading for relevant detail. The same core test time and question count are published for General Training, whose texts differ. ([IELTS Academic Reading format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading); [IELTS General Training Reading format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-general-training-test/ielts-general-training-format-reading))
- It is sensible to use verified, level-appropriate materials and to avoid practicing unreliable answer keys. The source’s blanket claim that online tests are unreliable is not demonstrated; official IELTS samples and other reputable materials are available. ([IELTS Academic sample questions](https://www.ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/sample-test-questions/academic-test))
- Vocabulary supports comprehension, but Reading is not simply a vocabulary test. Strategy, text understanding, locating evidence, task rules, and time all affect performance. Do not assume that an answer error is a vocabulary gap without checking the evidence.
- Self-marking with a trustworthy key can expose patterns, especially when a learner records the passage evidence and reason for each miss. Some errors still need teacher modeling or feedback; the claim that learners can improve Reading entirely on their own is too broad.
- The video’s figures (“95%” have one mistake, three failed attempts before joining the course, and a six-week average improvement) have no documented samples or methods. Its claim that fixing one “tire” will produce the target score is a guarantee and is not supported by the lesson.
- The advice not to book until an instructor says to do so, the claims about wasted fees and fixed timelines, the VIP discount and limited places, and the diagnosis analogy are part of the sales pitch, not exam policy. ([16:36–20:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=luafA46SVvE&t=996s))


## Companion 2026 diagnostic: match the error pattern to the repair

The second video reinforces the value of reviewing wrong answers and shows why the repair should fit the pattern rather than defaulting to a generic Reading course. Use the instructor’s cases as diagnostic hypotheses to check against more than one fresh set, not as guaranteed explanations for every learner. ([0:00–1:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UfdDaYv8Qp4&t=0s))

- **Errors cluster in a small number of task families:** inspect whether the learner is applying the task rule correctly, then teach and practise those specific methods. The video illustrates this with True/False/Not Given and matching headings. ([2:22–3:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UfdDaYv8Qp4&t=142s))
- **Accuracy falls mainly in Section 3:** check passage comprehension and vocabulary, then verify whether the learner ran out of time. The instructor uses Section 3 as the harder-text example; do not infer a single cause from section location alone. If accuracy is sound with extra time, build speed gradually. The 90→80→70→60-minute ladder is the instructor’s example, not an official IELTS preparation schedule. ([3:22–5:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UfdDaYv8Qp4&t=202s))
- **The learner locates the right answer but writes it incorrectly:** compare answer selection with the spelling of the entered word. If selection is consistently right and only spelling changes the result, practise spelling and answer transcription instead of assigning more strategy or vocabulary work. ([5:28–7:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UfdDaYv8Qp4&t=328s))
- **Errors recur across matching tasks:** diagnose the operation that failed. Was the learner unable to locate the relevant passage area (scanning), form a paragraph or passage gist (skimming), or interpret the evidence closely? “I get lost” or “I run out of time” may describe the experience without identifying its cause. ([7:05–8:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UfdDaYv8Qp4&t=425s))

The video recommends one unseen official practice test as a starting point and prefers two or three under exam conditions. For a robust course diagnosis, use multiple observations where feasible, review every miss with its text evidence, and check the proposed cause in targeted practice. Official material is useful for reliable task format and answer keys; the source’s claim that this method works only with official tests is not established. Its success figures, “always a reason” framing, and course outcomes are promotional or overgeneralized claims, not evidence that every performance pattern has one certain cause. ([8:14–9:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UfdDaYv8Qp4&t=494s))

## Companion: choose the reading mode the task needs

[Band 5.0 to 8.0 in IELTS Reading Using One Simple Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKYHb9MjxWs) argues that fluent English and careful general reading do not automatically produce strong IELTS Reading performance. Its example learner reads every word as if checking a medical record, leaving too little time to return to the questions. The useful distinction is between understanding an entire passage and locating and interpreting the evidence needed for a particular answer. ([0:00–2:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKYHb9MjxWs&t=0s))

- **Keep the three operations, not the percentages.** The instructor labels careful reading, scanning, and skimming as roughly 10%, 50%, and 40% of the test. Treat those shares as his illustrative heuristic, not a measured allocation or IELTS rule. In the course, teach the more actionable sequence: skim to form a gist, scan to locate likely evidence, and close-read to decide whether it actually answers the question.
- **For matching headings, name the paragraph before comparing choices.** Read for the paragraph’s central idea, write a short headline in your own words, then compare that meaning with the options. Reject choices that match only a supporting detail. The video gives learners about 15 seconds for its example; the transferable routine is gist-first comparison, not a universal 15-second limit. ([4:13–6:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKYHb9MjxWs&t=253s))
- **Practise the operation until it is usable under test pressure.** Watching a strategy demonstration or downloading a checklist is not practice. Have learners apply the steps to new questions, explain their decisions, and repeat until the method is increasingly fluent; then add timing. This reinforces the practice loop above without treating automaticity as a band-score guarantee. ([6:52–8:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKYHb9MjxWs&t=412s))

The reported learner’s movement from Band 5 to Band 8 is an anecdote, not independent evidence that a single strategy causes that gain. The video's assertion that Cambridge books are the only fully reliable practice source is also broader than its evidence; anchor format and answer-key checks in official IELTS samples and use other materials only when their provenance and keys are trustworthy. The later course offer and limited-place pitch are sales content, not exam guidance. ([8:24–10:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKYHb9MjxWs&t=504s))

The 31-second [True, False, Not Given Short](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/WT9Z_fD9ChE) illustrates the evidence test: a statement is True when it agrees with the passage, False when the passage contradicts it, and Not Given when neither relationship is established. ### Learner review: matching headings and True/False/Not Given

The 14-minute [Reading-advice interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vCRU-Skq4Ak) repeats a useful matching-headings routine: read enough of the paragraph to identify its main idea, draft a short gist heading in your own words, then compare that idea with the options. The learner’s “basement versus floor” example illustrates how assumptions about wording can distract from the text. ## Companion: train the operation, then add time pressure

The 10-minute video reinforces the same skill-building sequence: recognize that question families demand different operations, practise a new procedure slowly until learners can carry it out reliably, and increase speed through further practice. It distinguishes skimming for general meaning, scanning for a location, and close reading to interpret the evidence; the point is to select and combine them to fit the question, not to apply a single “skim and scan” shortcut everywhere. Matching headings generally needs a strong grasp of paragraph gist, while a statement-matching task may require locating and closely checking specific information. ## Companion: diagnose Reading myths and select the operation

In [Get Band 9 After Using These Reading Strategies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0RxRl9allI), the instructor argues that unstructured repetition and generic time-management tips cannot substitute for reading skill. The practical lesson is to practise a sound method, review evidence, and diagnose the actual bottleneck; timed work still matters for learning to meet the official limit and for measuring readiness. The instructor’s stories, average-score comparison, and 20-minute Band 9 anecdote are illustrative, not independently verified evidence. ([0:00–3:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0RxRl9allI&t=0s))

The video distinguishes five operations: understand the question and directions, skim for general meaning, scan to locate a relevant area, close-read the evidence, and decide among the answer options. Teach learners to combine these operations as the task requires. Matching headings asks for paragraph-level main ideas: read a paragraph, write a short gist title in plain language, then compare that meaning with the heading options. True/False/Not Given depends on close comparison between each statement and the passage; use the text rather than prior knowledge. Multiple choice calls for careful comparison of options and elimination of distractors. These are useful teaching procedures, not a claim that every task has one inflexible route. ([3:26–11:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0RxRl9allI&t=206s); [official Academic Reading format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading))

The instructor calls Reading a vocabulary or synonym test and shows paraphrases such as “civil/municipal” and “equal halves/12-hour periods.” Preserve the central point: learners must recognize meaning across paraphrases. Build that ability through sustained reading at an appropriate level, noticing unfamiliar words in context, inferring cautiously, checking a reliable definition, and recording useful vocabulary with context and an example. Do not replace contextual learning with an unreviewed word list, but flashcards and word lists can support learning when they include retrieval, examples, and spaced review. The video's fixed “8–12 words per day” limit and claims about how quickly people forget decontextualized words are not established by the transcript and should not be used as course rules. ([11:24–14:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0RxRl9allI&t=684s))

## Companion: evaluate advice with task evidence

Two Glasp-recovered videos, [IELTS Reading Tips + Tricks: Ultimate Guide 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G8Y2liwGRl8) and [How Do I Get Band 9 in IELTS Reading?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Uc4VRwGThE), overlap with the task-specific methods above but make the evaluation process more concrete. Before teaching a tip, identify the question’s reading demand, model the procedure on an official-style item, and check whether learners can apply it and explain their evidence. The videos contrast a generic skim → scan → close-read routine with a gist-first matching-headings procedure and show learners becoming lost with the generic routine. Treat this as a classroom demonstration, not a controlled comparison or proof that a single approach fails for every learner. ([G8Y2liwGRl8: 0:00–3:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G8Y2liwGRl8&t=0s); [1Uc4VRwGThE: 2:24–7:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Uc4VRwGThE&t=144s))

For matching headings, read a paragraph, form a concise title in your own words, then compare its main idea with the options. For True/False/Not Given, anchor judgments in the passage: True agrees, False contradicts, and Not Given cannot be established from the supplied information. The second video’s personal fact-sheet exercise (write known facts, then generate an agreeing, contradicting, and unverified statement) is a simple way to teach the distinction before returning to passage-based examples. It does not replace close reading or the full question procedure. ([G8Y2liwGRl8: 8:50–11:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G8Y2liwGRl8&t=530s); [1Uc4VRwGThE: 10:09–16:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Uc4VRwGThE&t=609s); [official IELTS definitions](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading))

Skimming and scanning are useful operations, not answer-finding substitutes. Their order and value vary with the item; locating a likely paragraph is not the same as interpreting the evidence or choosing between plausible options. Keep timing in the curriculum: develop accurate methods first, then use timed practice to diagnose pacing and prepare for the 60-minute test. Avoid claims that strategy eliminates the need for reading skill or that generic time advice is never useful.
