# CH74 — Staying in a Hotel

**Outcome:** Check in, ask about the room and services, report a problem, and review the bill at checkout.  

## Study

A **reservation** is a booking. **Key card** opens a room electronically. **Front desk** is the reception point. **Check-in** starts the stay; **checkout** ends it. A **crib** is a baby bed; **two beds** is clearer than assuming a room label describes the arrangement you need.

### Arrive and verify

**Guest:** Hi, I have a reservation under Jordan Patel for three nights.  
**Employee:** Welcome. Could I see your ID, please?  
**Guest:** Here it is. I paid through the booking site. Could you confirm what's already paid?  
**Employee:** Let me check the booking record.  
**Guest:** And is the room ready?  
**Employee:** Yes. Please complete this registration form, then I'll give you the key cards.  
**Guest:** Could I borrow a pen?  

A **registration form** records guest details. This model shows requests at one fictional hotel, not a nationwide ID or payment rule. If something differs: “My confirmation says two beds. Could you check that?”

### Ask about the stay

Fictional hotel card: Room 412; three nights at $120 per night; breakfast included, 7:00–10:00 a.m.; checkout 11:00 a.m.; pool hours available at the desk; optional room-service sandwich $18. The exercise bill below does not add taxes or other charges.

**Guest:** What time is breakfast?  
**Employee:** Seven to ten, in the restaurant.  
**Guest:** Is it a buffet?  
**Employee:** Yes. Choose a table, and help yourself.  
**Guest:** What time is checkout?  
**Employee:** Eleven.  
**Guest:** Could I leave my luggage here afterward?  
**Employee:** I'll check our storage arrangements for you.  

“Help yourself” here invites you to take food. It is not a request to solve a problem. Ask “Is breakfast included?” if your own booking is unclear. “Am I too late for breakfast?” asks whether the service is still available.

### Request something and handle a problem

**Guest:** Could we have two extra pillows and fresh towels?  
**Employee:** Certainly. What's your room number?  
**Guest:** Four twelve. Also, the room is too hot and the window won't close.  
**Employee:** I'll ask maintenance to check it.  
**Guest:** Thank you. About when can someone come?  

Other reports: “The lamp isn't working.” “The shower is leaking.” “The Wi-Fi password doesn't seem to work.” Other requests: “Is a hair dryer available?” “Could I arrange a wake-up call for seven?” “Can I order a sandwich to the room?” Availability belongs to the hotel, so a request may receive an alternative instead of a yes.

If the problem continues:

**Guest:** I reported the noise earlier, and it's still keeping me awake.  
**Employee:** I'm sorry about that. Let me see what we can do.  
**Guest:** Could I move to a quieter room?  
**Employee:** I'll check availability.  

“This isn't acceptable to me” is firmer than “It's a little noisy.” You can express a real complaint without minimizing it. **A little / a bit** can reduce the strength of a description; choose them when that matches what you mean, rather than automatically softening every problem.

### Check out and check the bill

**Employee:** How was your stay?  
**Guest:** The staff were helpful, but the noise was a problem.  
**Employee:** Here's the itemized bill.  
**Guest:** The room total is three hundred sixty, plus eighteen for room service. What's this other charge?  
**Employee:** Let me check it.  
**Guest:** Please do. I don't recognize it.  

**Itemized** means charges are listed separately. You can ask for a **receipt**, request “Could I stay one more night?” or ask “Could you help arrange a taxi?” Do not assume an extension or fee removal is available merely because you ask.

## Practice

Use the fictional card. You have requested two beds and have an $18 room-service charge. A later bill lists $360 room charges, $18 room service, and an unexplained $25 charge.

1. Check in and confirm payment and the bed arrangement. Partner says “Let me check.” Name the details needing confirmation.
2. Ask when breakfast ends and whether luggage storage is possible after checkout. Partner uses the card and checks the unknown storage condition.
3. Report the hot room and window problem. Partner offers a maintenance visit. Ask about timing.
4. Review the bill, identify the known subtotal of $378, and ask about the extra $25. You do not have to guess its cause.

Solo: speak both sides of one stage, then hide the guest model and make your own request. Later change the problem and desired outcome.

**Possibilities:** “My confirmation says two beds.” “Could someone check the window?” “What is the additional twenty-five-dollar charge for?” Check that staff can identify your booking, room, need, and disputed detail.
