# CH38 — At the Hardware Store

**Outcome:** Describe a home project, ask where to find supplies, and ask for clarification about a product.  

## Study

A **hardware store** sells tools and supplies for home projects. You can ask about an unfamiliar item by explaining your goal: **I want to hang a picture** or **I'm planning to paint a bedroom**. The goal may be clearer than an incorrect product name.

Useful project verbs are **paint** a wall, **sand** a surface, **tile** an area, **fill** a crack, and **hang** a picture.

A **hammer** is a tool for striking, including driving nails. A **screwdriver** turns screws. A **drill** makes holes and may also turn screws with an appropriate attachment. A **saw** has a toothed edge or blade for cutting. A **nail** is a pointed fastener usually driven into material; a **screw** has a spiral thread and is turned into place. Both attach things, but they are different items. These meanings help you identify what someone names; ask which product suits your particular project.

### Getting to the right place

**Customer:** Excuse me, where can I find picture-hanging supplies?  
**Employee:** Aisle six. What are you working on?  
**Customer:** I want to hang a framed picture. I'm not sure which product I need.  
**Employee:** Our hardware specialist can help compare the options.  
**Customer:** Great. Who should I ask?  
**Employee:** Jordan, at the counter at the end of the aisle.  

**Where can I find...?** asks for a location. **Who should I ask about...?** asks for a suitable person. **Could you show me where it is?** requests more help than an aisle number.

### Explaining what is missing

**I'm looking for a cordless drill** names a product. **I need something for filling a small crack** describes a use. **What would you recommend for this project?** asks for a recommendation. If the task involves a material or surface, describe it or say you do not know: **I'm not sure what the wall is made of.** You do not have to invent technical details to keep talking.

**Customer:** I'm looking for paint for a bathroom.  
**Employee:** Is it for the walls or the ceiling?  
**Customer:** The walls. I'm repainting them.  
**Employee:** Let's check which products are intended for that use.  
**Customer:** What does this label mean?  
**Employee:** Which part would you like me to explain?  
**Customer:** This line about the surface.  

**Intended for** describes the product's stated purpose. You can ask **Does this work with the material I described?** or **What information do you need from me?** An answer should come from the actual product information and relevant expertise.

### Price and availability

**Do you have this in stock?** asks whether it is available. **Is there a smaller size?** asks for another quantity. **What's the difference between these two?** requests comparison. To decline: **That costs more than I want to spend. I'll keep looking.** To pause a decision: **I'll check the measurements and come back.**

## Practice

For these fictional store facts, nails and screws are in aisle 3, painting supplies in aisle 5, and a tool-advice counter beside aisle 3. Speak the employee's part too when practicing alone.

1. Ask where to find screws. You do not understand **aisle three**; request clarification or ask the employee to show you.
2. You want to paint a bathroom but do not know the correct product. Explain the project. The employee asks **Walls or ceiling?** Reply **The ceiling**, then ask which information they need.
3. Two fictional cans are priced at $18 and $32. Ask what differs. The employee says **They are intended for different surfaces.** Request an explanation rather than choosing only by price.

**Sample possibilities:** “Where can I find screws?” — “Aisle three.” — “Could you show me?” / “I'm planning to paint my bathroom ceiling. What information do you need?” / “What's the difference between these?” — “They're for different surfaces.” — “Could you explain which surface each one is for?”  

Later, change the project and supplies. Check that the listener knows your goal and what you still need to understand.
