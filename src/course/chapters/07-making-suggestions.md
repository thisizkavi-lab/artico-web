# CH07 — Making Suggestions and Invitations

**Your goal:** Propose an activity, invite someone, and respond with acceptance, a refusal, uncertainty or another idea.  

## Study: Put forward an idea

| Pattern | Original example |
| --- | --- |
| **Let's + action** | **Let's take a walk.** |
| **We could + action** | **We could watch a movie.** |
| **How about + activity?** | **How about getting lunch?** |
| **Why don't we + action?** | **Why don't we meet at the park?** |

**We could** presents a possibility. **Let's** proposes something to do together. In **Why don't we...?**, the main purpose here is to suggest, rather than ask why you have failed to do something.

**Choosing what to do**

**Noah:** What should we do after class?  
**Quinn:** We could get lunch.  
**Noah:** Good idea. How about trying the noodle place?  
**Quinn:** Sounds good.  

**Good idea** approves the suggestion. **Sounds good** can accept the proposed plan. Chapter 5 helps you describe preferences; here you use them to reach a plan with someone.

## Study: Invite a person

**Would you like to come to dinner?** asks whether someone wants to join. **Do you want to join us?** is another clear invitation. Informal **Are you up for a game tonight?** asks whether they feel willing or interested.

**Quinn:** We're having a game night on Saturday. Do you want to join us?  
**Noah:** Sure, I'd like that.  
**Quinn:** Great. I'll send you the details.  

Other accepting responses include **I'm in**, **Count me in**, and **I'd love to**. In a plan with several people, **Count me in** asks to be included. You still need enough details to know what you have accepted; precise scheduling belongs to Chapter 12.

## Study: Decline or keep the answer open

You can decline without inventing enthusiasm or an excuse. Choose what you want the other person to understand.

- **No, thanks.** A short refusal.
- **I'd rather not.** You prefer not to do it.
- **I don't feel like going out tonight.** You do not want that activity now.
- **I'll pass this time.** You are not joining this occasion.
- **I can't make it.** You cannot attend.
- **Maybe. Let me check my schedule.** You have not decided or confirmed.

**An invitation and an alternative**

**Quinn:** Are you up for a movie tonight?  
**Noah:** I'll pass tonight. I'm tired. How about Sunday?  
**Quinn:** Sunday works for me.  
**Noah:** Great. Let's choose a movie later.  

An alternative is useful when you want another plan. It is optional if you simply want to say no. If the other person declines, **Okay, no problem** acknowledges that answer. **Maybe** and **Let me check** are not confirmed yeses.

**A clearer boundary**

**Quinn:** Want to go dancing?  
**Noah:** No, I don't want to go dancing.  
**Quinn:** Okay. What would you like to do instead?  
**Noah:** I'd rather stay home tonight.  

This is a direct exchange. Its meaning comes from the words, relationship and delivery; directness is not automatically an insult.

## Practice: Find a plan—or accept there isn't one

Use this fictional activity card. On your own, read the invitation and speak the reply, then rehearse the inviter's response.

| Activity | Scene information |
| --- | --- |
| Walk | Saturday afternoon; outside |
| Movie at home | Saturday evening; a quiet activity |
| Game night | Sunday evening; with several friends |

1. Suggest the walk using two different patterns. Your partner answers **Sounds good**. Acknowledge the acceptance.
2. Invite a friend to game night. Their role wants to join. Rehearse a complete three-turn exchange.
3. Your role dislikes games and wants a quiet evening. Decline game night and suggest the movie instead. Support: **Games aren't my thing**, **I'd rather...**, **How about...?**
4. Change the response: the friend says **Maybe. Let me check**. Answer without treating it as a firm acceptance. Support: **Sure. Let me know**.
5. Change your intention: you do not want any alternative plan this weekend. Give a clear refusal and let the inviter acknowledge it.
6. Hide the activity card. Make one invitation with invented details. Give a different answer from the one in the model and continue coherently.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Let's take a walk Saturday afternoon. / How about taking a walk? — Sounds good. — Great.**
2. **Do you want to join us for game night? — Sure. Count me in. — Great. I'll send you the details.**
3. **I'll pass on game night. How about a movie at home instead? — That works for me.**
4. **Sure. Let me know.**
5. **No, thanks. I don't want to make plans this weekend. — Okay, no problem.**

**Check:** Was the activity clear? Could the inviter tell whether the answer was yes, no or unconfirmed? Did a refusal remain a refusal even when no explanation was given?
