# CH69 — Bikes and Cycling

**Outcome:** Explain what kind of bike you need, arrange a rental, and describe a problem without guessing a repair.  

## Study

A **commuter bike** is used for trips such as going to work. An **e-bike** has electric assistance. Your **budget** is the amount you intend to spend. Tell the salesperson about the trip, not only the bike's appearance.

**Customer:** I'm looking for a bike for my commute.  
**Salesperson:** How far do you ride, and what's your budget?  
**Customer:** About three miles each way. I'd like to stay under six hundred dollars.  
**Salesperson:** Will you carry it upstairs or take it on transit?  
**Customer:** Upstairs, yes. Weight matters.  
**Salesperson:** Would you like to try this one?  
**Customer:** Yes. Could you explain the gears first?  

“I mainly need it for getting around town” describes purpose. “I'd prefer something lighter” expresses a preference. “That's more than I want to spend” is a clear limit; it is not a personal attack on the salesperson.

### Parts you can name

- **Seat / saddle:** where you sit.
- **Handlebars:** the bars used to steer.
- **Pedals:** the parts pushed with your feet.
- **Chain:** the linked metal loop transmitting movement.
- **Gears:** parts that change the relationship between pedaling and wheel movement.
- **Brakes:** parts used to slow or stop.
- **Tire:** the outer part around a wheel; **spokes** connect the wheel's center and rim.
- **Light / battery / charger:** useful equipment words, especially when discussing an e-bike.

### Rent and return

**Visitor:** Can I rent a bike for two hours?  
**Employee:** Yes. Do you want a regular bike or an e-bike?  
**Visitor:** A regular bike. How do I unlock it?  
**Employee:** We unlock the bike for you here.  
**Visitor:** Where do I return it, and how do I know the rental has ended?  
**Employee:** Bring it back to this station. At the desk, we close the rental and give you a receipt marked “Returned.” That confirms the rental has ended.  
**Visitor:** So I need to bring it to the desk and get the marked receipt?  
**Employee:** Yes.  

This is an invented rental service. “Can I leave it anywhere?” asks about a rule; it does not assume free parking. “Is a helmet available?” asks about equipment. App access, fees, and return rules vary.

### Report what you notice

**Employee:** What's up with the bike?  
**Rider:** The front tire keeps going flat, and the brake feels loose.  
**Employee:** When did you notice that?  
**Rider:** This morning. Could you check it?  

Here *What's up with the bike?* asks about a problem. It is not a greeting. Useful reports include “The chain came off,” “The light isn't working,” and “The battery won't charge.” Say “I don't know what's causing it” when you do not know. The lesson teaches reporting, not how to repair or safely operate a faulty bike.

## Practice

Choose a role: commuter, three miles each way, stairs, $600 budget; or visitor, two-hour regular-bike rental. For this invented rental, staff unlock the bike at the station. Return it to that station’s desk; staff close the rental and give you a receipt marked “Returned.”

1. Explain your need. Partner asks about distance, budget, or bike type. Use the role details.
2. Ask the rental employee about unlocking and returning. Partner explains: “We unlock it for you here. Bring it back to this desk; we close the rental and give you a receipt marked ‘Returned.’” Confirm both the return location and the confirmation you should receive.
3. Report a flat front tire. Partner asks “When did it start?” Answer “This morning,” then request a check.

Solo: supply each customer turn after reading the employee's prompt, then swap roles. Later change one need or fault.

**Possibilities:** “I need something light enough to carry upstairs.” “So I bring it back to this desk and get a receipt marked ‘Returned’?” “Could you check the front tire?” Check whether your purpose, limit, or observed fault is clear; a technical diagnosis is not required.
