# CH77 — At the Beach

**Outcome:** Ask about beach conditions and facilities, arrange an activity, and choose whether to participate.  

## Study

A **lifeguard** supervises water safety at a designated area. **Warning flags** communicate local conditions or restrictions; ask what they mean at that beach. A flag system or another person's assurance should not be turned into a universal guarantee of safe swimming.

### Check the conditions

**Visitor:** Is the swimming area open today?  
**Lifeguard:** Not at the moment. The conditions are rough.  
**Visitor:** What do the flags mean here?  
**Lifeguard:** The posted sign explains them. Today, stay out of the water.  
**Visitor:** Okay. Where can we check for updates?  
**Lifeguard:** At this stand.  

This is a fictional local notice. The U.S. National Weather Service describes beach flags as information posted by local beach authorities about current hazards. Ask the lifeguard where swimming is allowed today; flag meanings and marked swimming areas depend on the beach.

### Choose a place and an activity

**Friend:** Want to go swimming?  
**Visitor:** Not with the water closed. Let's find some shade instead.  
**Friend:** There's a shady spot by the walkway.  
**Visitor:** Great. Could we rent beach chairs?  

**Shade** means shelter from direct sunlight. A **beach chair** is a chair for use at the beach. A **bucket** and **shovel** can be used for sand play. A **beach ball** is a ball used for beach games.

A **surfboard** is a board used to ride waves, usually standing up. A **bodyboard** is a shorter board typically ridden lying down. A **paddleboard** is a board moved with a paddle. A **wetsuit** is a close-fitting suit that helps keep a person warm in water. The surfing and paddleboarding meanings connect with Chapter 72. A **pedal boat** moves by pedaling. If you do not know an activity, ask what it involves rather than equating all equipment.

### Ask about lessons or rentals

**Visitor:** Do you offer beginner surfing lessons?  
**Employee:** Yes, but today's sessions are canceled.  
**Visitor:** Could we book for another day?  
**Employee:** Tomorrow at ten is an option, subject to conditions.  
**Visitor:** Is equipment included, and how long is the lesson?  
**Employee:** In this offer, a board and wetsuit are included. It's an hour.  
**Visitor:** What happens if the conditions are unsuitable tomorrow?  
**Employee:** I'll explain our cancellation arrangements.  

Ask “Do you have a wetsuit in a child's size?” “How much is the rental?” or “When does it need to be returned?” Prices, requirements, and availability are particular to the operator.

You may say “I'm excited to try it,” “I'd rather watch today,” or “That's outside my comfort level.” Your decision does not need to copy a friend's enthusiasm.

## Practice

Fictional beach card: swimming closed now; updates at the lifeguard stand; beach-chair rental available; today's lessons canceled; possible beginner lesson tomorrow at ten, one hour, board and wetsuit included; cancellation terms not yet explained.

1. Ask about swimming and the flags. Partner uses the card. Tell a companion your alternative plan.
2. Ask about tomorrow's lesson, equipment, and cancellation terms. Partner gives known details and offers to explain the missing condition.
3. Decline the activity or accept only after asking your question. Use your own comfort level or a role.

Solo: speak both roles, then hide your visitor turns and respond to the employee prompts. Later change the activity to a paddleboard rental and ask for its own conditions.

**Possibilities:** “Let's stay on the beach today.” “Is the wetsuit included?” “I'd rather watch this time.” Check that the condition, question, and choice are clear. No water-safety skill or real rental is being tested.
