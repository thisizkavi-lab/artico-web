## Distilled method

The durable idea is **measure → prioritize → practise the specific gap → apply it in full writing → check again**. A learner who repeatedly writes complete essays without correcting the cause of a recurring problem may reinforce that problem. The instructor’s article-error example and Andrea’s word-order, article, and preposition examples illustrate narrowing a broad label (“grammar”) to observable, repairable targets; her account also models checking those patterns in a fresh draft. ([6:57–8:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4p4s5tz9Bk&t=417s); [10:45–12:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZAZ4YBHDsnk&t=645s); [28:45–30:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZAZ4YBHDsnk&t=1725s))

1. **Set the target correctly.** Identify the required overall score and any minimum score in each skill. The companion lesson frames the goal as reaching the required score efficiently, rather than studying to satisfy someone else or to display memorized language. Keep the actual score constraints visible when planning. ([3:02–4:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4p4s5tz9Bk&t=182s); [4:48–5:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSzJRe1SoWU&t=288s))
2. **Use diagnostic evidence to allocate study time.** Review recent, reliable results and samples against the skill requirements and Writing criteria. If another skill consistently meets its requirement, reduce its share of study time while retaining enough practice to maintain it. The companion suggests dropping any module that scored half a band above target and says this could cut study time by 75%; treat that as an unverified shortcut, not a rule. One score does not establish stable mastery. ([5:25–6:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4p4s5tz9Bk&t=325s); [5:52–6:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSzJRe1SoWU&t=352s))
3. **Name a specific, high-impact gap.** Move from a broad label to an observable pattern: unclear paragraph progression, an incomplete response, inaccurate word choice, or recurring article errors. Andrea’s feedback identified word order affected by Spanish and recurring problems with articles and prepositions. A brief personal error log can make the target visible; specific feedback can help a learner distinguish a grammar issue from a sentence that is unclear or irrelevant. Criterion-based feedback can help, but learners can also begin with public descriptors, comparison samples, and structured self-review; a paid service or former examiner is not an exam requirement. ([6:20–8:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4p4s5tz9Bk&t=380s); [6:35–8:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSzJRe1SoWU&t=395s); [8:00–12:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZAZ4YBHDsnk&t=480s))
4. **Practise the repair directly before another full simulation.** Use short exercises that isolate the identified skill, review the error pattern, and repeat until the change is more reliable. Turn feedback into action: annotate the pattern, practise corrected forms, revise the passage or essay, and check for transfer in a new response. Writing more full essays without examining recurring errors may simply repeat them. Keep enough whole-task writing to preserve task understanding and timed control, while making targeted practice the main work on the gap. ([8:08–9:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4p4s5tz9Bk&t=488s); [8:22–9:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSzJRe1SoWU&t=502s); [32:45–37:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZAZ4YBHDsnk&t=1965s))
5. **Return to timed writing in stages and re-diagnose.** The first lesson gives a 90 → 60 → 50 → 45 → 40-minute example; the companion suggests starting near one hour and reducing by five-minute steps toward 40. These are coaching examples, not validated schedules. Set the pace from the learner’s baseline, practise Task 2 near 40 minutes, and also practise the complete 60-minute Writing test, including Task 1. ([9:24–10:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4p4s5tz9Bk&t=564s); [9:08–9:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSzJRe1SoWU&t=548s))

## Scoring correction: do not teach “the weakest criterion sets the band”

The bottle analogy is useful for showing why a conspicuous weakness deserves attention, but both videos oversimplify the scoring when they imply that a candidate must reach the target band on every criterion or that the weakest criterion alone determines the result. Official IELTS guidance says each task is assessed independently; the four criteria within a task carry equal weight and are averaged. Task 2 then carries more weight than Task 1 in the final Writing score. A low criterion can pull down a task average, but it is not a strict ceiling on the final Writing band. ([1:23–2:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4p4s5tz9Bk&t=83s); [3:22–4:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSzJRe1SoWU&t=202s); [official IELTS Writing scoring](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail))

Teach the four criteria by task:

- **Task 1:** Task Achievement, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy.
- **Task 2:** Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy.

Each criterion is equally weighted *within its task*. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1. ([IELTS Writing resources](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/writing-test-resources))

The videos’ readiness rule—do not book until you consistently reach the target score on Task 2 essays within 40 minutes—is instructor advice, not IELTS policy. Official timing is 60 minutes for the full Writing test, not 40 minutes for Task 2 alone. Use 40 minutes as a preparation target for Task 2, then check readiness with full timed tests and the skill minimums required by the receiving organisation. ([10:43–11:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4p4s5tz9Bk&t=643s); [9:08–9:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSzJRe1SoWU&t=548s); [IELTS Academic Writing format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-writing))

- The claimed school-wide increase from average 5.5 to 7.5 “in just weeks,” the claim that scores improved “overnight,” and the claim that the method has transformed thousands of students are not independently supported in this video. ## Adaptation for the course

After each diagnostic, record strengths and priority gaps by skill and criterion, then choose a repair target with clear evidence and likely impact. Teach it, practise it in focused examples, apply it in a complete response, and check the new work against the same criteria. Track progress across more than one sample. Use separate practice targets for Task 1 and Task 2, preserve maintenance work in already secure skills, and reserve full-test practice to check transfer under the actual 60-minute Writing conditions.

## Companion: turn a learner's recurring errors into a small repair plan

[He Failed IELTS Writing 10 Times (Then Did This)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tJxFwm2B1Io) describes a learner who added vocabulary lists but continued making avoidable grammar, spelling, and word-choice errors. The useful diagnostic move was to inspect several recent essays, identify recurring singular/plural agreement and tense issues, keep a short list, and deliberately check those patterns in later writing. A learner can also replace a word whose meaning or use is uncertain with a clearer, controlled option. Keep the source's core priorities—answer the prompt, make the meaning clear, and work on demonstrated errors—but do not adopt its absolute “100% certain” vocabulary rule: the official Lexical Resource criterion also values range and flexibility when accurate and appropriate. ([0:00–3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tJxFwm2B1Io&t=0s); [3:45–6:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tJxFwm2B1Io&t=225s))

The learner also practises thinking before drafting: consider more than one relevant perspective and ask what evidence or example could support each point. This is a way to generate options, not a requirement to include both sides in every essay or memorize arguments. Writing prompts still determine the position and coverage required. His reported 10 essays in a week and 15-minute error hunt are personal study quantities, not recommended quotas. ([6:00–8:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tJxFwm2B1Io&t=360s); [8:00–10:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tJxFwm2B1Io&t=480s))

Two further learner reviews, [From Band 6.5 to 7.0 Using One Simple Method](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVjyI4fB6LY) and [IELTS Advantage VIP Writing Review: 6.5 3 Times to Band 7.5 Success](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oKRN18_FTUQ), overlap with the existing Task 2 plan/write/check notes. ### Cross-skill learner examples: answer the task and control the input

[Divya’s learner review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HYE-Y1jPH-M) describes getting conflicting advice to use impressive words and avoid repeating any word. That advice consumed time and diverted attention from grammar, revision, and clear meaning. The useful course lesson is to prioritize a relevant answer, accurate language, and readable sentences; vary wording where a natural alternative fits, but do not distort meaning to avoid repetition. Her experience of receiving more detailed corrections is one account of feedback, not evidence that only a paid service can provide it. Her scores and the VIP-course effect are self-reported. ([2:00–6:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HYE-Y1jPH-M&t=120s); [8:00–12:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HYE-Y1jPH-M&t=480s))

[From 6.5 to a Perfect 9.0 Using One Simple Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L5J3UJ_Z0KE) adds a cross-skill warning against answering only the general subject: identify the actual instruction and specific scope, then organize the response around those demands. The learner describes being pulled between contradictory online rules and adding language beyond her control. Keep clear, accurate language and a consistent evidence-based method; do not turn “keep it simple” into a ban on appropriate range or tell strong learners they have no language gaps without reviewing their work. See the [Reading error-analysis note](reading-practice-error-analysis.md) for the companion point about selecting Reading methods by task. ([1:00–4:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L5J3UJ_Z0KE&t=60s); [7:00–10:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L5J3UJ_Z0KE&t=420s))

[From Band 5.5 to 7.5 Using One Simple Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1GcJWXFaU0) reinforces a useful learning sequence: quality input, deliberate practice, specific feedback, then focused work on the identified weakness and another attempt.
