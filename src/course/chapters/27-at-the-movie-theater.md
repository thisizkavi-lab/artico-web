# CH27 — At the Movie Theater

**Your goal:** Choose a showing, buy tickets, ask about seating or access, and discuss the movie afterward.  

## Study: Find the showing you want

**Movie theater** names the venue. A **showing** or **screening** is a scheduled presentation of a movie. **Showtime** gives the start time. **Film** also works for a movie; you do not have to replace it in every sentence.

Use this fictional listing in Practice:

| Movie | Rating | Showtime and location | Information |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Hidden Garden | PG | 2 p.m., Theater 1 | 100 minutes; two adjacent seats available in row D |
| The Hidden Garden | PG | 5 p.m., Theater 2 | 100 minutes; open-caption showing; seats available |
| Night Train | PG-13 | 3:15 p.m., Theater 3 | 125 minutes; sold out |
| Night Train | PG-13 | 7 p.m., Theater 3 | 125 minutes; seats available |

**Scene facts:** Theater 1 is on the lobby level. Theater 2 is upstairs, with an elevator beside the lobby. The 2 p.m. showing starts in ten minutes in the first practice scene. Ticket prices and buying procedures are not needed for these tasks.  

**Customer:** What time is the next showing of The Hidden Garden?  
**Staff:** Two o'clock, in Theater 1.  
**Customer:** Two tickets, please. Do you have two seats together?  
**Staff:** Yes, in row D.  
**Customer:** Those are fine, thanks.  

**Sold out** means no tickets remain available for that showing. If your first choice is sold out, ask **What about the next showing?** or choose another movie. **How long is it?** asks about running time; **Which theater is it in?** asks where to go.

## Study: Ask a precise question about needs

You can ask **Are there seats near the back?**, **Is there a step-free route to Theater 2?**, or **Where's the elevator?** The specific answer matters more than an assumption about the venue.

**Customer:** Is the five o'clock showing the one with captions?  
**Staff:** Yes. It's an open-caption showing in Theater 2.  
**Customer:** Where's the elevator to that floor?  
**Staff:** Beside the lobby. It goes to the upstairs theater level.  

**Open captions** appear on the screen for the audience. **Subtitles** is another term you may see in listings, especially for dialogue in another language. Ask **Is this showing subtitled?** or **What captioning is available for this showing?** if the listing does not answer your need. Do not assume every showing has the same features.

Movie listings in the American MPA system use **G**, **PG**, **PG-13**, **R**, and **NC-17**. **PG** signals parental guidance; some material may not suit children. **PG-13** gives a stronger caution about material for children under thirteen. A rating is not a promise that a particular child will enjoy or be comfortable with the movie.

**Is there anything scary in it?**, **What is it rated for?**, and **What's the theater's admission policy?** ask different questions about content and entry. This lesson's fictional ratings do not supply a real review of either movie.

## Study: Before and after the movie

**Is there time to get popcorn?** asks about a plan before the start. A companion can say **We've got ten minutes. Let's go straight to our seats** or **Let's check the line first**. A **line** is people waiting their turn.

Afterward, share an evaluation and let your companion differ:

**Alex:** What did you think?  
**Riley:** The scenery was beautiful, but the story dragged in the middle.  
**Alex:** I liked the slow pace. I thought the ending worked well.  
**Riley:** The ending surprised me, but I didn't really like it.  

**Dragged** suggests it felt slow or boring. **The action scenes blew me away** expresses strong positive surprise. **The acting was awful** gives a strong negative opinion. Neither a good nor a bad review requires agreement from the other person.

## Practice: Choose, ask, and react

Speak both roles alone. Use the listing and scene facts above.

1. Buy two tickets to the next showing of The Hidden Garden at 2 p.m. Ask for seats together. Staff offer the row D pair.
2. Ask about Night Train at 3:15. Staff say it is sold out. Ask about and choose the later showing, or decline.
3. Choose The Hidden Garden's open-caption showing. Confirm the time and ask for the elevator to its floor. Staff answer from the scene facts.
4. Change the need: you are considering a movie for a child and want information about frightening content. Ask that question rather than equate the rating with guaranteed suitability. Staff answer **I don't have a detailed content description here**; choose to find more information before deciding.
5. There are ten minutes until the 2 p.m. showing. Suggest popcorn; the other person wants to go straight to the seats. Respond and agree on a plan.
6. Hide the models. Re-create a ticket exchange with one unavailable choice. Then describe one thing you liked and one you disliked about an imagined movie; a companion disagrees with one opinion.

### Sample possibilities

1. **Two tickets to The Hidden Garden at two, please. Can we sit together?**
2. **Is there space at seven instead? — Yes. — Then I'll take two tickets for that showing.**
3. **Is the five o'clock showing open-captioned? Where's the elevator?**
4. **Is there anything scary in it?** After the supplied answer: **I'll check the content before choosing.**
5. **Let's get popcorn. — I'd rather go to our seats. — Okay, let's do that.**
6. **I loved the scenery, but the story dragged. — I liked the slower pace.**

**Check:** Did the chosen showing still have tickets? Did your question identify the particular access or content information you needed? Could you give a real opinion without requiring the other person to share it?
