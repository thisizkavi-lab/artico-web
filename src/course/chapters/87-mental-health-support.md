# CH87 — Mental Health Support

**Outcome:** Ask for support, describe your experience, and state what you are ready to discuss.  

## Study

**Stressed** describes strain or pressure. **Anxious** describes worry or unease. **Low** describes a down mood; **calm** describes a settled feeling. **Depressed** can describe a feeling in everyday speech or refer to a clinical condition. Feeling low or using the word does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

You can say “I'm struggling,” “I'm having a hard time coping,” or “This is affecting my sleep.” **Cope** means manage a difficult situation. “I've been up and down” means your experience or mood has varied. These are starting points; you decide how much detail to share.

### Ask a person for support

**Person:** Do you have a few minutes? I'm having a hard time and could use someone to talk to.  
**Friend:** I can talk for a bit. What would help right now?  
**Person:** I'd like you to listen. I'm not looking for advice yet.  
**Friend:** Okay. Tell me what you're comfortable sharing.  
**Person:** I've been overwhelmed lately.  

**Overwhelmed** means feeling that demands or emotions are too much. A friend may also say “I care about you, but I can't give this enough attention right now. Could we talk later?” Availability and care are related but not identical. Requesting help does not require forced cheerfulness or immediate disclosure.

### Ask about a service

**Caller:** I'd like to ask about counseling appointments.  
**Employee:** Would you prefer an in-person or video appointment?  
**Caller:** In person, if possible. How do I arrange a first visit?  
**Employee:** I'll explain our intake process.  
**Caller:** What does intake involve? What are the costs and privacy arrangements?  

**Intake** is the initial information-gathering process for a service. **Counselor / therapist** names a professional role; ask about the particular provider or kind of care. Do not assume a walk-in service, insurance coverage, or a confidentiality rule from the word *therapy* alone.

### Describe and set boundaries in a session

**Therapist:** How is this affecting your day?  
**Client:** I'm having trouble concentrating, especially at work.  
**Therapist:** Could you tell me more about that?  
**Client:** I can talk about work, but I'm not ready to discuss my family today.  
**Therapist:** Let's stay with what you're ready to discuss.  

“So what you're saying is…” can introduce a summary. You can respond “Yes, that's close,” or “Not quite. I meant…” A **trigger** can name something that brings a strong reaction; explain what you mean rather than expecting the word to describe everyone's experience.

### In a group

**Facilitator:** Would anyone like to share how the week has been?  
**Participant:** I've had some difficult days, but today feels easier.  
**Facilitator:** Thank you. Would you like to say more?  
**Participant:** I'll leave it there for now.  

“Can I pass for now?” requests not speaking at that point. Group arrangements differ, so ask about expectations rather than treating every group as identical. “These sessions have been helpful for me” reports one person's experience, not a promise that a method helps everyone.

## Practice

Choose a fictional role rather than using private details if you prefer: trouble concentrating at work; wants listening rather than advice; interested in an in-person appointment; not ready to discuss family.

1. Ask a friend for time and state what kind of support you want. Partner asks “What would help?” Answer directly.
2. Ask a service about a first appointment, cost, and privacy. Partner offers an intake explanation. Ask what the word means.
3. Partner summarizes your concern incorrectly as “You're worried about your family.” Clarify your work concern and state your boundary.
4. In a group, share one short update or request to pass.

Solo: read the prompts and speak your turns; then hide the wording and try a different support need.

**Possibilities:** “I'd like you to listen for now.” “Not quite—I'm talking about concentrating at work.” “I'd rather pass this time.” Check that the need, experience, and boundary are clear. These are communication exercises, not therapy or a diagnosis.  

**U.S. immediate-help reference:** For suicidal crisis or emotional distress, call or text **988**, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. For a life-threatening situation, use **911**. Do not call either number for this practice exercise. [NIMH help information](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/find-help).
