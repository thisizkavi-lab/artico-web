# IELTS 2026: question-change claims and delivery facts

**Source:** IELTS Advantage, [IELTS 2026 Changes You MUST Know Before Your Test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oz1ZZL5u36w), published 6 March 2026. The English transcript was copied from Glasp Reader and reviewed through its final available caption at 12:23; the final caption stops mid-promotion. The teaching claims below are paraphrased, with claims about the live test checked against official IELTS information.

## Teaching points worth keeping

The video argues that learners should not redistribute study time based on online claims that certain question types have become common, rare, more scattered, or easier to find. It recommends learning a method for every question family and preparing for the full task range. Its strongest course-design point is task-specificity: a broad operation such as skimming or scanning may help locate material, but the answer still depends on doing the operation the question requires and checking the relevant evidence.

| Skill | Claims discussed in the video | Course-use takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Claims that answers are newly more scattered or organized, or that question distributions have shifted | Do not teach these trend claims as facts without representative evidence. Different task types can have different answer-order patterns; teach the procedure for each task and verify decisions against text evidence. |
| Listening | Claims that map questions are disappearing, Part 3 uses only a few question types, or accents have newly changed | Prepare all task families and practise understanding a variety of accents. The instructor’s historical claim that accents have not changed is not established by the video’s examples; IELTS describes Listening as featuring several English accents. |
| Writing | Claims that opinion or double questions now dominate Task 2 | Learn to identify and answer the prompt in front of the learner; practise all prompt families instead of predicting the next one. |
| Speaking | Monthly topic predictions and advice to prepare memorized answers | Build flexible, relevant answers across familiar and unfamiliar topics. Treat the video’s claim that examiners will spot and discard rehearsed answers as instructor rhetoric; teach natural response to the actual question and use current assessment guidance. |

The instructor repeatedly contrasts real English development with shortcut-chasing. Retain that as a course principle, but omit the video’s sales pitches, guaranteed-result language, and dramatic claims about test failure.

## Correct the blanket “no changes” claim

The video concludes that IELTS “has not changed in 2026.” That is too broad. IELTS announced on **5 March 2026** that paper-based test delivery would end from mid-2026, with exact rollout timing varying by market. In selected markets, candidates can choose to handwrite the Writing component as “Writing on Paper” within an IELTS-on-computer test. IELTS says this update does not change the skills assessed, test construct, or interpretation of results. Course materials should therefore distinguish the tested skills and task requirements from the delivery mode, and direct learners to check the current local booking options and provider instructions before the test. ([official IELTS delivery update](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/updates-to-ielts-test-delivery); [Writing on Paper format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-on-computer-writing-on-paper))

The video also says that there are no new accents. Current IELTS format guidance confirms a range of accents in Listening, but the video does not establish whether the accent mix changed in 2026. Keep the defensible advice—practise varied clear speech—without promising a fixed accent list or asserting a historical change/no-change. ([IELTS Listening format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-listening))

## Course placement

Place this material in **Understand IELTS and set the target** as a short lesson on checking claims and monitoring official updates. Revisit local delivery mode in test-day preparation. In Reading, Listening, and Writing, retain complete task-family coverage; use timed practice and learner error data to decide what needs extra attention rather than unverified frequency predictions.
