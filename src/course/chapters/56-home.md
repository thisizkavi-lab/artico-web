# CH56 — Home

**Outcome:** Recognize selected housing, room and furniture terms and use them to describe a place or need.  

## Study

This vocabulary supports Chapters 57–64. Start with your own setting or one practice home; use the other words as a reference.

### Types of homes and areas

An **apartment** is a home within a building containing other spaces or homes. A **house** is a residential building. A **duplex** can refer to a building divided into two homes in the American housing sense used here. A **townhouse** is a house joined to others in a row. A **cabin** is a small, often simple house, associated with places such as rural or vacation settings.

A **neighborhood** is the surrounding local area. **Upstairs** and **downstairs** describe higher and lower levels. A **basement** is below or partly below ground level. In U.S. building numbering, **first floor** usually refers to the ground-level floor. Check the signs in a particular building rather than assuming every numbering scheme is identical.

### Rooms and parts

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| bedroom | room used for sleeping |
| bathroom | room with a toilet and sink, often also a bathtub or shower |
| kitchen | space for preparing food |
| living room | shared room for relaxing or spending time together |
| dining room | room used for meals |
| hallway | passage connecting rooms |
| laundry room | space for washing clothes |
| garage | space for a vehicle or other storage |
| balcony | outside platform attached to a building |
| yard | outdoor land beside or around a home |

**Roof**, **fence**, **stairs**, and **front door** identify useful parts of a home. A **home office** is space used for working at home.

### Appliances and furniture

**Refrigerator**, often **fridge**, keeps food cold; a **freezer** freezes it. An **oven** cooks food with heat. A **washing machine** washes clothes. A **dishwasher** washes dishes. A **sink** is a basin with water facilities.

Useful furniture includes **bed**, **crib** for a baby, **dresser** with drawers, **closet** for storage, **sofa**, **armchair**, **coffee table**, **bookshelf**, and **dining table**. A **wardrobe** can be a freestanding clothes-storage unit; describe the object when a label is unclear.

Project combinations include **paint the wall**, **repair the fence**, **put up shelves**, **replace the carpet**, and **remove the wallpaper**. They help explain a requested job in Chapters 38 and 61.

## Practice

1. Identify the words: below-ground space; passage between rooms; appliance for clothes; furniture with drawers.
2. Use this practice home: apartment; one bedroom; kitchen and living room; balcony; no garage. Describe it in three sentences.
3. Choose three items you need for a bedroom or kitchen. Explain what each is for.
4. State a home-project request using **We need someone to...** and one combination above.

**Sample possibilities:** **Basement, hallway, washing machine, dresser.** “It's a one-bedroom apartment. It has a kitchen and a living room. There's a balcony, but no garage.” / “We need someone to repair the fence.”  

Cover the lists later and describe the same home using the words you selected. Check that you distinguished room, appliance and furniture instead of relying only on a memorized list.
