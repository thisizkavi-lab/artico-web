# Artico pronunciation syllabus: General American

**Scope:** A practical, internally consistent General American pronunciation reference for learning English. This is a teaching convention, not a claim that all Americans speak identically or that one regional variety is superior.

## 1. The sound inventory in the existing Study chart

The current IPA chart contains **41 teaching entries**: 24 consonants, 12 vowel reference entries, and five vowel glides. The number is **not** a universal phoneme count: sources differ in how they analyze glides, rhotic vowels and reduced vowels. It represents a useful beginner's sound reference rather than every possible phonetic realization.

### Consonants: 24 foundational categories

- Plosives: /p b t d k ɡ/ (6)
- Fricatives: /f v θ ð s z ʃ ʒ h/ (9)
- Affricates: /tʃ dʒ/ (2)
- Nasals: /m n ŋ/ (3)
- Approximants: /w j ɹ l/ (4)

**Total = 24.** Both the category count and common example words are supported by the Groningen American English phonetics course. The consonant *flap* [ɾ] is not counted as an additional underlying consonant in Artico; it is a frequent contextual realization of /t/ and /d/. Some technical phonetics texts list it as an additional phonetic category.

### Vowel symbols: 12 teaching entries

/i ɪ ɛ æ ʌ ə u ʊ ɑ ɔ ɝ ɚ/

The two r-colored forms reflect stressed versus unstressed syllables, not necessarily two separate phonemes. /ə/ is particularly important in unstressed syllables. /ɑ/ and /ɔ/ are listed to help readers understand dictionaries and variation; **many contemporary Americans merge them**, so forcing the contrast is *not* a universal requirement for General American.

### Vowel glides: five teaching entries

/eɪ oʊ aɪ aʊ ɔɪ/

Treating /eɪ/ and /oʊ/ as diphthongs rather than simple /e/ and /o/ is one coherent American-English transcription convention. Other references may use slightly different notation. Do not double-count these as separate symbols elsewhere.

### What does not require another chart entry

- [ɾ]: quick tap/flap in *city*; a common realization of /t/ or /d/.
- [ɫ]: dark L, typically in syllable-final positions; a realization of /l/.
- [pʰ tʰ kʰ]: aspiration of /p t k/ in suitable stressed positions.
- Vowel + /ɹ/: *near, care, car, door, tour*. Teach combinations; don't falsely present every R-vowel sequence as a universally distinct phoneme.
- /ju/ in *you*: /j/ + /u/, rather than a required sixth diphthong.
- /ʍ/ (distinct *wh*): regional/historical variation; not a core General American requirement.
- [ʔ] in selected positions: contextual glottal realization, not a separate core phoneme.
- Non-rhotic English diphthongs /ɪə eə ʊə/ are **not** added as separate General American phonemes.

## 2. Recommended teaching sequence

**Stage 1 — Letters and sounds:** distinguish spelling from phonetic sounds; introduce / / notation and example words.

**Stage 2 — Consonant categories:** 24 reference consonants; place/manner and voiced contrasts (e.g. thin/this, fan/van).

**Stage 3 — Vowels and glides:** high-value contrasts like seat/sit, moon/book, bed/bad; five vowel glides; optional cot–caught recognition rather than forced contrast.

**Stage 4 — Syllables and stress:** recognize syllables, primary /ˈ/ and secondary /ˌ/ stress; reduced syllables, /ə/, /ɚ/, and the contrast between stressed and unstressed word forms.

**Stage 5 — American R and contextual consonants:** pronounce R after vowels; learn [ɾ] (city), aspiration, dark L, and unreleased final stops as *patterns*, not new phoneme categories.

**Stage 6 — From words to connected speech:** consonant clusters, weak forms, linking, reductions, word emphasis, rhythm and intonation.

The existing IPA chart is the **Stage 2–3 reference**, not the entire Stage 1–6 course. It must not be labelled as every possible syllable, every actual spoken sound, or one fixed inventory agreed by all linguists.

## 3. Reader-facing editorial principles

1. Say why General American is selected: a widely recognizable, consistently teachable U.S. pronunciation reference.
2. Avoid implying that other accents are wrong or inferior.
3. Explain the important cot–caught variation in plain language.
4. Include sounds /ə, ɝ, ɚ/ for practical word reading but do not claim all are independent phonemes.
5. Use *sound category* for / / and *spoken realization* for [ ] to avoid confusing phonemes and allophones.
6. Never put TTS models, generation, hosting technology or implementation details in the learner-facing explanation.
7. Distinguish the sound curriculum from subsequent syllable/stress/connected-speech skills.
8. Keep the example word matched to the IPA category. Audio requires a separate pronunciation review; this change intentionally makes no audio changes.
