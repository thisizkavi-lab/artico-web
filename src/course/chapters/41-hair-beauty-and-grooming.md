# CH41 — Hair, Beauty, and Grooming

**Outcome:** Make an appointment, explain the result you want, and clarify a stylist's suggestion before agreeing.  

## Study

Useful services include **a haircut**, **a trim**—a small amount cut off—**a beard trim**, **a blow-dry**, drying and styling hair with a hair dryer, **highlights**, selected strands made lighter than the surrounding hair, **a manicure** for hands and nails, and **a facial**, a skin-care treatment for the face. Ask about the service rather than assuming its name includes everything you want.

### Making an appointment

**Customer:** I'd like to book a haircut. Do you have anything Thursday afternoon?  
**Receptionist:** We have two thirty with Morgan.  
**Customer:** That works. How long should I allow?  
**Receptionist:** For this appointment, about forty-five minutes.  
**Customer:** Great. Could you confirm the price?  
**Receptionist:** Forty dollars for the cut.  
**Customer:** Thanks. Please book that for me.  

The times and price are practice conditions. **Anything Thursday afternoon?** asks about an available time; **What is the earliest opening?** asks for the first available appointment. If the offered time does not work: **I can't make two thirty. Do you have anything after four?**

### Explaining a haircut

Describe the part and change: **A little shorter at the sides**, **Leave the top longer**, **Just a trim**, or **I'd like about an inch off**. **Bangs** is the hair cut across the forehead. If you are discussing length, **Could you show me how much?** makes an uncertain amount visible before you agree.

**Stylist:** What would you like today?  
**Customer:** Just a trim. I'd like to keep the length.  
**Stylist:** About half an inch off?  
**Customer:** Could you show me?  
**Stylist:** About this much.  
**Customer:** Yes, that looks right. Please leave the bangs a little longer.  

You can say **I'm not sure what would suit me** and ask **What would you suggest?** A suggestion is an option, not your decision. **I'd rather keep it longer** declines it; **That sounds good. Let's do that** accepts it.

### Checking another service

**Customer:** I'm interested in highlights, but I don't want a big change.  
**Stylist:** What kind of result are you hoping for?  
**Customer:** Something subtle. Could you explain the options and prices?  
**Stylist:** Sure. Let's go through them before you decide.  

**Subtle** means not strongly noticeable. **A big change** makes a stronger difference. Similar questions work for nail color or grooming: **What does the service include?**, **How long will it take?**, and **I'd like this color, please**.

### Speaking up during the appointment

**Could you leave that part longer?** requests a change. **Please stop for a moment. I want to check the length** is direct and useful when you need a pause. If the result differs from the agreement: **This is shorter than we discussed. What can we do about it?** focuses on the mismatch and the next step.

## Practice

Practice both roles alone or switch roles with a partner. You do not need an actual picture or treatment.

1. The salon offers Thursday at 2:30 or Friday at 4. You cannot attend Thursday. Book Friday and ask about the price.
2. Your role wants a trim while keeping the length. The stylist proposes an inch. Say you would prefer half an inch and ask them to show the amount.
3. You want a manicure but do not know what is included. Ask. The receptionist says **There are two options.** Request an explanation before deciding.
4. You need a pause during a cut. Make a clear request to stop briefly.

**Sample possibilities:** “Friday at four works. What is the price?” / “I'd prefer half an inch. Could you show me?” / “Could you explain what each option includes?” / “Please stop for a moment. I'd like to check the length.”  

Repeat later with a different service. Check that you communicated the desired result, confirmed what you agreed to, and could ask for clarification or a pause.
