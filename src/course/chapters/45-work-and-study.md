# CH45 — Work and Study

**Outcome:** Recognize selected job, education and employment terms and use them to describe a situation or ask a question.  

## Study

Choose the words relevant to your work or study. These groups support Chapters 46–55; you do not need to memorize every occupation before starting an interaction.

### People and jobs

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| server / chef | a person serving food / a professional cook |
| receptionist | a person welcoming visitors or handling incoming requests |
| teacher / librarian | a person teaching / a person working professionally with library services |
| doctor | in healthcare, a medical professional who evaluates and treats illness |
| nurse | a healthcare professional providing and coordinating patient care |
| pharmacist | a professional dispensing medicines and explaining their use |
| mechanic | a person repairing machines, often vehicles |
| electrician / plumber | workers specializing in electrical systems / pipes and water systems |
| accountant | a person working with financial records and accounts |
| engineer / architect | a professional designing technical systems / buildings |
| real estate agent | a person helping with property transactions |
| freelancer | someone taking work independently from clients |

Other useful jobs include **lawyer**, **dentist**, **veterinarian**, **flight attendant**, **courier**, **security guard**, **firefighter**, **app developer**, and **childcare provider**. Say **I work as a receptionist** or **I'm studying engineering** to describe a role or subject.

### Education

A **class** can be a teaching session or the students attending it. A **course** covers a subject through organized study. A **lecture** is a talk used to teach or explain a subject. **Homework** is study work assigned outside class; an **assignment** is a particular task. An **exam** or **test** assesses learning. **Pass** means meet its stated standard; **fail** means not meet it.

A **degree** is an academic qualification awarded after a college or university program. A **diploma** is a document showing completion of study; some programs also use the word as a qualification name. A **certificate** documents completion or a particular achievement; ask what a course's certificate represents. The names alone do not establish that qualifications are equivalent. **Graduation** is completing a course of study or the related ceremony. A **schedule** shows planned times. **Tuition** refers to the charge for instruction in the education context used here.

### Employment

An **employer** hires people; an **employee** works for the employer. **Full-time** and **part-time** describe working arrangements; ask what hours a particular job involves. **Temporary** work has a limited expected period; **permanent** describes an ongoing arrangement, not a guarantee of lifetime employment.

A **shift** is a scheduled period of work. **Overtime** is work beyond a relevant usual or defined period. **Wages** means pay earned for work, often discussed by the hour. A **salary** is an agreed regular amount of pay, often stated as a yearly figure. **Hourly pay** states payment by the hour. A job offer explains how that job's pay is calculated. A **pay stub** lists earnings and deductions. **Benefits** are employment offerings beyond ordinary pay. **Paid time off** names time away for which pay is provided under the employer's arrangements. **Resign** means leave a job by your own decision; **retire** means stop working as a later-life or career decision.

## Practice

1. Choose four terms that match your interests. Define each in your own words.
2. Identify the words: the hiring organization; a planned work period; a charge for a course; a record of earnings.
3. Use this fictional role: part-time receptionist, studying accounting, Tuesday classes. Say two sentences, then ask about the job's hours.

**Sample possibilities:** **Employer, shift, tuition, pay stub.** “I work part-time as a receptionist. I'm studying accounting, with classes on Tuesdays. What hours does the job involve?”  

Cover the lists later and retrieve only your chosen words. Check that you can distinguish the people, activity, qualification and work condition instead of treating related terms as identical.
