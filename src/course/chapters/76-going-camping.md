# CH76 — Going Camping

**Outcome:** Find your reserved campsite, ask about facilities and current rules, and request supplies.  

## Study

A **campground** is an area with camping sites. A **campsite** is an individual place to camp. An **RV** is a recreational vehicle; a **camper van** is a van equipped for camping. **Restrooms**, **showers**, a **camp store**, and a **water station** are facilities you can ask about.

### Arrive and locate the site

**Camper:** Hi, we have a campsite reservation under Alex Chen for two nights.  
**Host:** Is that for a tent or an RV?  
**Camper:** A tent. Where should we set up?  
**Host:** Site 14, across from the showers.  
**Camper:** Can we park our car at the site?  
**Host:** In this campground, use the marked parking space beside it.  
**Camper:** And where can we get drinking water?  
**Host:** At the water station next to the camp store.  

The parking arrangement is fictional. **Set up camp** means arrange your camping place and equipment. **Check out** means finish the stay. Ask “What time do we need to leave the site?” rather than assuming hotel checkout rules apply.

### Ask about fires and equipment

**Camper:** Are campfires allowed today?  
**Host:** There's a fire restriction today. No campfires.  
**Camper:** What about our camping stove?  
**Host:** Let me check the current rules for that type of stove.  
**Camper:** Please do. Where can we read the posted rules?  

Ask about current restrictions instead of relying on a previous visit. The National Park Service's Zion campground guidance explicitly directs visitors to current fire restrictions and a ranger or camp host. This example does not prescribe stove use or the rules of another park.

Useful words: **tent**, a portable shelter; **sleeping bag**, a sleeping covering; **campfire**, an outdoor fire; **firewood**, wood used as fuel; **matches**, small sticks for lighting a flame; **camping stove**, a portable cooking device; **fuel**, material providing energy. Different stoves use different fuel, so “Do you sell fuel for this stove?” is more precise than naming any container as suitable.

### Buy what you forgot

**Camper:** We forgot our flashlight. Do you sell one?  
**Clerk:** Yes. This one takes two batteries.  
**Camper:** Are the batteries included?  
**Clerk:** Not with this model.  
**Camper:** Okay, I'd like the flashlight and the matching batteries.  

You can also ask “Do you have matches?” or “Where are the trash bins?” “We forgot…” gives the reason; it does not require a long apology. With a companion, “Let's set up first, then explore” organizes the plan. “I'm not up for a long hike tonight” expresses a real limit.

## Practice

Fictional campground card: tent site 14; two nights; showers opposite the site; water next to the store; checkout noon; campfires prohibited today; stove restrictions require confirmation.

1. Check in. Partner asks tent or RV, then gives the site. Confirm its location and ask about water.
2. Ask about a campfire. Partner states the restriction. Ask about your stove without deciding the answer yourself.
3. Buy a flashlight. Partner says batteries are not included. Say what you want to purchase.
4. Tell a companion your first two actions. Change the plan if you are tired.

Solo: read each host or clerk turn and create your camper replies. Later describe the campground with the card hidden.

**Possibilities:** “Site fourteen, across from the showers?” “Could you confirm the rule for this stove?” “Let's set up, then get water.” Check whether you locate the site and distinguish an allowed activity from a rule still needing confirmation.  

*Source note: Current-rule approach informed by [National Park Service campground guidance](https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/camping-regulations.htm); the campground itself is invented.*
