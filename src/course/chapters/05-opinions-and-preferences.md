# CH05 — Opinions and Preferences

**Your goal:** Say what you like, give an opinion, choose between options, or explain that either choice works for you.  

## Study: Like, dislike, and strength

| Expression | Meaning in these examples |
| --- | --- |
| **I like it.** | A positive view. |
| **I really like it. / I love it.** | A stronger positive view. |
| **I'm into photography.** | You are interested in photography. |
| **I'm not really into it.** | It does not interest you much. |
| **It's not my thing.** | It does not suit your interests or taste. |
| **I can't stand it.** | A strong dislike. |

**Choosing a weekend activity**

**Dev:** Do you like escape rooms?  
**Riley:** Not really. They're not my thing.  
**Dev:** What are you into?  
**Riley:** I'm into photography. I'd rather go for a walk and take pictures.  

Riley gives a preference without claiming escape rooms are bad for everyone. **I can't stand them** would express a stronger dislike. You can choose strong language when you intend strong feeling; it may also make a disagreement more noticeable.

**I'm good** has different uses. After **How are you?**, it can report that you are well. After an offer, **No, I'm good, thanks** can decline it. The surrounding exchange supplies the meaning.

## Study: Give and ask for an opinion

**What do you think of it?** asks for an evaluation. **How do you feel about the plan?** asks about someone's view or reaction.

- **I think the smaller room would work better.** Give a view.
- **In my opinion, we need more time.** Explicitly present your opinion.
- **As I see it, the main problem is the cost.** Present your understanding of the issue.
- **I like the idea, but I'm worried about the price.** Give a mixed evaluation.

**A team considering a venue**

**Dev:** What do you think of the room?  
**Riley:** I like the location, but I think it's too small.  
**Dev:** What's your view, Morgan?  
**Morgan:** As I see it, we need a bigger space. We expect twenty people.  

A reason helps others understand the view. A work discussion can use **I think** as well as longer opinion frames; you do not need a ceremonial introduction before every opinion.

## Study: Choose between options

**Prefer** and **would rather** express a choice:

- **I prefer tea to coffee.** Compare two drinks.
- **I'd prefer the earlier train.** Choose an option for this situation.
- **I'd rather leave at six.** Follow **rather** with the action: **leave**, not **to leave**.
- **I'd rather not go tonight.** Say you would prefer not to do it.

**Dev:** Would you rather eat here or take the food home?  
**Riley:** I'd rather take it home. It's quieter there.  
**Dev:** That works for me.  

To leave the choice open, use **Either is fine**, **Both work for me**, or **I don't have a preference**. **I don't mind** can also accept an option. These need not mean you are uninterested in the person. **I don't care** can communicate genuine indifference, but in some exchanges it sounds as though the choice or conversation does not matter to you. Choose it if that is the feeling you mean to convey.

## Optional: Strong reactions

**No way!** can reject an idea emphatically, or show surprised disbelief. Compare **No way! I'm not paying that much** with **You won? No way! That's amazing**. The following words help make your intention clear.

## Practice: Choose and explain

Read these fictional choices. Speak both roles if you practice alone.

| Choice | Facts supplied for the scene |
| --- | --- |
| Workshop room | Small room: quiet, seats eight. Large room: brighter, seats twenty. Your group has twelve people. |
| Dinner | Noodles or tacos. Your role likes noodles, dislikes tacos, or has no preference—choose one. |
| Evening | Stay home or go to a loud concert. Your role is tired. |

1. Complete **What do you think of the small room?** Give a mixed or negative view with a reason. Support: **quiet**, **too small**, **twelve people**.
2. Ask about dinner, answer with a preference, and respond to the choice. Use **I'd prefer...**, **I'd rather have...**, or **Either is fine**.
3. You are tired and do not want the concert. Give a clear answer without pretending to like the plan. Support: **I'd rather stay home**, **I'm tired**, **Loud concerts aren't my thing**.
4. Change the intention: you strongly dislike loud concerts. Try **I can't stand...**. Then make a milder version and describe the difference in strength.
5. Hide the table. Explain one personal or invented preference and ask for the other person's view.

### Sample possibilities

1. **I like that it's quiet, but it's too small for twelve people.**
2. **Would you rather have noodles or tacos? — I'd prefer noodles. — Okay, that works for me.**
3. **I'd rather stay home. I'm tired.**
4. **I can't stand loud concerts** versus **Loud concerts aren't really my thing**.

**Check:** Did you separate a preference from a claim about everyone? Did the strength match your intention? Could the other person tell whether you chose an option or left the choice open?
