# CH51 — Starting a New Job

**Outcome:** Introduce yourself on the first day, clarify instructions, and discuss how the work is going.  

## Study

**It's my first day** explains why you need help finding people or understanding routines. A **supervisor** oversees work; a **coworker** or **colleague** works with you. An **orientation** introduces a workplace and its practices. **Training** develops the skills for the work.

### Arriving and meeting people

**New employee:** Hi, I'm Maya Torres. It's my first day. I'm here to meet Jordan.  
**Receptionist:** Welcome, Maya. I'll let Jordan know you're here.  
**Employee:** Thanks. Where should I wait?  
**Receptionist:** You can wait by this desk.  
**Coworker:** Hi, Maya. I'm Lee. I work on the customer-service team.  
**Employee:** Nice to meet you. I'm looking forward to getting started.  

These introductions apply Chapter 2 to a new workplace. **Who should I report to?** asks who is responsible for directing your work. **Could you show me around?** asks for a tour; **Where can I put my things?** asks about storage.

### Clarify a routine

For practice, the employee's fictional schedule is: orientation at 9, a break at 10:30, lunch at 12, and a training session at 1.

**Supervisor:** We'll start with orientation at nine.  
**Employee:** Okay. Is that here or in another room?  
**Supervisor:** In the training room. Your first break is at ten thirty.  
**Employee:** Thanks. Could you write down the schedule?  
**Supervisor:** Sure. Training starts at one after lunch.  
**Employee:** Got it. Who should I ask if I get stuck during training?  

**Get stuck** means reach a point where you cannot continue because you need help. **Just to check...** introduces confirmation: **Just to check, training starts at one?** If an instruction is unclear, **Could you show me how that works?** asks for a demonstration.

### Accounts, equipment and task-specific instructions

**Coworker:** Your account is ready. We'll go through the setup instructions together.  
**Employee:** Thanks. Where can I find the password requirements?  
**Coworker:** They're in the account instructions.  
**Employee:** And who should I contact if I can't sign in?  
**Coworker:** I'll give you the support contact.  

You can ask **Which equipment am I expected to use?**, **What training do I need before using it?**, and **What protective equipment is required for this task?** A role may involve rules you have not seen yet; **Where can I find the written instructions?** makes that need explicit. You are practicing requesting the workplace's guidance, not inventing a technical procedure.

### A progress check-in

**Coworker:** How's it going so far?  
**Employee:** Pretty well. There's a lot to remember, though.  
**Coworker:** Anything you're unsure about?  
**Employee:** I'm not sure how to record the completed orders. Could someone go over that with me?  
**Coworker:** Sure. Let's ask Jordan.  
**Employee:** Thanks. That would help.  

**So far** asks about progress up to now; this is more specific than a quick greeting. **Go over that with me** asks someone to review a process together. You can also say **I'm getting the hang of it** when beginning to understand a task, or **I need another explanation** when you are not.

## Practice

Read the coworker or supervisor's lines, answer aloud, and rehearse both roles if alone.

1. Arrive as Maya and explain whom you are there to meet. Ask where to wait.
2. Use the schedule to confirm the break and training times. Ask for a written copy.
3. You cannot sign in. Describe the problem and ask who handles account support.
4. Your coworker asks how the job is going. You like the work but are unsure about recording orders. State both parts and request help.

**Sample possibilities:** “Hi, I'm Maya Torres. It's my first day. I'm here to meet Jordan.” / “Just to check, my break is at ten thirty and training is at one?” / “I can't sign in. Who should I contact?” / “It's going well, but I'm not sure how to record orders. Could someone go over that with me?”  

Try later with a different uncertainty. Check that you could acknowledge what you understood and clearly identify what remained unclear.
