## The five habits to avoid

1. **Do not assume a sample is high quality just because a website calls it Band 9.** Check the source and, where possible, its provenance and relationship to official descriptors.
2. **Do not rely on passive reading.** Give the learner a question or a language feature to investigate before opening the model response.
3. **Do not try to score essays by gut feeling.** A learner’s own impression of “advanced” language is not an examiner judgement. Use the official criteria and guided comparison.
4. **Do not memorize phrases or paragraphs for insertion into unrelated prompts.** Study what makes the response work, then produce a fresh answer.
5. **Do not copy a full essay into a test answer.** Write a task-specific response of your own.

These reflect the instructor’s practical warnings. His claims that more than 95% of online Band 9 essays are false, that thousands fail monthly by passively reading, and that his own samples are genuine examiner-marked work are not independently substantiated in the video.

## Active study routine

First identify a writing weakness from a real draft or feedback. Use one or two of these exercises to work on it, then write a fresh answer to check transfer.

- **Idea relevance:** Cover the model essay. Read and unpack the prompt, then write a few relevant ideas of your own. Compare for relevance and scope, not exact matches. A different relevant idea is not automatically wrong. The source’s “simplest idea” prompt can reduce pressure, but do not turn it into a ban on brainstorming or complex arguments; select ideas you can answer and develop.
- **Idea development:** Before reading the body paragraph, choose one main point and write its explanation and a relevant example. Compare how the model explains and supports its point. Study several responses to see that valid development can differ.
- **Organization:** Mark the role of each paragraph and the job of sentences within it. Compare essays for the same prompt or task type. Learn the response’s logic; do not infer that a four-paragraph plan or a single template is required for every question.
- **Cohesion:** Underline cohesive devices and label their function (for example, contrast, reference, or example). Check whether each device is accurate and useful. Do not copy an essay’s linker count or teach that a high-scoring response must use few linkers; the official criterion is appropriate organization and cohesion, not a quota.
- **Grammar diagnosis:** Mark a suspected error, classify it (such as articles, prepositions, punctuation, or verb form), correct it, and state the rule in your own words. Sample essays can contain errors; verify the correction rather than assuming every stylistic difference is a mistake.
- **Grammar noticing:** Select a sentence and work out why its tense, clause, or non-finite form fits the meaning. Make a new sentence of your own that uses the same function. Do not copy a complex sentence without understanding how it works.
- **Vocabulary learning:** Mark unknown or uncertain words and collocations. Infer from the surrounding sentence and paragraph, verify the meaning, note the part of speech and a fresh example, then retrieve and use it later. Do not treat synonyms as interchangeable or collect phrases you cannot use accurately.
- **Vocabulary revision:** Check for imprecise word choice, unintended repetition, and collocation problems. Replace a word only when the alternative preserves the intended meaning and fits the grammar and register.
- **Full-answer comparison:** After targeted practice, answer a new or familiar prompt under suitable timing and compare your response with a model using Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Focus on task coverage, relevance, development, and organization. Differences in wording or opinion alone do not make your answer wrong.

## Course implementation

A compact lesson can use one authentic prompt and a reputable model response: read the prompt, draft an outline before seeing the essay, compare the relevance and development of the main ideas, choose one language feature to inspect, produce an original paragraph, and revise it against the official Writing criteria. Follow with a fresh prompt or timed essay. The samples are objects of analysis, not answers to memorize.

The source’s offer of a 100-essay pack and claims about score outcomes are promotional. The availability and examiner verification of that pack were not checked for this synthesis. Do not present the advertised Band 7–9 labels as independently verified evidence.
