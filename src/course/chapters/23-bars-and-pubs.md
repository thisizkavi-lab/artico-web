# CH23 — Bars and Pubs

**Your goal:** Order a drink, ask about the choices and payment, buy a round, or decline another drink.  

## Study: Ask what is available

A **bar** can mean the business or the counter where drinks are served. A **pub** is another kind of drinking establishment; use the venue's own name rather than assume a fixed menu or atmosphere. You can order alcohol or choose a drink without it.

| Choice | Meaning in this setting |
| --- | --- |
| Beer on tap / draft beer | Beer served from a tap rather than a bottle or can |
| Lager | A type of beer |
| Red, white or sparkling wine | Wine choices; sparkling wine has bubbles |
| Spirits / liquor | Distilled alcoholic drinks, such as gin or whiskey |
| Cocktail | A mixed alcoholic drink |
| Mocktail | A mixed drink made without alcohol |
| Soda / soft drink | A nonalcoholic drink, such as cola |

**What do you have on tap?**, **Do you have any mocktails?**, and **Could I see the wine list?** open different choices. **Dry wine** has little sweetness, in contrast with sweet wine; dry here does not mean alcohol-free. **I'd like a dry white wine** asks for that kind of wine. If you do not know the choices, ask **What would you recommend?** You can accept a recommendation or say **I'd prefer something else**.

## Study: Order with the details you want

Use this fictional menu for Practice. Prices are supplied only to make the order concrete.

| Menu item | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Draft lager, one pint | $6 |
| House white wine, one glass | $7 |
| Gin and tonic, with ice and lime | $8 |
| Lime-and-mint mocktail | $5 |
| Cola or sparkling water | $3 |

**Customer:** Could I get a pint of the lager and a lime-and-mint mocktail?  
**Bartender:** Sure. Anything else?  
**Customer:** No, that's all. Should I pay now?  
**Bartender:** Yes, please.  

You can also use **I'll have…** or **I'd like…**. These are order choices, not a rule requiring one exact sentence. A **pint** is a serving measure; **glass**, **bottle** and **can** identify other servings.

**Customer:** A gin and tonic, please, with no ice.  
**Bartender:** Would you like lime with that?  
**Customer:** Yes, please.  

If the menu wording leaves an important detail unclear, ask directly: **Does that contain alcohol?**, **Can you make it without alcohol?**, or **Which size is that?** Ask **Do you serve food?** or **Could I see the food menu?** if you want to eat.

## Study: Rounds and payment

A **round** is a set of drinks bought for a group. **This round's on me** offers to pay for that round. It does not require someone else to keep drinking.

**Alex:** I'm getting a round. What would you like?  
**Riley:** Sparkling water, please.  
**Casey:** Nothing for me, thanks. I'm done for tonight.  
**Alex:** Okay. One sparkling water and a lager for me.  

**My round** and **It's my turn to buy** are other ways to offer. **I'll get this one; you can get the next one** proposes an arrangement that the other person can accept or decline.

A **tab** is the bill or account for what you order. You can ask **Can I start a tab?**, **Can you put this on my tab?**, or **I'd like to close out my tab**. The staff explain the venue's payment procedure. **We'll pay separately** is useful when you do not want a shared bill.

## Study: More drinks or the end of service

**Another one of those, please** repeats an order. **Same again?** asks whether someone wants the same drink again. Reply **Yes, please**, **Just water this time**, or **No, I'm good**.

**Last call** announces a final opportunity to order before service ends. It is not a request that you must buy another drink.

**Bartender:** Last call. We're closing in fifteen minutes.  
**Customer:** No more drinks for me. Could I close out my tab?  
**Bartender:** Of course.  

For pressure from a companion, **I said no. I don't want another drink** makes the boundary explicit. An explanation is optional.

## Practice: Order, change, and finish

Speak both roles when practicing alone. Use the menu above; for tab practice, the fictional bartender allows tabs and asks for the customer's name.

1. Order a glass of white wine and a sparkling water. Ask whether to pay now; staff answer **Yes, please**.
2. Order a gin and tonic without ice. Staff ask about lime; choose yes or no.
3. Change the response: the lager is unavailable. Staff offer bottled beer or sparkling water. Choose one, or decline the order.
4. Offer a round to two friends. One wants cola; the other does not want a drink. Place the resulting order, including your own mocktail.
5. Start a tab under the name Dana. Later, last call is announced. Decline another drink and ask to close the tab.
6. Hide the models. Ask about one choice, order, and respond when a companion offers another drink. Repeat with the opposite choice about having another.

### Sample possibilities

1. **A glass of the house white wine and a sparkling water, please. Should I pay now?**
2. **Could I get a gin and tonic with no ice? — Lime? — Yes, please.**
3. **I'll have sparkling water instead.**
4. **This round's on me. What would you like?** Then **One cola and one lime-and-mint mocktail, please.**
5. **Can I start a tab? The name is Dana.** Later: **No more drinks, thanks. I'd like to close out my tab.**

**Check:** Did the serving and drink match the order? Did a declined drink stay declined? Did you distinguish ordering another drink from paying for the drinks already ordered?
