# CH49 — Applying for a Job

**Outcome:** Prepare short application text from supplied facts, follow a stated submission request, and explain an application problem.  

## Study

A **resume** summarizes relevant experience, education and skills. A **cover letter** connects your background to a particular position. An **application form** asks for information in the employer's chosen format. A **deadline** is the stated latest time for submission. **References** are people an employer may ask about your experience or work; they are not the same as contact details for yourself.

Use the document name requested by the employer. An application may request a CV or other materials instead of, or alongside, a resume. Ask if the requirement is unclear: **Which documents should I submit?**

### Preparing and discussing materials

**Applicant:** I want to apply for the store-assistant job. Could you look over my resume?  
**Friend:** Sure. Have you included your customer-service experience?  
**Applicant:** Yes, but I need to update the dates.  
**Friend:** Does the posting ask for a cover letter?  
**Applicant:** It does. I'll explain why I'm interested in the store.  

**Look over** means review. **Update** means make the information current. **Proofread** means check for errors in the wording or text. **Could you check whether this is clear?** makes a useful request without asking someone to invent experience for you.

### A short original resume extract

The following role is invented for practice:

> **Sam Rivera — Store Assistant Applicant**  
> **Experience:** Café assistant, June 2025–June 2026. Took customer orders, answered menu questions and organized supplies.  
> **Education:** Completed a business certificate, May 2025.  
> **Skills:** Customer communication, order accuracy and teamwork.  
> **Availability:** Afternoons and weekends.  

**Experience** says what the person did. A skill label such as **teamwork** becomes clearer when linked to an actual example. This is a short extract to discuss and revise, not a required resume format.

### A short cover-letter example

For the same role, Sam enjoys books, wants to help bookstore customers, and can start November 1. A short original letter connects those facts to the position:

> Dear Hiring Manager,  
> I'm applying for the store-assistant position. During a year working in a café, I took orders, answered customers' questions and organized supplies. I also completed a business certificate. I'd like to bring that customer-service experience to your bookstore because I enjoy books and helping people find what they need. I'm available afternoons and weekends and could start November 1. Thank you for considering my application.  
> Sam Rivera

The letter identifies the position, relevant experience, interest and availability. Use the facts you have; **helping customers** does not require inventing sales figures or management experience.

### Read the actual request

For this fictional application: submit a resume and a short cover letter through the employer's form by 5 p.m. October 20. The form requests PDF files and a contact email address.

**Applicant:** The form asks for a resume and cover letter as PDFs. Is five o'clock the deadline?  
**Contact:** Yes, on October twentieth.  
**Applicant:** Thanks. The upload failed when I tried to send the cover letter. What should I do?  
**Contact:** Tell us the error message so we can explain the submission options.  
**Applicant:** It says the file is too large. Is there another way to submit it?  

**Upload** sends a file to an online system. **Submit** sends an application for consideration. **Save a draft** keeps an unfinished version. A successful upload is not automatically confirmation that the whole application was submitted: **How can I confirm you received the application?** asks about that final result.

### Form questions and support

Useful questions are **What does this field mean?**, **Do you need the full address?**, **Should I list my most recent job first?**, and **Is this section required?** A **required field** must be completed under the form's instructions. If something does not apply or you are unsure what to enter, ask rather than making up a fact.

**Applicant:** This asks for my earliest start date. Does that mean when I could begin work?  
**Contact:** Yes.  
**Applicant:** I could start November first. I'll enter that.  

## Practice

Speak both roles alone using the contact's responses. Write the short revision and letter from the supplied role facts; no real application is submitted.

1. Use the fictional request to state the documents, format and deadline. Ask one confirmation question.
2. Revise Sam's skills section by adding a concrete example from the experience given. Do not add an achievement absent from the role facts.
3. Your upload says **file too large**. Report the exact problem and ask for an alternative submission option.
4. Ask what **earliest start date** means, then give November 1 as your role's available date.
5. Write a three-sentence cover-letter body for Sam: identify the job, connect one experience to the role, and state availability. Use the model for support, then check the facts against the resume extract.

**Sample possibilities:** “I need a resume and cover letter as PDFs by five on October twentieth. How can I confirm receipt?” / “Customer communication: answered customers' menu questions.” / “The upload failed. It says the file is too large. Is there another way to submit it?”  

**Possible letter body:** “I'm applying for the store-assistant position. I have a year of café experience answering customers' questions, and I'd like to use those skills in your bookstore. I'm available afternoons and weekends and could start November 1.”  

Later, change the stated requirements and read them before responding. Check that the application facts are accurate, the requested documents are identified, and any problem is described clearly.
